                 TOWN OF MOUNT PLEASANT, SC
                SPECIAL TOWN COUNCIL MEETING
                       Monday, June 3, 2024

                                MINUTES

1.       Call to Order 12:30 p.m.
     Mayor Haynie called the meeting to order at 12:30 p.m. Daniel
     Brownstein, Howard Chapman, Laura Hyatt, Jake Rambo, John
     Iacofano, Gary Santos and Carl Ritchie were all present. Guang
     Ming Whitley was absent.
2.       Public Comment
            Chris Colman, 118 Pitt Street stated he wanted to comment on
     some of the standards he read, with a focus on NC (Neighborhood
     Commercial). He said there are some new uses, one that he did not
     understand why it would be wanted in neighborhood commercial
     areas, and that is Brew Pub. He said he also think it should be
     clarified that short term rentals are not allowed in the Old Village
     Historic District by cross-referencing 217. He said he did not like the
     way areas were designated for boutique hotels, and the areas should
     be listed. Mr. Colman said he thought it was unnecessary to remove
     the outdoor dining category, mostly because he thinks the prohibition
     on amplified sound outside should still stay in the NC district because
     it is right next to residences. He said the recommendation in NM
     (Mixed Use Neighborhood) for restaurant size of limit of 1200 square
     feet should be applied in NC2 as well. He said there are a few other
     allowed uses that may not be neighborhood commercial such as
     mortuary, animal boarding, crop and greenhouse production.
3.       New Business
       a. Review of Zoning Code Draft Subchapters B: Zoning
          Districts, C: use Regulations, and G: Signs, including
          Planning Committee Recommendations below:
          Presentations were given by Kelly Cousino and Sean
          Scoopmire of White & Smith, LLC, Planning and Law Group.
Mr. Scoopmire (virtually) gave the following presentation.
Mr. Iacofano asked if a sign was lost during a storm would the
new sign fall under the new rules. Mr. Scoopmire said he
believed it would fall under the new rules, but he would need to
refer to the non-conforming section to confirm that.




Mr. Scoopmire said murals were discussed at the Planning
Committee and it was recommended to come to full Council for
further discussion.
Mr. Scoopmire said the question for Council is, the current draft
does not allow murals, and what is the interest in direction, to
either allow or not allow them.
Mr. Iacofano said he thinks they have a place but it will be
difficult to delineate where.
Mr. Brownstein said he agreed with Mr. Iacofano and when
murals are done correctly they add a lot to the community, and
it is a way to make less attractive buildings more attractive. He
said he would like to allow all murals.
Mr. Rambo said he has been driving around looking at the ones
that are currently in place, he said he has issues with
administration as when you get into words and characters it is
more complicated, and he likes the fact that if we allow murals it
would be limited to the rear and side of the building.
     Mr. Rambo moved to allow murals in non-residential
districts, on the rear and side of buildings, seconded by Mr.
Brownstein.
Mr. Pagliarini asked for clarification if that is the fourth option on
the list, with no limits on images or words. Mr. Rambo
responded in the affirmative.
Mayor Haynie said he has no problem with them being on the
front of a building. He asked Mr. Pagliarini for a definition of
when is it art and when is it advertising that gets around the
sign ordinance. Mr. Pagliarini asked Mr. Scoopmire to expand
on the statement of no limit on images or words, asking if the
business could use that as additional advertisement. Mr.
Scoopmire responded in the affirmative. Mr. Pagliarini said the
Planning Commission got to this level, and there were several
questions. He said it cannot be based on what is liked or
disliked, but it must be restricted by lettering, by size, by shape,
but what is said, subject to certain constitutional restrictions
such as certain explicit language and things you cannot say,
that is limited. It is either allowed or not allowed.
Mayor said everyone has seen the old-fashioned Coke signs on
the side of barns, and technically that is an advertisement and
that would be allowed.
Mr. Pagliarini said when we start making these decisions as to
what will or will not be allowed, that is when it becomes all or
nothing.
Mayor Haynie said in terms of this motion, the legal advice is, it
is all or nothing. Mr. Pagliarini responded in the affirmative.
Mayor Haynie said if we pass this motion and we have murals
that are on the front of buildings, will they stay on and be non-
conforming. Mr. Pagliarini responded in the affirmative.
Mr. Santos said he thought having no limit on images or words
may not be appropriate. He said he likes it the way it is now,
where it can be done on the side of a building and must be
done in a professional-looking design to keep it clean.
Ms. Hyatt asked if by saying no limit, does that mean all four
walls could be painted. Mr. Pagliarini responded in the
negative, that it referred to the content. Ms. Hyatt said there is
only a limited space for signs but if you are allowing words, that
would be doubling the space that is normally allowed for signs.
Mr. Pagliarini said this would certainly allow some businesses
who have this wall space to have more advertising square
footage.
Ms. Hyatt said she was comfortable with limiting the characters.
Mr. Rambo said some businesses may have logos, which is an
image, and not necessarily a word, so that business is able to
put a large logo on the side of their business. Mr. Pagliarini
said there is a self-policing idea that generally businesses will
do what is in their best interest.
Mr. Iacofano said the Design Review Board and Planning do a
lot of work to make this town look and feel a certain way and we
do not want to undo that, but maybe there could be more of a
discussion about allowing it on the side and rear of a building
but not street side. He said there are plenty of businesses
where the side of their building is on the street side, and can we
limit it to where the mural is not on the street side.
Mr. Pagliarini said certainly, and we have wrestled with what is
the front and what is the side of commercial and residential
properties. He said it could be severely limiting, and then you
get into regulating the distance from the street. Mr. Iacofano
said clarification needs to be made as to whether the murals
will be allowed on the street side, or the side and rear of the
building.
Mr. Scoopmire said that is a good question and the draft
defined placing on a secondary wall, not in relation to the public
entrance, but as it may not be located on an exterior wall or
structure that does not have direct street frontage.
Mr. Pagliarini said the definition as he understands it does
include that frontage along a public street would not be
considered a side.
Mr. Rambo said all of the examples of murals he can think
about are on the side, but are visible from the street, or they
face the street in some way so why would someone want to put
up a mural where no one can see it. His motion was intended
for the side and rear, as the front would be determined to have
the main entrance and he did not have a limit. Mr. Pagliarini
said that was his understanding as well
Mr. Santos asked if his motion included no limit on images or
words. Mr. Rambo responded in the affirmative stating it is just
in relation to whether it can have words or characters. He said
it still has content limitations and cannot be profane or
pornographic or anything like that.
    Mayor Haynie called for the vote and all present voted in
favor.

presentation and Ms. Cousino would continue from here.


Ms. Cousino gave the following presentation.
Mr. Brownstein asked if the height restriction includes the
pitched roof, and Ms. Cousino stated the height would be
measured at the average height of the highest roof surface
which is what is in the Building Code.
Mr. Rambo asked if there is no motion made by the body then
would the Planning Committee recommendation stand, and Mr.
Pagliarini responded in the affirmative.
Ms. Cousino said her understanding of how the plan will work is
once the code is reviewed with Council, another draft will be
produced with some minor revisions and the Planning
Committee recommendations in redline, so what Council will
see as the public hearing draft will include the Planning
Committee’s recommendations and it will be very clear where
those changes were made in the code, unless full Council
directs otherwise as we proceed with the review process.
Mr. Iacofano asked if on the attainable housing it is for a lease
or purchase and Ms. Cousino responded it is for either. Mr.
Iacofano asked if it is when they purchase or lease that they
must be employed, and Ms. Cousino responded in the
affirmative, stating tenants are not required to vacate if their
employment situation changes. She said if it is a rental, the
tenant will be able to renew the lease for one additional term,
not to exceed two years.
Ms. Hyatt stated the Planning Committee recommended
eliminating this. She said it brings up fifty percent affordable
housing which we are already requiring in Mixed Residential.
She said rather than floating where they could be placed
anywhere, we wanted simple base zoning, with these options
available if someone wanted to do a planned development and
incorporate some of these things without it being part of the
base zoning. She said a planned development would need to
come before Council for approval.
Ms. Cousino said there were not any provisions for attainable
housing in the Multi Family Mixed Residential District. She said
that is carried forward in its current state.
Ms. Reed clarified that it is in AB.
Mr. Brownstein asked if any properties are currently zoned for
this mixed neighborhood, and Ms. Cousino responded in the
negative. She said we are not proposing any properties to be
zoned this way, and that would require a property owner to
propose it and make the request of Town Council for the
rezoning. Ms. Cousino said any property would need to meet
the requirements that are in the code, and they could not
request any deviations from these standards.
Mr. Brownstein said this will encourage a variety of housing
configurations on the same project, but he is unclear why we
should remove this option.
Mr. Rambo said he thinks the confusion is that on February 15,
the Planning Committee voted unanimously to remove this as a
district. He stated the issue came up again on March 1
because of state law requirements about affordable housing
opportunities. Mr. Rambo said it came up that we currently
have affordable housing planned development opportunities
that could be developed as a planned development. He said the
choice is to be able to include them in planned developments,
or as a base zoning district for which people can apply. Mr.
Rambo said he likes the planned developments as they can be
negotiated and made site specific, where as this will apply a
standard set of guidelines and then once someone rezones,
there is no control over whatever they want to build that is
within the guidelines. He said the other discussion at the
Planning Committee meeting is the bedroom requirement, and
it was decided not to make any recommendations on that
because it had been recommended to eliminate it, but then it
was something that Planning Committee wanted look at more
closely to see if Council wanted to keep this as a zoning option,
as opposed to the Workforce Housing Planned Development
option.
Mayor Haynie asked if this is automatically a recommendation
from the Planning Committee that comes as a motion that does
not require a second or does there need to be a motion.
Mr. Pagliarini said there does not need to be a motion on it. He
said currently this item is not in the draft, but ultimately it will
come back to Council for the Public Hearing. Mr. Pagliarini said
the question is would you prefer it to be in the draft at this point,
or not.
Ms. Cousino said if Council wants to keep the Workforce
Housing Planned Development in lieu of adding this new
district, there would need to be some changes to ensure it
complies with the South Carolina Planning definition of Planned
Development, which requires both commercial and residential
uses, which the Workforce Housing Planned Development does
not currently require.
Ms. Cousino continued with the presentation in relation to
