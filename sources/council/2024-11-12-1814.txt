      Craig Tozzolino, 1431 Glencoe Drive stated he bought his house
      about twenty-months ago and has had difficulty with noise coming
      from a public supermarket with which he shares a fence line. He said
      there are landscaping blowers being used at 3:00 a.m. He spoke
      with management, and then they started power washing at 2:00 a.m.
      Then they were replacing air conditioning units lasting until 2:00 a.m.
      He said he was dealing with the supermarket management and they
      were very responsive but they are not the ones responsible for when
      the work is done. He says when he calls the police he gets the word
      “judgement” where sometimes they shut the work down, and
      sometimes they do not.
      Allison Shore, 2220 Cultura Circle stated she wanted to talk about
      the garage enclosures on the elevated homes in Liberty Hill Farm.
      She said she had her garage enclosed to protect her belongings and
      provide safety for herself. She said installing vents as recommended
      by FEMA would certainly appear to resolve concerns. She said she
      never saw or signed a non-conversion agreement. Ms. Shore said the
      builder told her a non-conversion agreement was not required for a
      certificate of occupancy for their home. She asked Council to review
      the issue and ordinance as many have requested.
      Roy Trifilio, 2264 Cultura Circle stated he contacted Weeks and
      Irving Law Firm to see if there was a non-conversion attached to their
      deed, and there was none located. He said if they knew they could
      not enclose their garage, they never would have built here. He said
      when they bought their townhouse, the first thing he looked at was
      the space in their garage. He said security is also an issue, as the
      back door was a garden gate.
      Carol Trifilio, stated her husband had already said everything.
Bob Lawrence, 2217 Cultura Circle stated they have done significant
research into the Town website and the FEMA and CRS websites.
He said their property does not have a non-conversion agreement on
file at the Charleston County ROD as required. He said he
understands there are many homes in Liberty Hill Farm without the
non-conversion agreement on file as specified in the Town’s rules.
He said this is a violation of the Town’s rules, and a violation of the
fact that the building permit is not accompanied by the non-
conversion agreement and the certificate of occupancy when issued
is not accompanied by the non-conversion agreement. He said they
never heard anything about a non-conversion agreement during the
buying and building process which was seventeen months. He said
at the recent Flood Resilience Committee last week, the Committee
listened and indicated they needed to take time to look at the
concerns and would get back to us, but no timeline was given and
that information would be appreciated. He said they have offered
garage enclosure alternatives that are detailed in the FEMA and CRS
information. He said there have been no responses to this point. He
said their next steps will be to make the appropriate FEMA and CRS
contacts and present their facts to them. He asked to work together
to resolve this issue.
David Kalishman, 2135 Annie Laura Lane stated he is the
Homeowners Association President and representative for Annie
Laura Lane, and is here to address the recently completed
sweetgrass maintenance and recycling facility and to request that the
city conduct an objective sound study to identify excessive
disturbances and determine the most effective cost-efficient solutions
for additional noise abatement. He thanked Council for their
continued attention to the community’s concerns regarding the new
facility. Mr. Kalishman said that now that construction is complete it is
time to consider additional measures for buffering and protection. He
said they appreciate that the Town is considering enhancing the tree
buffers, but there urgently needs to be more buffering as there is
persistent excessive noise that penetrates their homes and disrupts
their lives. He invited Council to visit their neighborhood at 6:30 a.m.
when operations are beginning to experience the daily noise and
vibrations firsthand. He said they respectfully ask for Council’s
utmost commitment to protecting the neighborhood.
Joan Spier, 1848 West Canning Drive stated last Tuesday, a public
services truck went down her street cutting overhanging limbs off the
roadway. She said when they got to her home, she spoke with the
crew chief and asked him what was going on and he told her they
would be pruning her tree. She asked him what the ordinance states,
and he replied they can prune twelve feet from over the sidewalk and
fourteen feet over the curb over the road. She said that seemed
extreme and asked if she could call Mr. Bernard and speak with him,
or take care of the pruning herself. He initially said no but Ms. Spier
did not move so they eventually left. Ms. Spier said she called Public
Services and asked what the ordinance stated, and they gave her the
same information. Ms. Spier checked the ordinance herself and it
states seven feet from the sidewalk and fifteen feet from the road.
She said they returned this morning at 7:50 a.m. and she told them
what the ordinance stated and he said he was going to prune the
tree. Ms. Spier said she was going to show him the ordinance and
when she went inside to get her phone to show him, he cut off one
side of her tree. She said we are a Tree City and this should not be
happening.
Billa Sperry, 2248 Cultura Circle said when she and her husband
purchased their town home, they were told there would not be an
issue with enclosing the garage which is part of the reason why they
purchased the town home, for the extra space. She said before they
enclosed it they checked with their flood insurance company and
were told that as long as they installed FEMA approved vents they
would be insured. She said if she has to remove that enclosure it will
make her nervous from a safety standpoint, as well as wildlife
intrusion. She asked Council to consider modifying the ordinance to
allow the enclosures.
Dan Ward, 2194 Cultura Circle stated he has gone through four
different requirements per Mount Pleasant. He said first they had
three-inch louvres with spacing, then six-inch louvres with spacing
with the forty percent. He said then they were told they could enclose
it as long as it had the vents, then he was told they could not enclose
it, that it must be screened or shadow-boxed. He said he has a letter
saying he could be fined five hundred dollars a day if this is not
cleared up. He said in 2021 the Town passed an ordinance to slow
the growth of the town, and the AE flood zone was re-evaluated. He
said he just wants this clarified.
Carol Knoche, 1651 Asher Avenue stated said they were not given
any information stating they were not allowed to enclose their garage
and they put in eight FEMA vents and breakaway walls. She said her
biggest concern is not having anything molding which is a huge
health issue.
Isaac Stappas, 1529 Asher Avenue stated they moved here in 2022
and they were excited to buy an elevated home in Liberty Hill Farm.
He said the additional space under the home was a huge selling
point. He said they were told by the builder they could enclose the
space to protect their belongings and offer privacy. He said this
ordinance is creating a difficult hardship as they would never have
purchased the home if they could not use it for its intended purpose.
Mr. Stappas said with the current flood ordinance guidelines as
written, it prohibits them to utilize the space, and makes it completely
useless due to weather, safety, security, animal and pest intrusion, as
well as financial losses due to tools and equipment ruined by mold,
rust, etc. He said most importantly the flood ordinance has severely
underscored their property values as prospective buyers see this as
an undesirable restriction. He stated his home is their most valuable
asset and the flood ordinance has dealt them a significant financial
blow if they wanted to sell their property. Mr. Stappas said there are
homes existing and new construction in north Mount Pleasant within
similar flood zones or worse that have been able to utilize this space
without retaliation from the Town. He is requesting that Town Council
take the time to study this matter and truly understand the hardship it
is creating, and he hopes a revision of the ordinance can be made to
satisfy all parties.
Laura Langevin, 2229 Cultura Circle stated they were living in
Oyster Point and needed to enclose their garage after two years due
to damage. They contacted their insurance company, they approved
the enclosure, a contractor was hired, and a permit was never
mentioned. She said they then moved to Liberty Hill Farm with the
same plan, called insurance, hired a different contractor and they
were never told they needed to request a permit. She said there are
a minimum of eight contractors who have enclosed garages in their
neighborhood who have never told anyone they needed a permit.
She said they asked their real estate agent, and they do not really
need to know codes, but this is a really big change that the Town has
made and their agents were unaware. She said after receiving a
       letter from the Town asking them to comply, they began to investigate
       what a non-conversion agreement was and were told by the Registrar
       of Deeds there is not one attached to their lot, and the closing
       attorney stated the same. She said they also informed her they
       should have never been given a building permit or certificate of
       occupancy. She said they also looked at the FEMA CRS
       coordinator’s manual and it states a non-conversion agreement must
       be filed with the deed and other property records so it will be effective
       with ownership if the property changes in the future. She said in
       reading the higher regulatory standards from the CRS manual, she
       found elements A through Q listed, and she sees that the Town has
       decided on G, enclosure limits, and right beside that in a box it says
       the community should not amend its ordinances solely to earn
       community rating system credit points, nor should it necessarily adopt
       the example from the CRS coordinator’s manual, and that ordinance
       language should be carefully written to support the community’s own
       goals and the purposes of it’s regulatory system. Ms. Langevin said
       she appreciates the Town trying to keep the CRS level at a 6, but at
       this time it is evident that there are multiple problems and rules have
       been violated from the Town’s lack of attaching the non-conversion
       agreement to properties in accordance with the FEMA guidelines to in
       her opinion neglecting to educating the community, from real estate
       agents, contractors and builders. She respectfully requests that
       Council come to a conclusion that we can all live with.
       Lori Johnson, 1601 Farmers Way stated they informed the builder
       they intended to enclose their garage, and had them install outlets in
       the garage so they could put a dehumidifier in. She said they did the
       work themselves to enclose their garage with breakaway boards, and
       she would never have purchased that house if they had known they
       were not able to enclose their garage. She said there is very limited
       storage space inside the home and their plan was to store their
       belongings in the garage. She said they are hoping to work with the
       Town to come to a resolution.
       Paul Langevin, 229 Cultura Circle said he appreciates the support of
       Veterans by Council as he is a Veteran and is one hundred percent
       disabled. He stated he has equipment in the garage that he uses
       daily, and he would appreciate it if Council would take a good look at
       this.
