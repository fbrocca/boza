Questions from Council on April 6, 2026

Q:1 Process for evaluating proposals for potential (not obvious) isolated
wetlands impacts at internal staff level.
1. Check for the signed affidavit from the owner of no known wetlands within the
   property boundaries and increase of density.
2. Internal evaluation to determine if the any wetlands are identified by any prior
   wetlands delineation, wetlands studies, watershed plans, National Resources
   Conservation Services (NRCS) inventory data for hydric soils, Or the US Fish
   and Wildlife National Wetlands Inventory.
3. Staff field inspection to visually verify database results, if deemed a potential. If
   not deemed a potential for impact, there will be no further wetlands evaluation.
4. If suspected onsite from field inspection, location is within general area of land
   disturbance where a potential impact may occur, a wetlands evaluation by a
   professional shall be required from the owner before further review of the
   application.
5. When deemed to be wetlands, the design is to comply with the ordinance with
   protective buffer prior to a plat approval or a building permit release.

Mr. Mitchell presented the wetlands protection ordinance, explaining
revisions based on four motions from the previous meeting. He outlined
the staff evaluation process for potential wetland impacts: checking for
properties under five acres with known wetlands or density increases,
consulting GIS databases and internet sources, conducting staff field
verification when indicated, and requiring professional wetland
delineation when necessary.
Mayor Pro Tem Chapman questioned the timing of staff assistance, and
Mr. Mitchell confirmed that GIS information and field visits would be
available during feasibility reviews, development review team meetings,
or building permit applications.
Town Attorney Pagliarini addressed concerns about town project
exemptions, explaining that the original amendment could create
problematic situations where planning commission might override town
council decisions. He proposed revised language splitting exemptions
into emergency infrastructure situations handled by professional
engineers and public projects approved by town council, with council
retaining authority to seek advisory opinions.
Council Member Rambo moved to amend the ordinance to adopt the
town attorney's proposed exemption language. The motion was
seconded by Council Member Hyatt and approved unanimously.
