     Ms. Corley asked what happens to the outdated equipment.
     Ms. Cotov stated that according to the Procurement Ordinance, staff
     in the Procurement Division places the outdated equipment on a site
     called “Gov Deals”, for sale. Once the equipment is sold, the revenue
     returns to the Town’s budget. She said a section was added to the
     ordinance to allow the Town to utilize a broker if staff feels they are
     able to obtain a better selling price, or if the broker is able to lower the
     purchase price of new equipment. She stated that the Town utilizes
     all these mechanisms to obtain the best pricing for the Town.
         All present voted in favor.

    Mr. Santos moved to approve; seconded by Mr. O’Rourke.
Mayor Haynie asked Mr. DeMoura to provide a brief explanation of
why the total amount proposed and the total amount recommended
differ. He stated that there is a difference of approximately $6 million.
Mr. DeMoura stated that the amended budget is different from the
original proposed budget, which received first reading prior to the
pandemic. He said that the amended budget is a responsive budget,
or a budget that has been developed in response to what is expected
to be a significant financial hit to the Town of Mount Pleasant. He
stated that the goal is to maintain services to the Town’s citizens and
business owners throughout the year. This budget contains a multi-
layered approach. He said the big issue is that the money that may
have been reserved for a particular project, or employee raises or
wage and classification implementation and salaries, have instead
been lifted and sent to a reserve account where it will sit and may
only be spent by Council. He said this funding is there and may only
be deployed depending on the conditions that the community is
facing going forward budgetarily.
