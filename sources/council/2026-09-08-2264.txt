Mr. Pagliarini presented the ordinance, explaining that it would
impose a 120-day temporary moratorium on applications for data
centers, using a definition derived from proposed state legislation,
with the intent of ensuring that smaller businesses would not be
inadvertently captured. The moratorium could be extended by Council
at any time, and Council would have authority to attach a pending
ordinance designation.
Mayor Haynie raised concern that the moratorium may be
unnecessary, noting that Mount Pleasant is located in a capacity use
area for groundwater where any entity drawing more than three
million gallons per month must obtain a state permit—a threshold
effectively preventing hyperscale data centers from locating in the
area. He questioned whether the ordinance was "window dressing"
given the practical impossibility of a hyperscale data center being
sited in Mount Pleasant.
Council Member Brownstein stated he was proactive in bringing this
forward after discovering that certain zoning designations allow data
centers by right without any public process, particularly given
anticipated changes in the Long Point Road area. He acknowledged
the mayor's point on water availability but argued the ordinance was
a safeguard to prevent the town from appearing in headlines like
other areas of the state.
                                                     Town Council Meeting
                                                       September 8, 2026
                                                             Page 9 of 54

Mayor Pro Tem Chapman shared that he attended a seminar at the
Municipal Association that included discussion of a data center
proposed for Dorchester County. He stated that a short moratorium
was appropriate to ensure the town had the necessary ordinances in
place.
Council Member Tinkey raised the importance of ensuring that Mount
Pleasant Waterworks—which is planning a data center-style facility
using solar power as part of its water processing infrastructure—
would not be impaired by the moratorium. He also noted that the
state General Assembly was expected to take up the issue and
suggested the moratorium timeline align with legislative action.
Council Member Iacofano asked whether planning could begin
working on the underlying ordinance during the 120-day period, and
received confirmation that the Planning Committee could move
proposals forward to the Planning Commission in the interim.
Council Member Rambo expressed support for the moratorium as a
redundancy safeguard and indicated he would favor a longer period
on second reading.
Council Member Hyatt stated she did not see harm in being proactive
and addressing gaps in the zoning code, even if it was unlikely a data
center would actually locate in Mount Pleasant.
Mayor Haynie reiterated his opposition, characterizing the moratorium
as an unnecessary and empty gesture given that the conditions for a
hyperscale data center—water, land, and electricity—do not exist in
Mount Pleasant. Council Member Whitaker also stated she would
vote no.
Council Member Rambo moved for first reading, seconded by Mayor
Pro Tem Chapman. Council Member Whitaker and Mayor Haynie
voted no with all others present voting in favor. The motion carried
with a 7-2 vote.

Mr. Matt Brady briefed the Council on the history of the Faison Road
property, a roughly 38-acre tract purchased by the town in 2021 and
2022. Following an unsuccessful public-private partnership
solicitation process and a failed sale contract in 2024, the property
remains town-owned. He noted that no single preferred vision had
emerged and framed three paths for Council consideration: take no
action and retain flexibility; issue a new Request for Proposals with
clearer expectations; or pursue another direction. The Economic
Development Committee's recommendation from its most recent
meeting was to take no action.
Council Member Brownstein updated the full Council that on the
preceding Friday, he, along with Council Members Tinkey and
Iacofano had met with representatives from the YMCA of Coastal
Carolina and the YMCA of Greater Charleston—specifically David
Bird, Dave Curry, and Paul Stoney. He reported that the YMCA
representatives expressed interest in potentially utilizing a portion of
the Faison Road site, noting its proximity to senior housing and
multiple neighborhoods as ideal characteristics. They proposed
                                                      Town Council Meeting
                                                        September 8, 2026
                                                             Page 42 of 54

senior programming, and natatorium as an anchor tenant.

childhood education, senior programming and a natatorium as an
anchor tenant for the town’s Faison Road property and to report back
to the Economic Development Committee, seconded by Council
Member Iacofano.

Tree Task Force Chair James McKee presented the final report on
behalf of the seven-member committee. He emphasized that despite
the members' diverse backgrounds, they were united in the belief that
trees are important to the residents of Mount Pleasant and provide
measurable benefits including cooling, flood mitigation, and
community livability.
Key findings included that the latest canopy study shows a 54 percent
canopy coverage—an improvement—but that 1,200 acres of
plantable area remain across the town. The public tree inventory also
identified 1,300 locations where a tree once stood but no longer does,
representing significant replanting opportunities.
The Task Force offered two primary recommendations. First, the
Council should establish a goal of planting 1,500 additional trees per
year and hire a dedicated staff leader to drive toward that goal,
coordinate community engagement, and develop a planting plan. Mr.
McKee noted that the town currently has no measurable tree
gain/loss tracking in place. Second, the Task Force recommended
targeted ordinance changes, including: modifying the tree protection
zone to allow more flexibility for encroachment so that developers are
more likely to retain trees rather than remove them; extending
minimum canopy tree requirements to residential construction;

Mayor Haynie presented the ordinance, noting that South Carolina
Code Section 5-7-250 provides that special meetings may be held on
the call of the mayor or a majority of the members of the body—
meaning five of nine council members. He stated that he had
surveyed multiple neighboring municipalities, all of which follow state
law on this provision, except for one, and that the Municipal
Association confirms the same interpretation.
Mr. Pagliarini provided historical context, noting that in 2019 Council
changed the threshold for placing an item on an agenda from one
member to three, and at that time the special meeting provision was
considered roughly equivalent. He acknowledged that the ordinance
as written is ambiguous, that a conservative reading supports the
majority-of-members interpretation, and that he would defer to state
law if directed by Council.
Council Member Brownstein suggested the impetus for the ordinance
appeared to be a special council meeting he had requested alongside
Council Members Crosby and Tinkey in early August to hear from
residents of Carolina Park regarding the Faison Road property. He
stated the meeting was held on an existing committee day, required
no additional scheduling burden on staff, and produced no formal
action. He characterized the proposed change as concentrated
control that would weaken individual council members' ability to
                                                     Town Council Meeting
                                                       September 8, 2026
                                                            Page 52 of 54

represent their constituents, and stated he saw no reason to cede
any of Council's powers absent evidence of abuse.
Mayor Haynie replied that this was not personal and was simply a
matter of aligning the town's code with state law, as all other
surveyed municipalities had done. He stated that a precedent of
allowing three council members to call special meetings could result
in repeated requests from resident groups and expressed his
commitment to operating within the bounds of the mayor's authority
under law.
Council Member Iacofano and Council Member Rambo expressed
opposition to the change, with Council Member Iacofano arguing that
calling a meeting is not the same as taking action and that requiring a
governing majority merely to hold a conversation concentrates power
inappropriately.
Mayor Pro Tem Chapman observed that the committee meeting
structure already provides a venue for three members to place items
before their colleagues, and expressed comfort with following state
law.
Council Member Whitaker noted that any random day special
meeting presents a greater concern than meetings on established
committee days, and expressed confidence that council members
would generally be open to discussing issues when asked.
Mayor Haynie moved for approval, seconded by Mayor Pro Tem
Chapman. Upon a roll call vote, Mayor Pro Tem Chapman, Council
Members Whitaker, Crosby, Hyatt, Tinkey, and Mayor Haynie voted in
favor, with Council Members Brownstein, Rambo and Iacofano voting
opposed. The motion carried with a 6-3 vote.
