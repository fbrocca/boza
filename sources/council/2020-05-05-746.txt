IV.       Presentation by Charleston Regional Development Authority
      on re | IGNITE regional steps to safely re-open businesses
      Brent Jonas, of CRDA stated that he would like to introduce General
      Hank Taylor. He stated that the CRDA was approached by Mayor
      Tecklenburg of the City of Charleston and Chairman Elliott Summey
      of Charleston County Council with a request for CRDA to lead the
      region’s Return to Work Safely. He said over the past two and a half
      weeks, through their One Region program they have partnered with
      approximately 28 organizations, which continues to grow, to discover
      a way to open the region safely.
      General Hank Taylor, Chairman of the One Region Executive
      Committee, stated that they have been working together as a region
      for quite some time, particularly when it comes to economic
      development challenges. He stated that this goes back to the closure
      of the Naval Shipyard when the three counties decided to come
      together to form the CRDA to work together as a region for economic
      challenges. He said there has been good success over the past
      twenty years and they continue to work together and execute
      strategies. He said One Region is based on four basic values; (1) To
      be a strong and resilient economy; (2) to have opportunities where
      everyone strives; (3) to be balanced on how opportunities are
      accepted and making sure we continue to keep what makes us
      unique in the region; and (4) to be a connected community. He said
      they are executing a One Region strategy to take on the economic
      challenges and at the same time, there is a new challenge - a public
      health challenge. He stated that they have been working with their
      medical community to strategize how they can safely balance the
      public health challenge as well as the economic development
      strategy. They have in place a cross section of the community on
      their executive board; from non-profits to businesses to banks and
      volunteers. He said as they go about getting commerce back open
      and running, they added values. The first value is that they are
      putting together an overall plan to indicate how they will approach this
      as a region – one region to reignite the economy, and at the same
      time, balancing and making sure that safety is at the forefront. The
      first step, in the next week or so, we will see an overall plan that
      comes from the CRDA. Secondly, they will be looking at individual

commerce up and running, they will need consumers, who will only
come out if they feel safe. He said they are well on their way with

V.      First and Final Reading: An Emergency Ordinance to
     temporarily authorize the use of new or additional areas for
     outdoor dining and retail services. (Ord. No. 20028)
     Mr. DeMoura stated that from the staff perspective, they have been
     listening to members of Council and with the leadership of
     Councilwoman Landing, Chair of Economic Development, as well as
     members of this committee, this ordinance is a way to assist retailers
     and restauranteurs during this time.
     Ms. Corley stated that the ordinance is very well done.
     Mr. Pagliarini stated that this is an emergency ordinance and requires
     an affirmative vote by two-thirds of Council members. He said with
     seven Council members present, this will require five favorable votes
     to pass.
        Mr. Santos made a motion to approve; seconded by Ms. Corley.
     Mr. Rambo stated that being part owner of a restaurant, he does not
     feel this meets the guidelines for him to recuse; however, out of an
     abundance of caution, he would like to abstain.
     Ms. Landing stated that in reference to the first item, for those
     restaurants who do not typically provide outdoor service, the
                                                                     Page 6 of 11
      ordinance indicates that they must first apply to the Planning
      Department. She asked how this will be handled in terms of volume.
      Ms. Farrell stated that everything is in place and staff’s intention is not
      to slow anything down. Staff has been working since yesterday on
      this ordinance as well as an application that they will likely launch this
      evening or first thing in the morning. She said there will be a link to
      the Town’s website. The business owner will receive an
      acknowledgement from the Planning Department and if there are any
      issues, the Planning Department will contact them. Otherwise, they
      are able to operate their business. She said what will be beneficial to
      the business is that an external map will be available so the public will
      be able to see those businesses participating on the Town’s website.
      She said this is a benefit to help promote any of the businesses that
      are participating.
      Ms. Landing asked if a restaurant that currently provides outdoor
      dining and wishes to expand their outdoor dining area, will also have
      to apply.
      Ms. Farrell stated that they would still need to fill out the form,
      because part of staff’s thought is that the restaurant may wish to use
      parking spaces or more sidewalk and staff needs to review to
      determine if those spaces are used as handicap parking or impedes
      emergency access. She said the process is very simple.
             Mr. Rambo abstained. All others voted in favor. Motion carries 6-
      0-1.
VI.       First and Final Reading: An Emergency Ordinance replacing
      Emergency Ordinance 20025 and Temporarily Suspending the
      Operating Procedures of Town Council Meeting and other
      Boards and Commissions; and Other Emergency Provisions
      related to COVID-19 Outbreak. (Ord. No. 20029)
      Mr. DeMoura stated that Legal Counsel is prepared to discuss both
      items VI and VII.
      Mr. Pagliarini stated that this is essentially a replacement of
      emergency ordinance 20025, which was passed by Town Council on
      March 17, 2020. This ordinance was only valid for 61 days by State
      Law; therefore, a decision must be made either to allow it to expire or
      move forward essentially renewing it with a new ordinance. He said
      the Police, Judicial and Legal Committee voted to extend the
      ordinance with minor changes, which are: normal operating
                                                                       Page 7 of 11
procedures for Town Council are suspended just as they were on
March 17, 2020 and also included Planning Commission, Board of
Zoning Appels and Commercial Design Review Board. He said this
does not mean that this is a vote for approval that those bodies must
meet; however, if they must meet, it allows them to do so. He said
this is the first change. He said the second change is that the
termination date on this ordinance is not shown as 61 days, but the
Committee felt that June 30, 2020 was appropriate as a termination
date. He said Council may change or extend if they so wish. This also
includes the same provision as previously passed, which is that the
Town Administrator is authorized to enact plans to ensure continuity
and delivery of government services. The Town Administrator may
revoke or cancel special events or permits and it also discusses the
price gouging prohibition, as well as deadlines imposed by Town
ordinance are continued to be told. He said there is very important
information in this ordinance.
Mr. Rambo directed his question to Legal Council and stated that at
the Police, Judicial and Legal Committee yesterday, Committee
members were discussing the timeframe for when this should end.
He said it was originally suggested for the end of May 2020 and it
was then changed until the end of June 2020, because Ms. Whitley
addressed the price gouging rule. He asked if this was necessary,
because the price gouging law is state law; therefore, whether this
ordinance expires or not, the law would still be in effect.
Mr. Pagliarini stated that Mr. Rambo is correct. He said as reflected in
the ordinance, it is South Carolina Code Section 395145. He said this
was requested by a Council member the first time to be included in
this document; however, it is not necessary.
Mr. Rambo stated that with this in mind, he would like to make an
amendment.
    Mr. Rambo moved to amend the new ordinance to have an end
date of May 30, 2020; seconded by Ms. Corley.
    Mr. Rambo stated that he meant the last day of May which is
actually May 31, 2020 and would like to amend as such; Ms. Corley
amended her second.
Ms. Whitley asked Mr. DeMoura if the Town is prepared to go back to

VIII.       Discussion and possible action to amend gathering rules in
        parks
             Mr. Santos moved to modify Phase 2 of the Mount Pleasant
        Recreation Department’s re-opening plan to allow groups to gather in
        accordance with the CDC guidelines in the Town’s parks and facilities
        that are currently open. This will take effect immediately. He stated
        that this would also include Pitt Street Park to open effective Friday,
        May 8, 2020; seconded by Mr. Rambo.
        Mr. O’Rourke stated that he believes there will be some very happy
        residents regarding Pitt Street Bridge. He asked if there was an
        operational discussion by Police Chief Ritchie as it relates to assisting
        with a traffic pattern that would not enable people to actually gather.
        Mr. Santos stated that Chief Ritchie may comment on this; however,
        as proven by the gatherings previously, there would be no need.
        Ms. Landing asked if people are allowed now to put a blanket down at
        Memorial Waterfront Park and not have to keep moving.
        Mr. Santos stated that residents will still need to abide by CDC
        guidelines and as long as they follow the six foot rule, then they are
        able to sit on a blanket and picnic.
        Ms. Landing stated that a family can be together but maintain six feet
        with anyone else.
        Mr. Santos responded in the affirmative.
        Mr. O’Rourke stated that there appears to be more consistency with
        what is going on in the Town anyway. He said it only makes sense to
        do this in the parks as well.
        Ms. Whitley asked if the playgrounds with all the shared equipment
        will still be closed in Phase 2.
        Mr. Santos stated that there are no changes in the initial Phase 2 that
        was approved. He said it is only being amended to include the
        gathering and to include re-opening of the Pitt Street Bridge Park. All
        else remains the same.
        Ms. Whitley asked if Police Chief Ritchie or Recreation Director,
        Steve Gergick could provide Council with information on how things
        have gone thus far since the parks have re-opened.
        Police Chief Ritchie stated that his department has been working very
        well together with Mr. Gergick and the Recreation staff. He said there
                                                                      Page 10 of 11
      have been no issues at the parks. He said the few that were not really
      considered an issue, were people sitting down and when asked to
      move, they complied. He said thus far, it has gone very well through
      the entire pandemic.
      Mr. Rambo directed his question to Legal Counsel. He stated that the
      public is allowed to sit eight people at an outside restaurant table, and
      they do not have to be your own family. He said with what is now
      being done with the parks, may a group of four or five kids get
      together and have a group workout or throw the football around or
      play two-on-two basketball. We are now saying this is not illegal.
      Mr. Pagliarini stated that this was discussed at PJL Committee. He
      said executive order 2020-31 issued by Governor McMaster over the
      weekend states that Law Enforcement Officers of the State may
      prohibit or disburse any congregation or gathering of people unless
      authorized or they are in their homes, in groups of three or more
      people, if any such congregation or gathering is deemed to pose or
      could pose a threat to public health. He stated that it is gray;
      however, it seems to provide some leeway to Law Enforcement to
      impose as they see fit.
      Mr. O’Rourke stated that the reason there have been no issues is due
      to the culture of the Town’s Police Department. They are going to
      make sure that people are safe, and they do so with discussions, not
      force.
          All present voted in favor.
