                   SPECIAL TOWN COUNCIL MEETING
                         Tuesday March 12, 2024
                               4:45 p.m.
                  Municipal Complex, Council Chambers
             100 Ann Edwards Lane, Mount Pleasant, SC 29464

                                    MINUTES

1.       Call to Order 4:45
     Mayor Haynie called the meeting to order at 4:45 p.m. Daniel Brownstein,
     Howard Chapman, Laura Hyatt, Guang Ming Whitley, Jake Rambo, John
     Iacofano, Gary Santos and Carl Ritchie were all present.
2.       Public Comment
          Marie-Louise Moreto, 1133 Liberty Court stated the arts deserve the
     same thought, discussion, and planning, including input, that is given to
     new sports fields and sports facilities. She said for an arts center to
     succeed, it must work for the art organizations and the individual artists
     who will use it. She said to her knowledge, no input has been sought
     either from art organizations, individual artists, or the general public on a
     community arts center. Ms. Moreto said there also did not appear to be
     a real plan to move this forward or how it will progress in the long run.
     She stated her organization, the Mount Pleasant Community Arts Center
     Committee held a brainstorming session on February 6. She requested
     that Council reconsider the RFP which was posted on a national hiring
     platform, but only three applicants applied. She said instead of having an
     artistic director/operator, she asks that Council consider hiring a cultural
     planner or specialized theater consultant to guide the process. She said
     a community arts center can be an economic benefit to the town if it is
     done right. She urged Council to have the courage and foresight to think
     long term as a quality arts center will pay for itself many times over in
     direct and indirect economic impact.
         Denise Fugo, Executive Director of Housing For All Mount Pleasant,
     2222 Ashley River Road stated she wanted to let Council know that the
     board is supportive of the affordable housing that is recommended on
     Faison Road, and they hope that project will move forward quickly.
         Jill Kajouras, 3657 Maidstone Drive said that we are experiencing a
     desire for the attainable housing project number two for Carolina Park
Boulevard when already there is an appreciated, moving forward project
of attainable housing on Faison Road. She said the Faison project is
thirty-eight acres, although only eleven acres has been approved for
housing, which will be thirty units. Ms. Kajouras said the rest of the land
has yet to be determined as to what it will be, however, adding in another
one hundred units on Carolina Park Boulevard which already has
incredibly heavy traffic, is absolutely unwanted by the taxpayers, and she
said she would speak again at the next meeting. She said so those who
live in Carolina Park, Park West, Charleston National, who have paid
dearly for their homes and for which they have worked all of their lives, do
not want the second project. She said there is already one in place, and
forcing a second is absolutely undesirable.
      Kevin Cunnane, 3032 River Vista Way said he wanted to speak
about the town manager’s contract. He said he was sure there was a
compensation study out there that shows what cities he is compared to in
the state. He said he thinks he does a good job, but if he is compared to
five cities for his role, the police and firefighters should be compared to
those cities as well. He said if the town manager gets a three and a half
percent raise every year, make sure the firefighters get it and it is not
doled out based on favoritism. Mr. Cunnane said when politics enters the
role of the manager, speaking about the role, not the current encumbent,
it is a very poisonous problem. He said Council is in charge, and there
should be safeguards to make sure that politics does not enter that role,
and that nobody in elected office is using any town resources for politics.
He said in January there was an article put out by the Town that the Mayor
is one of the 50 most influential people in Charleston. He asked how that
helps us as taxpayers. He said that goes back to not letting the Town be
taken over by politics, which is why we have the strong council system.
     Carol Hallman, Parsonage Woods Lane stated she would like to be
sure that we are calling attention to the big picture regarding the proposal
for a temporary community arts center as a transition point to possibly a
larger facility. She said her biggest concern at this point is that we bring
in professional leadership that knows the pitfalls and potential problems if
we do not plan for the vision that the RFP set forth which is choral music,
orchestra music, visual arts, theater, and so forth. Ms. Hallman said she
keeps hearing it called a theater, and that is just a portion of the intended
use. She said she hopes that Council will take advantage of the
opportunity to hire someone who has the professional experience in

      Howard Chapman, 824 Natchez Circle stated he is the Chairman of
the Public Services Committee. He stated there has been some
information in e-mails and public comments about the efforts that our
Public Services made on Saturday with the heavy storm. He said first of
all, for those that did not see the news, the storm we had was a record
rainfall of over three inches in a short period of time, and it came with a
flood tide. He said having been through a number of hurricanes, that flood
tide was pretty high, and there is nowhere for the rain to go. He said most
importantly, our Public Services employees started on Thursday going out
and inspecting the hot spots around town that normally flood. He said
they also did the same thing a second time on Friday. Mr. Chapman said
on Saturday morning there were trucks and personnel out ready to
respond to locations such as Pitt and Ferry Streets, Royall and
Williamson, Bowman and the Highway 17 on-ramp, Chimney Bluff Drive,
Shem Creek Park parking lot, Rifle Range Road, Mathis Ferry Road, and
Long Point Road. He said they were out there when everyone was being
told to stay off the roads. Mr. Chapman said our Public Services
employees were doing what they could but with three inches of rain and
a flood tide, that water was not going to go anywhere. He said the flood
tide is causing the water to come in, not go out. He said he is here to
thank the Public Services crews who worked very hard over the weekend.
Mr. Chapman added that Police and Fire worked hard as well, but they
were not being criticized.
     Barbara Ferris stated she is an artist and a sculptor and said a true
arts center for the arts will distinguish Mount Pleasant from other towns.
She said this has been under discussion for several years as a temporary
space is being considered, and she believes this is a first step in
developing an extraordinary city. She said Mount Pleasant is already a
financial and retail center, and a center for the arts will enhance residents’
quality of life. Ms. Ferris said the center reaches beyond the ballfields for
the youth and will serve the entire community. She said many cities with
populations similar to Mount Pleasant in growth potential have built state
of the arts centers, and it has been documented that once it is established,
the theater and visual arts will bring millions of dollars of revenue into the
cities and the business communities. She stated center for the arts will
boost the image of Mount Pleasant and spur economic growth. Ms. Ferris
said there is a lot of work to be done to have a state of the arts building
