1. Call to order
Mayor Will Haynie called the special town council meeting to order on
Monday, October 6, 2025 at 11:00 AM.

2. Public Comments
Mayor Haynie opened the floor for public comments, allowing speakers
2.5 minutes each.
Ellen Moore thanked Council Members Santos, Iacofano, Rambo, and
Whitley for their years of service. She expressed support for the wage
and compensation study and noted that nearly 25% of firefighters are in
their first year and require mentoring from experienced leadership. She
highlighted concerns about low police numbers compared to other
municipalities, especially considering that 10 SROs are included in that
count. She referenced study recommendations, emphasizing the
importance of investing in town employees. The need for more police
patrols and Chief Mixon's indication that fire trucks often operate with
only 3 firefighters.
Lisa Schnipke questioned why council was considering budget and
compensation changes without input from department heads. She
expressed confidence in the town administrator and department heads,
suggesting that staffing changes should go through the regular
budgeting process where department heads can make requests through
their committees.
Mike Van Horn addressed the timing of the wage study, acknowledging
that while people hate paying taxes, it's important to take care of those
who protect residents. He noted that first responders put their lives on
the line with every 911 call and that many are leaving to work outside
Mount Pleasant due to inadequate pay. He pointed out that all positions
except the fire chief are behind other municipalities in compensation. He
also noted the town's revenue is 58% higher than last year and the
police department budget is 2% less than last year. He suggested
changing the timing of processes so the wage study occurs before the
comprehensive plan for better cohesion.
Kevin Cunnane shared his concerns about the fire department's
response times, citing a neighbor who died in a March fire. He stated it
took 8.5 minutes for the fire department to reach his neighbor from the
nearby station, and the Charleston crew from Cainhoy took 10 minutes.
He criticized the lack of proper staffing and equipment, noting that
Charleston firefighters had to enter the building three times, creating
cardiac risks. He expressed frustration that no one on council seemed to
care about determining appropriate staffing levels for police and fire
departments. He accused the administration of manipulating crime
statistics and noted that patrol cars are only responding to calls rather
than proactively patrolling neighborhoods.
Mayor Haynie closed public comments.

3. Discussion and possible action to implement the proposed 2025
Wage Study, and consideration and possible action of the addition
of personnel to the police and fire departments
Mayor Haynie began with a timeline of events leading to the special
meeting. He noted that on September 23rd at 12:23 PM, Councilman
Iacofano requested this special meeting within 24 hours of the millage
rate vote. The Mayor criticized that this request bypassed committees
and single-handedly instructed staff to develop recommendations for
applying the town's new millage capacity. He noted the agenda was
changed at 4:30 PM Thursday to include "consideration and possible
action" for additional personnel, after committee agendas were already
set.
The Mayor expressed concerns about bypassing the committee process,
noting that only the HR committee had seen the wage study report and
they had not voted on it. He stated that fire, police, judicial and legal, and
finance committees were being bypassed with only 3 people providing
public input at this special meeting rather than at a regular monthly
council meeting.
Mayor Haynie reminded council that the millage vote debate was cut
short at Ms. Whitley's request due to a medical appointment for her
child, and that if the four opposing members had not agreed to that
request, the vote would have failed 4-4. He criticized the lack of
transparency, noting there was no advance information to the public
about a 15% property tax increase and no public hearing.
The Mayor stated this was political theater scheduled 30 days before an
election, noting council never does budget items this close to an
election. He emphasized that new members could be on the body in less
than 30 days and they would oversee the $5.2 million annual windfall
from the second major tax increase in two years.
Mayor Haynie cited positive statistics showing crime numbers are down,
attrition is down, and 24 new fire recruits are in training. He questioned
various aspects of the wage study that needed clarification, including
that it focused only on base pay not full benefits packages, that
educational incentives are a fraction of peer organizations, and Chief
Arnold's request for more rapid response vehicles.
Mayor Haynie moved to immediately devise an amendment to the
budget ordinance that would be first reading when delivered from the
attorney to implement immediately the $1.5 million recommended in
phase 1 of the wage study, with the source of funding left to the
discretion of the town administrator. Part B of the motion was to formally
accept the 2025 wage study and refer it to all appropriate standing
council committees for in-depth analysis and review, for them to vote on
recommendations after receiving input from staff and send those back to
council.
The motion was seconded by Councilman Chapman.
Councilman Chapman, as chairman of the fire committee, noted they
had not met to discuss improvements with the fire department. He
suggested several things might be as important as wage increases,
including the difficulty for public service employees to afford living in
Mount Pleasant. He agreed the town needs to implement the $1,537,000
from the report immediately.
Chapman questioned why the fire committee doesn't have the
opportunity to review the wage study as it relates to the fire department,
especially when mid-year budget revisions are suggested. He noted the
fire department has 21 recruits in training and cited attrition rates: police
at 13% in 2022, 11% in 2023, and 13% in 2024; fire at 14% in 2023 and
11% in 2024, which he characterized as "pretty good" compared to other
areas.
Councilman Tinkey emphasized that every council member supports
police and fire and compensating them competitively. He clarified it's not
about whether to fund raises but how to fund them. He supported
passing the $1.5 million immediately while taking time to carefully review
other additions through the committee process.
Councilman Iacofano criticized the Mayor for "hijacking" the meeting and
making a grandstanding speech before the motion. He pointed out the
fire committee had only met twice in two years. He questioned whether
attrition rates include vacant positions and asked if the town is losing
employees to other municipalities at an alarming rate.
Chief Mixon reported losing 3 firefighters to Goose Creek, 2 to
Charleston, 1 to St. John's Fire Department, and others to the private
sector last year. He explained attrition is calculated based on 132 full-
time employees, noting they're typically fully staffed after spring recruit
school but experience attrition throughout the year.
Chief Arnold explained police calculate turnover based on actual
employees, not budgeted positions. He noted they lost 6 officers to other
local agencies two years ago and a couple this year, though last year
was an anomaly due to the election.
Councilman Iacofano distinguished between the 1% sales tax for tourism
($240 million) and the current discussion about public safety. He
emphasized his concern about ensuring public safety personnel are
retained and compensated fairly, stating he doesn't want to find out what
happens in 4 years without adequate public safety staffing.
Councilman Brownstein regretted how personal the discussion had
become and questioned why a motion was made to prevent hearing
staff's prepared information. He shared that at the morning's HR
committee meeting, they saw examples of police and firefighter
paychecks: a first-year police officer's take-home pay for 2 weeks was
$1,700, while a firefighter's was $1,200. He stated the time for talk is
over and it's time for action.
Councilwoman Hyatt believed everyone wants to pay first responders
and address the wage study as soon as possible, potentially starting in 2
weeks. She supported implementing the wage study with excess funding
going back to committees for review. She felt a more deliberative
process would get more citizens on board, noting funds will not arrive
until January.
Councilwoman Whitley requested to see the staff presentation to
understand the proposal before voting on the motion. She wanted to
know how much of the $5 million would be spent immediately versus
what would remain for committee consideration.
Wage Study Recommendation
CFO Tammy Harness presented the staff recommendation showing:
  ● $5,284,000 in expected revenue (after accounting for non-
    payment)
  ● Phase 1 wage study: $1,537,000
  ● Additional requested positions: $2,114,000 (12 total positions - 6
    firefighters, 4 corporals, 2 CSOs)
  ● Remaining for salary increases: $1,633,000 (with a 4% increase
    costing $1,305,000)
Harness explained the first-year costs for new positions include vehicles
and equipment, with ongoing costs being lower in subsequent years.
Councilwoman Whitley moved to amend the mayor's motion to fulfill the
additional requested positions (12 members of fire and police
departments) along with the Phase 1 wage study, with the remainder
going back to committees for determination on deployment.
The motion to amend was seconded by Mayor Haynie.
During discussion of the amendment, Chief Mixon explained the 6
additional firefighters would allow 4-person minimum staffing on 2
additional engines, working toward the NFPA industry standard. Chief
Arnold explained the 4 corporals would provide proper span of control
with a sergeant and 2 corporals per team, and the 2 CSOs would be
workforce multipliers handling minor incidents so sworn officers can
focus on enforcement.
Councilman Rambo stated he would vote no on the amendment
because he wanted to see both the Phase 1 wage study and the 4%
increase implemented immediately, with potential further discussion on
additional positions.
Councilman Iacofano expressed concern about not earmarking money
now, fearing it could go to "pet projects" rather than public safety as
intended by the 5 council members who voted for the tax increase.
The vote on Councilwoman Whitley's amendment passed 6-3, with
Brownstein, Iacofano, and Rambo voting no.
Councilman Brownstein then moved to amend by adding the 4% raise to
the motion.
The motion to amend was seconded by Councilman Iacofano.
HR Director Christy DeLuca presented slides showing that even with the
4% increase and educational incentives, Mount Pleasant would still be
$4,796 below Goose Creek for fire and $4,158 below Charleston for
police with bachelor's degrees.
Discussion continued about whether passing this amendment would fully
appropriate all funds with nothing remaining for committees to evaluate.
Council members debated the timing, with some arguing for immediate
action to support first responders and others advocating for committee
review.
Councilman Tinkey reiterated his support for raises but wanted time to
examine the full compensation package mix, including possible housing
allowances and other benefits.
The vote on Brownstein's amendment failed 5-4, with Council Members
Chapman, Hyatt, Santos, Tinkey, and Mayor Haynie voting no, and
Council Members Brownstein, Whitley, Rambo and Iacofano voting in
favor.
Council then voted on the main motion as amended by Councilwoman
Whitley's amendment. All council members voted yes, passing the
motion unanimously. This approved implementation of the Phase 1
wage study ($1,537,000) and authorization for 12 additional positions (6
firefighters, 4 police corporals, 2 CSOs), with the remainder going to
committees for review.

4. Adjourn
There being no further business, the meeting was adjourned at 12:29
p.m.
