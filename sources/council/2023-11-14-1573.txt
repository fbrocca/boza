VI.   PUBLIC COMMENT

           David Kalishman, 2135 Annie Laura Lane stated he is here with
      a contingent of his neighbors to discuss concerns with the fleet
      maintenance facility adjacent to their neighborhood. He said they
      understand the necessity of the site, but they do not think the current
      plans are sufficient to abate the noise. They are making two requests.
      One is to extend the buffer, and the second is to conduct a proper
      sound assessment once the facility is complete. He said he would like
      Council to see the issues they are facing in person, and they would like
      to work with Council to resolve the issues. He thanked the Mayor and
      Council for their time.

           Cari Stafford, 2139 Annie Laura Lane stated there is a need to
      extend the buffer near the fleet maintenance facility. She said they are
      very close to the facility, and they are being exposed to constant
      auditory and visual disruptions. She said they would like the Town to
      work to fortify a dense buffer. She said it is currently insufficient.

            Pat Gillespie, 2182 Annie Laura Lane stated the conclusion of
      the construction of the new maintenance facility is coming soon, and
      there are still problems with noise and dust. He stated with respect to
      the noise, Montomery County Maryland has stopped the use of tonal
      back-up alarms as they have come up with a new way to alert people.
      Instead of making the tonal noise, the truck emits a white noise sound
      to where there is an object in the path behind the truck. He said it is
      quiet and dissipates quickly, but the person in the path knows it is
      there. He said with tonal alarms, most people ignore it because they
      don’t know where it is coming from. He asked the Town to test it and
      see how it works. He said he hopes something can be done about the
      dust.

       Marie-Louise Moreto, 1133 Liberty Court stated she is
representing the Mount Pleasant Community Arts Center Committee
(MPCAC). She stated they have been advocating for a community arts
center since 2016, and she would like to applaud Mayor Haynie, Town
Council and the Culture, Arts and Pride Commission for seriously

     Myra Jones, 730 Dragoon Drive stated she hoped Council had
the opportunity to review her letter about the need for a hate crimes

for more attainable housing and it is time the town had an arts facility.

      Michael Jerome Singleton, 2985 Joseph Glover Road stated he
grew up in Mount Pleasant and graduated from Wando High School in
1978. He said he would appreciate the support of Council to rezone
his land so that he could take care of his family.

      Keely Enright, 1245 Dearsley Court stated after the Village
Playhouse outgrew their space in 2012, they moved downtown as
there was no where for them to go here. She said they left a vacuum
that was never filled. She said people in Mount Pleasant have the
desire and the means to cultivate and support a community arts venue
that can offer a myriad of programming options. She urged Council to
adopt the Culture, Arts and Pride Commission’s recommendations.

       Mary Smith, 1161 Sharpstown Court stated she lives in a
development called Porchers Preserve and there are only two ways to
get through the development. She said the only way you can exit
Sharpstown Court onto Billy Swails is by making a right turn. She said
when the Market at Oakland project is completed it is going to be
difficult to get onto Porchers Bluff Road. She said this is a very small
development so she does not know if they are being taken into
consideration with the changes that will be made, and perhaps there
will be a traffic light needed.

              Scott Schmitz, 1193 Sharpstown Court stated he would like
       Council to reject the Market at Oakland conceptual plan due to the cost
       to install an unsignalized intersection at Porchers Bluff and Bella Oaks
       Drive. He said the traffic study shows it will make it much more difficult
       to exit their community, and it does not account for a single vehicle that

       Association and is here to speak in favor of the resolution encouraging
       Governor McMaster to declare an economic disaster due to the
       dumping of imported shrimp into the United States markets. He said
       they have seen an influx of imported shrimp for the past four or five
       years and it has increased drastically. He said as of 2022, 2.1B pounds
       of shrimp were imported into the United States and 1.7B pounds have
       been consumed. He said Mount Pleasant would be the second town
       in South Carolina to adopt such a measure.

IX.        COMMITTEE ACTION ITEMS

      A.       Health, Recreation & Events Committee

            Discussion and possible action regarding implementation of the
            Culture, Arts & Pride Commission’s recommendation for a Town
            Community Arts Center

            November 6, 2023 Health, Recreation & Events Committee
            Minutes

               Ms. Whitley moved to approve the Culture, Arts and Pride
            Commission plan, seconded by Mr. Iacofano.

            Ms. Hyatt said the Culture, Arts and Pride Commission’s
            recommendation was to put out the RFP and to allow the person
            selected to choose the location. She said the Health, Recreation
            and Events Committee would like Council to choose the location
            and put out the RFP. She said the Committee would like to hear
            what Council would like to do before a decision is made.

            Ms. Whitley said drafting the RFP, releasing it, gathering
            applicants and selecting someone is in her opinion going to be a
            six-month process and this concept has been sitting before
            Council for months. She said she would like to implement the
            Culture, Arts and Pride Commission’s plan and in the interim, Mr.
            Brady could, through action of Council, be directed to actively
            seek out options, and if one would come into play before the RFP
            goes out, move forward with it, and then the new person would
            have the role of getting the location upfitted to be used for the
            community. She said her concern with waiting until Council finds
            a location is that we will never find one. She reminded the public
            that this will be an interim space for maybe five to seven years to
            be a proof point that would demonstrate the need for a dedicated
            arts facility in the community. She said we need to take action
            and not delay this further.

            Mayor said he thought by the motion to approve the plan it meant
            we needed to hire the person first, but what he just heard Ms.
            Whitley say is that we can find the place and lease it. He asked
                                                                     Page 9 of 11
           Ms. Hyatt if that is what the Committee intended. Ms. Hyatt said
           as long as Ms. Whitley’s motion includes that the Town can still
           search for the location, she is in agreement.

           Ms. Whitley amended her motion to state that she moved to
           approve the Culture, Arts and Pride Commission’s
           recommendation for a town community arts center, but in the
           interim as we work toward releasing the RFP, staff be tasked
           with actively seeking a location. Mr. Iacofano amended his
           second.

               After further discussion, Mayor Haynie called for the vote
           and all present voted in favor.
