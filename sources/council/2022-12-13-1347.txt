A.      First Reading: An Ordinance to enact Section 43.02 titled
     Memorials and Monuments Policy of the Mount Pleasant Code of
     Ordinances. (Ord. No. 22065)
     December 5, 2022 Police, Judicial & Legal Committee Minutes
         Mayor Haynie moved for first reading, seconded by Mr.
     Ritchie.
     Mr. Ritchie stated he would like this to go back to the Police,
     Judicial and Legal Committee to discuss the three memorials that
     have been actively in the planning and fundraising process and
     determine what we can do to get these projects completed.
     Mr. Chapman said he is very respectful of Patriots Point, and they
     have information and exhibits for World War II and Vietnam. He
     said this is where we are honoring the wars of the United States,
     and they currently have nothing for 9/11 which was a war fought
     in the United States for 20 years. He said Patriots Point needs to
     do something to recognize 9/11 and this would be the perfect
     way. Mr. Chapman said he thinks they can do something faster
     there and recognize the national impact.
     Mr. Santos said people should not have to pay money to see a
     memorial. He asked Mr. Pagliarini to give a brief overview of the
     ordinance.
     Mr. Pagliarini said currently the Town does not have a process for
     making decisions related to monuments and memorials. He said
     Staff and Legal have a definitive interest in guidance as well as
     making the process open to the public for comment, which we do
not have currently. He said the process splits them into large and
small memorials. Small memorials are under $1,000 and would
be things such as park benches, with staff review for
appropriateness and approval. For large memorials there are
criteria which include demonstration of a high level of
significance, that the group, item, or issue has made significant
contribution to cultural, political, or social development of the
Town, the State of South Carolina or the United States of
America as well as internationally. He said the event is
historically or culturally significant and/or represents an important
and unique town or civic anniversary, and whether the
person/event/place is already memorialized elsewhere in the
Town. He said there are design and content criteria, which we
currently do not have the capacity to review in detail. He said
there is a trailing ordinance further on in the agenda which
expands the authority of the Historical Commission to review
these types of issues for content, and the Design Review Board
for design. He said the other two issues are how the cost is
being borne and how the maintenance is being handled moving
forward. Another aspect would be the appropriateness of the
location. Mr. Pagliarini said the applicable Boards and
Commissions would review for these items and it would be
brought to Council in a public manner for comment.
Mayor Haynie asked Mr. Pagliarini to review what was added to
the ordinance should future Councils or residents wish to remove
monuments or memorials.
Mr. Pagliarini said that there is a provision which requires a
significant level of due diligence and cause, also be supported by
various Boards and Commissions. It would require a super-
majority of Council, a two-thirds vote, to remove the same. Mr.
Pagliarini said we are also subject to the state statute which
addresses removal of public memorials and it is fairly restrictive
as well. There is also a provision to address temporary or pop-up
type monuments, which if placed in a public right of way or is
causing danger to the public, they can be remove with notice, and
the memorial would not be destroyed.
         Mr. Santos said that sounds good but the three that have been
         worked on for the past year need to be grandfathered in.
         Mr. Iacofano said he appreciates and understands the need for
         this ordinance and he thinks it is important that it goes back to the
         Police, Legal and Judicial Committee before final reading. He
         said three groups are raising money and obtaining a piece of
         history and he does not think they should be required to pay to
         see their own memorial, and they should be grandfathered.
         Ms. Whitley said these three should be grandfathered, but she
         thinks the ordinance is necessary to establish guidelines and a
         process.
             Mayor Haynie called for the vote. Mr. Santos voted no and all
         others present voted in favor. The motion carried with a 7-1 vote
         (Mr. Rambo was absent).
