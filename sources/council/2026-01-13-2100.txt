4. Public Hearings, Awards, & Presentations
a. Presentation of the Cresco Award
Kate Miller presented the Cresco Historical Stewardship Award to
Lynette White, founder of the Plantation Singers. The award recognized
Ms. White's dedication to the preservation, education, and celebration of
Gullah Geechee culture. Ms. Miller highlighted that Ms. White formed the
Plantation Singers in June 1996 to educate the public about Gullah
Geechee heritage. The group performs locally, including at the town's
annual Black History Month celebration, and has performed
internationally. Ms. Miller also noted that in September 2022, Ms. White
launched her own radio show, "Gullah Voices," to share stories and
preserve the region's unique heritage.

6. Public Comment
David Spedden expressed concern about the lack of updates regarding
Highway 41. He mentioned attending a public meeting where county
representatives claimed they had reached out to set up a meeting with
town officials. He noted that the county project team had committed to
providing a detailed update by January 15th. He encouraged Council to
make this issue a priority, suggesting it could influence how Mount
Pleasant residents vote on the upcoming sales tax referendum.
Richard Habersham raised concerns about properties being annexed
from settlement communities into the town. He stated that notification of
annexations was insufficient, not giving community members enough
time to respond. He emphasized that property owners buying in their

community should respect existing county zoning rather than seeking
town annexation to circumvent county regulations. He also asked
whether properties annexed from the Phillips community, which is on the
National Register, would receive the same historic protections under
town governance as they currently have under the county.
Fred Smalls, a resident of the Phillips community, spoke against the
proposal to annex property from the Phillips community into Mount
Pleasant. He noted that the property in question is located at 1651
Highway 41, directly across from his parents' house and in the center of
their community. He expressed concern that African American
communities are disappearing due to annexation, overdevelopment, and
transportation projects, citing the example of what happened to the
community along Highway 17.
Mike VanHorn raised safety concerns along the Highway 17 corridor,
particularly around the Towne Centre area between Venning and Isle of
Palms Connector. He noted recent accidents, including a fatality, and
requested traffic calming measures or crossing lights for residents in the
Montclair area who walk to Towne Centre.
Myra Snipe Richardson, asked Council to stop annexing properties
from Gullah Geechee communities. She specifically objected to the
town's ordinance 51.092, which she said forces residents to annex if
they want sewer service. She requested that if areas are annexed, they
should receive the same historic district protections as the Old Village.
She invited Council and staff to attend a Charleston County meeting on
January 20, 2026, regarding historic preservation.

annexing properties from historic communities, noting that areas like
Brickyard, Seaside, and the Old Village have protective overlays that

denying annexation would not prevent development. He explained that if
annexation is denied, Mount Pleasant Water Works would still have to
provide water and sewer by law, allowing development to proceed

these communities should have protection similar to HOA
neighborhoods, pointing out that not all neighborhoods have HOAs and
not all property owners want to be included in historic overlays.

work to draft and adopt a zoning overlay, and that Hamlin Beach would
be the next community to develop such protections, followed by Phillips.
She noted that town staff are working to determine how the town could
adopt the same historic overlay and whether that might involve creating
a joint commission.
After further discussion about the timing of potential historic overlay
protections, the Mayor indicated he would vote with the Planning
Committee's recommendation to deny the annexation.

Mayor Haynie explained that this item concerned potential projects for
inclusion in a $4-5 billion Charleston County sales tax referendum. He
noted that the Transportation Committee had developed priorities but
suggested that the full Council defer detailed prioritization until their
upcoming retreat. He emphasized that the referendum would fund not
only transportation projects but also resiliency infrastructure, drainage,
and possibly undergrounding power lines and green space acquisition.
Council Member Chapman explained that county officials had asked the
town to submit preliminary projects to help their consultants begin work.
He suggested that the town could submit the projects previously
approved by Council two years ago, with certain modifications, while
making clear that this was not a final or complete list. For example, he
noted that the previous description of improvements at Highway 17 and
Houston Northcutt Boulevard included an overpass option that is no
longer preferred.
Several Council members expressed differing views about specific
projects:
Council Member Iacofano argued in favor of keeping an overpass option
for the Houston Northcutt intersection, noting that the bridge ends at a
stoplight only 600 yards away, creating significant congestion.
Council Member Brownstein agreed that both options (overpass or at-
grade intersection) should remain under consideration for Houston
                                                           Town Council Meeting
                                                               January 13, 2026
                                                                  Page 28 of 33
Northcutt, citing data that an overpass would improve traffic flow by 30-
40% compared to only 19% for an at-grade solution. He also suggested
moving the Darrell Creek Trail realignment higher on the priority list as it
would benefit Carolina Park residents whose support would be crucial
for passing the referendum. He stressed the importance of county
officials addressing Highway 41 concerns to ensure Mount Pleasant's
support for the referendum.
Mayor Haynie emphasized that while Highway 41 concerns are
important, they are separate from the need to secure Mount Pleasant's
fair share of the referendum funding. He noted that he and other Council
members have made it clear to county officials that satisfactory
resolution of Highway 41 issues is necessary to gain Mount Pleasant's
support for the referendum.
After further discussion, Council reached consensus that staff would
share the working draft list with county officials, making clear that it was
preliminary and that Council would establish final priorities at their
January 27th retreat.
