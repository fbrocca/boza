VI.   PUBLIC COMMENT
           Nathan Bailey - 320 Seaport Way, stated he is the Field
      Representative for Congresswoman Nancy Maces Office. He
      stated Service Academy nominations are open from now until the
      end of October. Also, there is a Congressional App Challenge
      which was just launched where middle and high school students
      can work to create an app for iOS or Android which will be judged
      and the winner along with their family will get a free trip to
      Washington DC to promote the app. He also wanted to make
      everyone aware of their constituent services for IRS, passport or
      social security issues. He said they are also available for letters of
      support for grant applications.
         Lee Gastley - Seamon and Whitesides, stated he is available
      should anyone have any questions on Item VIII.A.3 under Planning
      New Business.
          Bill Ennis - President, Lowcountry Tennis Association (LCTA),
      stated there has been explosive growth of registered LCTA tennis
      players and the Recreation Department is at capacity. He said in
      2023 there will be more than double the players as in 2018, and in
      that time no tennis courts have been added. We are in desperate
      need of more courts. He also expressed appreciation for the hard
      work of Hailey at the tennis center.
          George Freeman – 1450 Bowman Road, stated he worked on
      the Settlement Communities Task Force and as a member he is
      here to make a request that Council make a decision on the
      recommendations of the Task Force.
          Ricky Waters – 880 Island Park Drive, stated he is with
      Bowman Consulting and is speaking about item VIII.A.2, approval
      of Impact Assessment and Conceptual Plan for 3236 North
      Highway 17, specifically the conditions put forward by the Planning
      Commission and Planning Committee. He said one of the
      conditions was adhering to the 100 buffer requirement on Highway
      17. He said there are other properties on Highway 17 with a 50
buffer, and they are requesting a 50 reduction for this project. Mr.
Waters said there were noise concerns about Pet Paradise. He said
of their 47 locations in the country there have been no formal noise
complaints. They are well managed and operated and they work
hard to be a good neighbor to their surrounding properties. Mr.
Waters said the last condition was regarding All American
Boulevard, and he asked Council to reconsider the
recommendation that a portion of the road be built as part of the
project as it is a planned and fully or partially funded project. He
does not think the recommendation was justified for this reason.
      Tommy Dinkins – 1612 Florentia Street stated she was
speaking on behalf of a majority of the homeowners regarding
Gather Mount Pleasant. She said there is a pending appeal with
the Board of Zoning Appeals, and at the first hearing it was stated
that a covenant agreement would be required by the developer and
their HOA in order to move forward with the final approval. Ms.
Dinkins asked that the Town not approve or enter into a covenant
agreement between the townhomes and Gather Mount Pleasant.
She said this would prevent homeowners from parking in front of
their own homes during Gathers normal business hours which they
believe to be six days a week, 8:00 a.m. to 10:00 p.m. She said
during off-hours the parking is at fifty percent capacity and during
normal hours there are no spaces. Ms. Dinkins said the second
part of their request is with respect to a pending zoning amendment
that Mack Cross submitted, and they understand he is requesting to
add another parcel to the Planned Development agreement, and if
that happens and he has sufficient land for his parking, the hope is
that the covenant agreement will not be needed. They are asking
for the amendment to be approved.
Perry Rourke – 2004 Brook Kiln Parkway, stated he would like to
thank the Health, Recreation and Events Committee and Council
for approving the Recreation Advisory Commission. He also asks
that Council support the referendum to be placed on the ballot in
November.
Mack Cross - 1496 Goblet Avenue stated he is the applicant for
the Planned Development amendment for the Midtown project, as
well as the developer of Gather Mount Pleasant. He asked that
       Council consider not prohibiting access to Hungryneck. He said
       they are proposing a right in, right out on Hungryneck. He said he
       is looking forward to continuing to work with the townhome owners
       and Ms. Dinkins on finding acceptable solutions to some of their
       concerns, especially parking.

2.      Final Reading: An Ordinance to approve a referendum
     regarding a proposed tax millage increase to construct and
     maintain recreation facilities. (Ord. No. 22045)
         Ms. Whitley moved for final reading, seconded by Mr.
     Iacofano.
     Mr. Rambo stated the Health, Recreation and Events
     Committee voted 3-0 to recommend amending the ordinance
     to expand the Mugsy Kerr Tennis complex, amend the Park
     West Pool renovation project to remove the multipurpose
     room and reduce some costs, and from the Rifle Range Road
     Park project the boardwalks and fishing piers would be
     removed to reduce costs in order to apply that money to the
     tennis complex.
         Mr. Rambo moved to amend the referendum ordinance as
     recommended by the Health, Recreation and Events
     Committee, seconded by Mr. Iacofano.
     Mr. DeMoura reviewed the specifics of the recommendations
     and after further discussion, Mayor Haynie called for the vote
     on the amendment.
         Mayor Haynie opposed with all others present in favor of
     the amendment. The amendment passes with a vote of 8-1.
          Ms. Corley moved to amend the ordinance to include a
     third option of a reduced plan with two grass fields, parking
     and no lights, which would reduce the impact on the
     environment and wildlife which currently inhabits the space at
     Rifle Range Park.
Mayor Haynie said this would amend what could be done at
Rifle Range Park which would make it the only option as the
referendum is a yes or no vote on the whole referendum. Ms.
Corley asked if an option could be added.
Mr. Pagliarini said the referendum is a yes or no choice and
he is not aware of any way to include options.
Ms. Corley said we are going to appoint a commission to look
at this and we need to give them time to give Council
feedback. She said looking at the financial landscape of the
nation currently, she cannot see supporting this.
Ms. Corleys motion fails for lack of a second.
Mr. Chapman said at the last Council meeting he mentioned
that 49% of the voters are going to be 50 or older, and that is
who will be voting on this referendum.
    Mr. Chapman moved to amend the referendum ordinance
to include a senior center for the northern part of town.
The motion failed for lack of a second.
    After further discussion, Mayor Haynie called for the vote
on the ordinance as amended. Ms. Corley and Mayor Haynie
voted opposed with Mr. Chapman, Ms. Hyatt, Mr. Santos, Mr.
Rambo, Mr. Iacofano, Ms. Whitley and Mr. Ritchie voting in
favor. The motion carried with a 7-2 vote.
