this property (1240 Six Mile Road), as it is now.

    Richard Habersham, 2938 Bennett Charles Road, stated that he

     Marcus McDonald, 63-B Ash Street, stated that he is here in
support of the Six Mile Community and the Sweetgrass Basket Overlay
District. He said black lives and black culture matters and black houses
matter as well as their environment. He said between 1910 and 1997,

      Chris Donato, 1250 Fairmont Avenue, stated that he is the
engineering representative for the Watts family and is here for
annexation and zoning. He added that this is an existing development.
They would like to join the Town, pay their taxes, and put 30% of their
site as open space, because currently, there is zero. They would like
to pay the impact fees for the homes they build, remove the septic
tanks that are currently servicing the thirty-one properties, add gravity
sewer, pay MPW impact fees, and beautify that section of Six Mile
roadway. They would like to do this and not increase the lot count and
only maintain what is there now. He believes it is an incredible
opportunity for the Town. The requested R-2 zoning has a 30% open
space requirement; however, the Sweetgrass Basket Overlay only has
a 20% open space requirement.
      George Freeman, Director of Community Activities for CAGE
(Community Action Group for Encouragement). Before 1994, spot
zoning was out of control in the state and the county. He said during
that time, the Town placed a dump in the Six Mile Community and the
county approved three trailer parks. He said the dump still exists, but
two of the trailer parks are gone and replaced with appropriate zoning.
He said the third trailer park will be gone soon; however, the owner is
requesting a non-conforming R-2 spot zoning. He said the residents of
Six Mile Community and the residents of the Town of Mount Pleasant
via the comprehensive plan agree that Community Conservation is the
appropriate zoning for this location. He asked Council to respect the
will of the people and deny this zoning request. Community
Conservation zoning protects the community and the trees.
     Linda Balzac, 422 Hobcaw Drive, stated that she is present
