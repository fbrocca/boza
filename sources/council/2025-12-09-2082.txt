David Pagliarini, Legal Counsel, introduced the public hearing. He
clarified that this concerned a private activity bond used for capital
expenditures for their school building, not operations.
Mr. Pagliarini emphasized that the Town was not the bond issuer, would
not be liable for any bond, was not a beneficiary, and that the bond
would not constitute a debt of the Town. He explained that federal law

Lathan Carey, Head of School at Crown Leadership Academy,
addressed the Council. He described how he and his wife Sarah moved
to Mount Pleasant in 2001, started a preschool, and eventually founded
Crown Leadership Academy in 2010. The school had been leasing
space from Trident Academy on Whipple Road for ten years and was
now purchasing a building at 546 Long Point Road. Mr. Carey explained
they were financing through tax-exempt private bonds with a maximum
amount of $20 million, though they were currently seeking about $9.8
million.
Council Member Brownstein asked about the bond amount and
confirmed it would be used to upfit the building at 546 Long Point Road
and improve the property.
Mayor Haynie noted he had attended the ribbon cutting and was
impressed with the property. He mentioned that the federal requirement
for local approval dated back to the TEFRA Act from the Reagan
administration, suggesting it existed to ensure the community could
vouch for legitimate operations.

6. Public Comment
Wendy Lockhart from Chandler Road in the 10 Mile Historic District
spoke about zoning designation for properties in the 10 Mile community
recently annexed to Mount Pleasant. She requested that zoning of
annexed properties align with the 10 Mile Overlay District being finalized
by Charleston County Council. She noted that if high-density zoning
were granted for annexed land, it would circumvent existing density
requirements and establish a dangerous precedent. Ms. Lockhart
described that 31 acres of available property on Chandler Road alone
could potentially result in 93 new homes if allowed to bypass the current
density requirements.
Barbara Smith from Royal Avenue expressed concern about proposed
changes to the old part of Mount Pleasant mentioned in agenda item
8B(3). She stated she had not been aware of these changes until
recently seeing information on Nextdoor. Ms. Smith worried about the
impact on property rights and values, particularly for long-time residents,
and requested that the vote be postponed until homeowners could be
better informed.
Dave Spedden from Toomer Kiln Circle spoke about Highway 41,
indicating he had met with the county project team but found conflicting
information in their documentation. He offered to help broker a
presentation or work session between the Town and Charleston County,
expressing concern that without dialogue, the situation might become
litigious.
Angela Singleton from Tim Ascue Lane, representing the 10 Mile
Neighborhood Association, spoke against the Chandler Road parcel
annexation. She expressed concern that this annexation would have a
lasting impact that could undo years of work by the community. Ms.
Singleton asked the Council to work collaboratively with the community,
Charleston County, and Mount Pleasant Water Works to create a fair fee
structure rather than dismantling historic communities.

Encouragement (CAGE), addressed Highway 41 concerns. He

Michele Reed, Director of Planning, Land Use, and Neighborhoods,
explained that this amendment would close a gap in the zoning code by
specifying that properties outside the urban growth boundary that are
annexed would default to rural conservation zoning (one unit per three

(1) First Reading: An Ordinance to amend Section 151.004, entitled
Extensions of Vesting Periods and Limitations Thereto, of the
Mount Pleasant Code of Ordinances. Ord. No. 25055

David Pagliarini explained that this amendment would allow the Planning
Commission to extend vested rights that were originally granted by the
Town Council. He noted that under current rules, the extension of rights
must be approved by the same body that granted them, requiring
Council approval for extensions of rights Council had originally granted.
The amendment would allow Planning Commission to handle these
extensions, with Council retaining the ability to call issues back if
needed.
                                                         Town Council Meeting
                                                            December 9, 2025
                                                               Page 21 of 29
Mr. Pagliarini emphasized that state law largely requires these
extensions to be granted unless there is a specific reason to deny them,
and that denials without cause could expose the town to legal liability.
Council members expressed differing views on whether this
responsibility should be delegated. Some felt the Planning Commission
was well-qualified to handle these extensions as part of their statutory
duties, while others preferred that Council maintain this responsibility to
stay informed about ongoing projects.
Motion by Council Member Jake Rambo to approve was seconded by
Council Member Tinkey. The motion passed 5-4, with Council Members
Chapman, Crosby, Rambo, Tinkey and Mayor Haynie voting in favor,
and Council Members Brownstein, Hyatt, Iacofano and Whitaker voting
opposed.
