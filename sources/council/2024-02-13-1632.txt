IV.       COMMENTS BY CHARLESTON COUNTY COUNCIL CHAIR
       HERBERT SASS
       Chairman Sass, 413 Hobcaw Drive, stated he would like to thank
       Council for the opportunity to share some good news about roads,
       bike and pedestrian access, intersection improvements, resurfacing
       roads, greenbelts, conservation, and public transportation. He said
       County Council is putting together a list of projects all around the
       County for the proposed third transportation sales tax program.
       Chairman Sass stated this would not be an additional tax but will
       replace the first half-cent sales tax which will have raised $1.3 Billion
over its 23-year term, and as promised, it will end early because the
goal was reached. He said the first half-cent sales tax funded $75
Million for the Arthur Ravenel bridge, $80 Million for the Johnnie
Dodds Boulevard corridor improvements from the bridge to 526, the
Ben Sawyer pedestrian improvements from Mount Pleasant to
Sullivans Island, land on Rifle Range Road for a park, various
intersection improvements, sidewalks and resurfacing all around
Mount Pleasant. Chairman Sass said the second half-cent sales tax
is funding the ongoing Highway 41 and 17 improvements,
preservation of Boone Hall, and other various intersections and
bike/pedestrian improvements, including a sidewalk in Cooper
Estates. In addition, County and Town Transportation staff identified
much needed interim improvements for Highway 41 at Joe Rouse
Road and Highway 17, which have just been funded and awarded,
which will make traffic flow better on Highway 41. He said Town staff,
along with the County have identified five projects in Mount Pleasant
for consideration in the third half-cent sales tax. Chairman Sass
stated this includes Johnnie Dodds corridor improvements with a
flyover, Mount Pleasant Way bike/pedestrian improvements, Long
Point Road corridor improvements, Darryl Creek Trail realignment
and Chuck Dawley/Coleman Boulevard/ Ben Sawyer intersection
improvements. He said studies are being conducted as to which
projects are most needed in all areas of the County, including Mount
Pleasant, West Ashley, North Charleston, James Island, Johns Island
and Charleston, including the extension of the Mark Clark
Expressway. He said over $400 Million have been included for
greenbelts and conservation in this sales tax. He said that is twice as
much as the first two sales tax programs combined and is a real
opportunity to preserve more open space from future development,
especially if conservation easements are utilized. Chairman Sass
said like the first two sales tax programs, there are components for
public transportation including the bus rapid transit. He said a year or
two ago, there was talk of the South Carolina Department of Motor
Vehicles (SCDMV) moving to Clements Ferry Road. The County and
SCDMV are working to get approvals for a permanent SCDMV office
to be constructed at the service center at the corner of the Isle of
Palms Connector. He said planning and approvals are ongoing to
complete the ground lease to SCDMV and they will build their own
building. Chairman Sass said lastly, the new sidewalks on Mathis

VII.         PUBLIC COMMENT
            Carl Mittelstadt, 2759 Gaston Gate stated he is requesting that
       the Brickyard Parkway sidewalk project be canceled as it will not have
       any significant impact on the majority of the stated program goals. He
       stated it will not increase the Walk Score for any home in Brickyard and
       is not a wise use of limited Town resources. He said he is speaking
       on behalf of 272 Brickyard residents that have signed a petition in
       opposition to the sidewalk installation. He stated they have not finished
       collecting signatures and have not yet gone door to door.



                                                                       Page 3 of 9
       Mike Hughes, 1045 Wharf Indigo Place stated he and his
neighbors are here this evening to request that the Town take over
their small private road in the Creekside neighborhood and make it
public. He said they believe the town’s intervention can provide
resolution in ensuring neighborhood interconnectivity continues. Mr.
Hughes stated their request is tied to the liability associated with traffic
on the non-private road from non-homeowners as it serves as a vital
cut-through connecting multiple neighborhoods including Creekside,
Cooper Estates, The Groves, Bay Tree, and Pine Hollow. The traffic
includes walkers, bikers, and golf carts, providing a convenient route
for residents going to and from the Creekside pool and tennis club,
access to Shem Creek and children biking to and from Moultrie Middle
School. What makes this situation distinct is the presence of an
easement and bridge installed around 2012 and owned by the Town,
rated for light vehicles, that connects the private road to the public road
system.
      George Green, 1039 Wharf Indigo Place stated the residents of
Wharf Indigo are in favor of the increased interconnectivity that is
happening as a result of the bridge, but the potential liability associated
with non-residents utilizing the private road is forcing Wharf Indigo
residents to consider shutting off public access by installing key fob
activated gates that would only be able to be used by the Wharf Indigo
residents and their guests. He stated they do not want to do this, and
the only viable path forward for keeping this neighborhood
interconnectivity would be for the Town to take over the road and make
it a public road. Mr. Green said they are willing to donate the road, as
is, to the Town and are willing to work with the Town to make this
happen and increase interconnectivity between the surrounding
neighborhoods. Mr. Green stated he is formally requesting this issue
be taken to the Town Transportation Committee for further action and
thanked the Mayor and Council for their time. He said there were
visuals that were provided to Mayor and Council that showed the issue.
     Steve Kendrick, 1052 Wharf Indigo Place reviewed the visuals
that were provided to the Mayor and Council. Mr. Kendrick said in
closing they would like to reiterate the formal request to have this
added to the Transportation Committee for further action and to accept
the road into the Town, as is. He said it is their hope that
interconnectivity can remain open to the surrounding neighborhoods.
He thanked the Mayor and Council for their time and consideration.
