The following individuals addressed the Council during public comment:
C'Anne Wolf addressed the Council regarding comments made by
Mayor Pro Tem Howard Chapman at the July 21, 2026 Charleston
County Council meeting. She stated that his characterization of

against Bessemer Road and Phillips community residents was a false

Ben Brown expressed gratitude to the Council for their engagement on
the Gregory Ferry Landing affordable housing amendment and
encouraged the Council to view workforce housing policy as a living,

pending AMI amendment and noted the importance of welcoming
families into the community.
Mike Van Horn expressed disappointment with the Council's decision to
grant municipal consent to Charleston County regarding the proposed
road through Laurel Hill County Park, arguing the road would not resolve
long-term traffic issues.
Curtis Peterson provided an overview of VFW Post 106's community
service activities and urged support for veterans amid federal funding
concerns.
Andrew Johnston, a Gregory Ferry Townes resident, spoke in support of
raising the AMI ceiling to 120 percent, noting the difficulty of qualifying at
80 percent AMI given current interest rates and housing costs.
Dimitry O’Sullivan expressed support for the Mount Pleasant Police
Department's response to street takeover activities and urged vigilance
given the organized nature of such events elsewhere in the country.

  (1) Final Reading: An Ordinance providing for an amendment to the
      Gregorie Ferry Landing PD-MU-SU (Ordinance 06082, as
      amended), specifically pertaining to the Attainable Housing
      Development’s definition of Affordable Units. (Ord. No. 26036)
      (Ord. No. 26036 FINAL)
                                                          Town Council Meeting
                                                               August 11, 2026
                                                                 Page 23 of 27




Director Reed noted that this item had received first reading the prior
month and was subsequently returned to the Planning Committee for
further discussion. The amendment pertained to the definition of
Affordable Units within the Attainable Housing Development.
    Council Member Daniel Brownstein moved to amend the ordinance
    to cap the total area median income at 120 percent, which was
    seconded by Council Member Iacofano.
Council Member Kathryn Whitaker stated her opposition, citing that the
development opened in 2020 with an intended AMI of 80 percent or
below, that a unit sold in the current calendar year for $367,500, and that
nontraditional financing programs such as South Carolina Housing and
Fannie Mae Home Ready exist to assist sub-80 percent AMI buyers.
She expressed concern that raising the income ceiling shifts the target
population from low-income to middle-income households and does not
address the town's broader need to diversify housing price points.
Council Member John Iacofano expressed support, noting that the
workforce housing AMI band of 80 to 120 percent is specifically intended
to serve teachers, firefighters, police officers, health care workers, and
hospitality employees. He further argued that the underlying formula is
broken because it does not account for rising interest rates, which
effectively disqualify 80 percent AMI buyers from obtaining conventional
mortgages.
Council Member Brownstein agreed with Council Member Iacofano's
interest rate argument and added that even a Charleston County teacher
                                                         Town Council Meeting
                                                              August 11, 2026
                                                                Page 24 of 27

earns above the 80 percent AMI threshold, and that a married couple
would find it exceptionally difficult to qualify. He stated that the
amendment maintains, rather than shifts, the original intent of the
development.
Council Member Alex Crosby supported the 120 percent figure, noting
that the original concept was intended to provide a pathway to
homeownership and equity, not a permanent residence, and that current
homeowners must be able to sell in order to give the next family the
same opportunity.
   Mayor Haynie called for the vote and Council Member Whitaker
   voted opposed with all others present voting in favor. The motion
   carried 8-1.
  This Ordinance SIGNED, SEALED and DELIVERED, this date.
  (2) Final Reading: An Ordinance to provide for a temporary
      moratorium on the acceptance and processing of major
      subdivision applications in Charleston County Historic Districts.
      (Ord.26039)
                                                          Town Council Meeting
                                                               August 11, 2026
                                                                 Page 25 of 27




    Council Member Rambo moved for final reading, seconded by
    Council Member Tinkey.
Director Reed reported that Charleston County was scheduled to hold its
third and final reading of a parallel moratorium ordinance on August 21,
2026. At second reading, the county had voted to include the Ten Mile
community for one year, while the other historic districts would be
subject to a two-year moratorium.
Council Member Brownstein raised concern that a misalignment
between the town's moratorium and the county's final ordinance could
produce a jurisdictional shopping problem, where applicants choose
whichever government's rules are more permissive. Mayor Pro Tem
Chapman recommended deferring the item until the county's final
reading was complete, so that the town could align its action with the
county's outcome.
Council Member Tinkey expressed a preference for approving the
ordinance as written in order to send a strong message to the county
ahead of their third reading, and noted that the Ten Mile community had
been diligent in completing its character appraisal and historic
designation process. Legal Counsel Pagliarini confirmed that because
the pending ordinance doctrine was written into the record at first
reading, a temporary subdivision moratorium on the historic districts
remains in effect regardless of deferral.
In light of the pending ordinance doctrine remaining active, the motion to
approve was withdrawn.
