C. Solar Eclipse Presentation
  Professor Laura Penny, College of Charleston stated that on August
  21st, there will be a total solar eclipse, where total darkness will
  cross over Mount Pleasant and other regions of the low country.
  She stated that this eclipse is due to the moon’s shadow as it falls
  on the earth. She stated that there is the dark portion of the moon
  shadow which is the umber which is very, very small. She stated
  that there is a bigger region, called the head umbra, which is much
  bigger. She stated that only individuals who were in the very small
  dark shadow will see the entire sun covered up. She stated that a
  lot of people think that they have seen a total solar eclipse, but
  unless you were in Charleston in 1970 or in Washington State or

                                                            Page 10 of 80
  Oregon in 1979, or somewhere else around the world, you did not.
  She said you would have only seen part of the sun covered, but not
  the whole sun covered, which is an entirely different experience.
  She stated that safety is a big issue, because if you attempt to look
  at the sun, it is painful and damaging, but once it is covered up
  partially, you are able to look at it without pain, but still damaging.
  She stated that the College of Charleston is giving out some
  protective glasses at their locations. She stated that public libraries
  and some businesses are also giving them out. She stated that the
  glasses should be marked with “safe for direct solar viewing” or CE
  or ISO. She stated that you can also do the old fashioned pinhole
  viewing. She stated that a solar eclipse can happen once a year, but
  traditionally they do not fall over well populated areas. She stated
  that the last total solar eclipse was in 1970. This particular total
  solar eclipse is going to go all the way across the United States from
  Oregon, across the Rockies, down through Nashville, Columbia and
  the Lowcountry. She stated that it is going to pass over major
  metropolitan areas. She stated that it is projected that the area will
  see millions of visitors coming into South Carolina to watch the
  total solar eclipse that day. She stated that Forbes magazine stated
  that this could potentially be the largest traffic jam in American
  history. She stated that this will be national news and all hotels are
  completely sold out for the night of August 20th. She stated that the
  sun will start to become covered up at approximately 1:15 pm and
  the totality phase will begin at approximately 2:45 pm to 2:48 pm
  and then it will totally be uncovered at approximately 4:10 pm. She
  stated that there is no school that day. She stated there will be a
  teams at Park West, Palmetto Island County Park, behind
  Whiteside’s Elementary and possibly Alhambra Hall, where people
  can come, get information and talk to astronomers.

Clay Duffie, Mount Pleasant Waterworks, stated that he would like to
provide a brief update on the water quality situation in the Town. He
stated that this all began with some unfortunate situations with
families located in the Highway 41 area, where family members were
diagnosed with brain tumors. He stated that there were 7 boys and 4
girls that had developed brain tumors in this area and there was some
concern as to why this may have happened, as well as if this was what
was referred to as a “cluster”. However, DHEC (Department of
Environmental Control), did review this situation and stated that it did
not meet the CDC (Center for Disease Control) criteria to declare this a
cluster. He stated that MPW was in touch with DHEC and the parents
of those children diagnosed with brain tumors and DHEC has agreed to
further investigate whether the 11 cases have commonalities. He
stated that they will be bringing in health and environmental officials
to review these cases. He stated that people began using test kits to
test their water and thought that there may have been pesticides in
the water. He stated that they were using test kits that clearly state
not to use to certify whether water is safe or not safe to drink. He
stated that they do not meet EPA or DHEC standards for testing. He
stated that only the tests MPW or DHEC conducts are certified. He
stated that MPW has had a long history of “zero” pesticides in the
water and this is what was coming up positive on the home test kits
which had residents very concerned. He said that there was a
community meeting yesterday with Charleston Water Systems and the
concerned parents. He stated that MPW has been coordinating with
Charleston Water Systems and DHEC and conducted sampling today in
Rivertowne, Dunes West and the Philips Community and DHEC will be
here again tomorrow sampling the feed points at Charleston Water
Systems and MPW reverse osmosis. He stated that MPW has ordered
five additional samples and Charleston Water Systems will be
conducting parallel samples with DHEC tomorrow at their plant and at
their feed points coming into Mount Pleasant. He stated that they do
not expect any pesticides to be found and meet all the Safe Drinking
Water Act Standards for safe drinking water. He stated that Mount
Pleasant’s employees are moms and dads just like everyone else and

  1. Consideration of a framework governing appointments to the

     Mr. Brimmer stated that this will be the steering group for the
     Comprehensive Planning process. He stated that this is
     mandated by statute to be completed every ten years. He stated
     that the Town is starting the process a year earlier. He said that
     as intended, the Comprehensive Plan should be resident driven;
     therefore, this requires assembling a steering group of residents
     here in the Town and may have other representations as well.
     He stated the Planning Committee reviewed the
     recommendations from the Planning Commission regarding the
     composition of Comprehensive Plan Planning Forum Group. The


                                                             Page 70 of 80
Committee concurred with the recommendations of the
Commission that the forum should consist of a diverse group of
residents with a composition reflective of the Mount Pleasant
Community. The Committee also discussed that the
appointment of members to the forum should not be a political
process. The Committee instead suggested an application
process, whereby residents could apply to serve on the forum. It
was further suggested that the Planning Commission and staff
would review the applications and select candidates who
represent and reflect a diversity of interests and communities in
Mount Pleasant, with final consideration by Town Council. He
stated that as a final note, the consultants of this project have
suggested that a group of 20 to 30 people typically works best
for this type of forum or committee, Town Council has been
provided with a staff memorandum that more thoroughly
describes the recommendations and discussions of the
Commission and the Committee, a draft application and a draft
advertisement that may be utilized to solicit applications. He
stated that Town Council now needs to provide specific
directions to staff to begin this process.
Mr. Brimmer recommended that Town Council direct staff to
move forward with promoting and advertising, advising that the
Town is now accepting applications for the forum, and that the
Planning Commission and staff will review and select
applications, as has been discussed, and that the forum should
consist of a group of 20 to 30 residents.
