  Lorianne Riggin, SCDNR, stated that she would like to provide the

  that they have been planning to protect and restore Crab Bank. She

  why DNR is involved and why they care. She said she will then turn

  Ms. Riggin stated that Crab Bank has been a symbol within the

She stated that the property is owned and managed by the
Department of Natural Resources. She stated that this property is
only one of five seabird sanctuaries along the coast.




She stated that these are animals that nest on the ground and many
harbor islands along South Carolina’s coast are important to these
birds because there is natural ebb and flow. She said these islands
are continually washed over and lose sand and grow by attributing
more sand from general wave action. She said this is great for the

ground nesters, because they are not large enough to grow a great
deal of vegetation which allows for predators to then come and
forage on the nestlings and on the eggs of these birds. She said prior
to Hurricane Irma in 2017, Crab Bank was a nesting sanctuary for
seabirds and shorebirds along the coast. She said Hurricane Irma
came through and washed away the remaining nesting high ground.
She said what is important about Crab Bank is that it provides a
predator free refuge primarily for seabirds and shorebirds
specifically, brown pelicans and various tern species. She said these
are all important species to DNR and the citizens of South Carolina
as what is called the State Wildlife Action Priority Species. These
species that are categorized here are either endemic to South
Carolina, are rare and declining, or they simply do not know much
about them. She said in the case of seabirds and shorebirds, they are
declining. She said to provide an illustration of how important Crab
Bank has been over the past 50 to 60 years, the following
photographs will illustrate Crab Bank over time.

Holly Carpenter, Army Corps of Engineers, stated that this is actually
a complex history at Crab Bank and how it was established.




                                                           Page 11 of 87
She stated that beginning in 2011, the Charleston Harbor Deepening
and Widening project also known as Post 45, the feasibility phase
was initiated with the execution of the feasibility cost-sharing
agreement with the South Carolina State Ports Authority. She said
that the Post 45 feasibility phase was developed in order to address
transportation inefficiencies in the Charleston Harbor. She said
following the initiation of the feasibility phase, there were times
when they welcomed public comment and input through that
process. She said there were several public meetings and they also
released draft documents for public review. She said one of the
many activities that were completed during this phase was the
identification of a placement area for the material to be dredged
from the harbor. She said the recommended plan from the feasibility
project was a seven-foot deepening of the inter-harbor areas from
45 to 52 feet which is the project currently under construction in the
entrance channel today. She said in 2013, they worked with their
stakeholder agencies to identify the least cost and environmentally
acceptable placement locations for the material from Charleston
Harbor. Crab Bank was identified as a preferred placement
opportunity by those resource agencies and was included in the draft
documentation for the Post 45 project. She said it was included in
the final integrated feasibility report and environmental impact
statement which was released for public comment in 2014 and
finalized in September of 2015. She said the report also included that
a beneficial use alternative for the Post 45 project would be
evaluated in more detail during the design phase of the project. The
design phase was initiated in December 2015 and in the summer of
2016 the Charleston District completed additional geotechnical
boring campaigns in the Charleston lower harbor. Through the
additional data collection, the district was able to verify that there
was enough quality material that is at least 65% sand content
material for placement on Crab Bank. She said this analysis was
documented in the Post 45 supplemental environmental
assessment, which was completed and released for public comment
in October 2016, in which many favorable comments were received.

                                                           Page 12 of 87
She said through an email campaign from the Audubon Society, they
received approximately 276 support emails as well as emails from
resource agencies supporting the project. That document completed
the environmental compliance requirements for the proposed
federal action. She said it was also determined at the same time that
Crab Bank was to be at an increased cost of the least cost
environmentally acceptable placement alternative for that portion
of the material from the lower harbor. She said the least cost
alternative for that portion of the material is placement in the ocean
dredge material disposal site which is located just offshore from
Charleston Harbor and designated by the EPA. She said in order for
the Army Corps to construct the project at Crab Bank as a beneficial
use, another non-federal sponsor must step in to pay the increased
cost or to cost share under a different authority in the construction
of the project. She said this is where the South Carolina Department
of Natural Resources stepped in and provided a request for the
Charleston District to evaluate beneficial use alternatives associated
with the Post 45 project under the Army Corps continuing authority’s
programs, section 204. The Army Corps continuing authority’s
program allows the Corps of Engineers to undertake small
construction projects without a specific authority from either the
study or the construction of that project. She said particularly in this
case, section 204 allows the Corps to look at the beneficial use of
dredge material for the purposes of ecosystem restoration or flood
risk management with federal participation at a 65% cost share to a
35% non-federal cost and up to a maximum cost of $10 million
dollars. In order for the federal government to participate in the cost
of construction for beneficial use alternatives including Crab Bank,
an additional feasibility study had to be conducted. That feasibility
study optimized different alternatives looking at all the different
uses for material from Charleston lower harbor and ultimately
recommended placement at Crab Bank. She stated that this study
was drafted in September 2017 and recently approved by the Army
Corps South Atlantic Division in May of 2018. The recommended
plan from that draft report is the placement of 660,000 cubic yards

                                                             Page 13 of 87
of dredged material from the Charleston lower harbor onto Crab
Bank, the purpose of which is to create additional brown nesting
pelican habitat. Approximately 28 acres of brown nesting pelican
habitat, which is the amount of ground approximately two feet
above mean-high water or a total of an 80-acre island in reference
to mean-well or low water, will be created from the construction of
this plan. She stated that there is still design and construction for this
plan. In order for the Corps of Engineers to move forward with the
design phase for the project, a project partnership agreement with
the South Carolina Department of Natural Resources and the Federal
Government must be executed. If they are able to successfully
execute a project partnership agreement, both entities have
committed to completing the design for the project and also
concurrently completing a numerical model to validate the proposed
plan from the feasibility study. She said at this time, neither the
Army Corps of Engineers or the South Carolina Department of
Natural Resources have secured the full funding for the construction
of the project, but they are currently at this time, looking at moving
into that design phase.
