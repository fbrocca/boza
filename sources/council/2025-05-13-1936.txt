7. Discussion and Possible Action to Create a Process Parallel to
   the County's Efforts to Preserve Historic Settlement
   Communities
Mayor Haynie clarified that the town had changed its annexation law to
allow residents with existing septic tanks to connect to available sewer
without annexing into the town, provided they have one residential
equivalent unit. He emphasized that this change was made to protect
the health of citizens and prevent overdevelopment in settlement
communities.
Planning Director Michele Reed presented information on the county's
efforts to preserve historic settlement communities. She explained that
three communities east of the Cooper (Phillips Community, Ten Mile
Community, and Hamlin Beach Community) have a designated historic
overlay in the county. The county has implemented a two-year
moratorium on subdivisions of four lots or greater within these historic
boundaries to allow for further study and tailoring of guidelines specific
to each community.
Council members discussed various aspects of the issue, including:
   ● The challenge of creating a historic overlay for properties not
     currently in the Town of Mount Pleasant
   ● The possibility of creating a parallel process to the county's efforts
   ● Concerns about the impact of annexation on property values and
     community character
   ● The need for balance and community input in any preservation
     efforts
Councilman Chapman moved to direct staff to bring to council
specifically what the county is doing, how they're doing it, who it affects,
and the legal responsibility they have accepted to do this. Councilman
Iacofano seconded the motion.
Councilman Brownstein proposed an amendment to the motion,
requesting that staff also provide a matching copy of the moratorium in
effect for the county, including maps and language, so the council could
see what it would look like for the town. Ms. Hyatt seconded the
amendment.
Mayor Haynie called for the vote and the amendment passed
unanimously.
Mayor Haynie called for a vote on the main motion, as amended, and it
passed unanimously.

    B. Planning Committee
       (1) Discussion and Possible Action Regarding Guidance to
           Establish a Tree Task Force
Council members discussed the formation of a tree task force, including
its composition, mission, and reporting structure. Councilman Tinkey
presented a draft mission statement and suggested potential categories
for task force members.
Councilwoman Whitley moved to direct staff to draft a vision statement
and parameters for a 7-member tree task force with a one-year term,
encouraging inclusion of members from the GREEN Commission,
Planning Commission, local business community, expert certified
arborists, and Board of Zoning Appeals. Councilman Chapman
seconded the motion.
Councilman Tinkey proposed an amendment to have the task force
report through the Flood Resilience Committee and have oversight
through the executive office. Councilwoman Hyatt seconded the
amendment.
Mayor Haynie called for a vote and the amendment passed by a vote of
8-1, with Councilman Brownstein voting opposed.
Mayor Haynie called for a vote on the main motion, as amended, and it
passed unanimously.
      (2) Discussion and Possible Action Regarding Heights of
          Detached Accessory Structures
Councilwoman Hyatt expressed concerns about the impact of increased
heights for accessory structures in older neighborhoods. Councilman
Chapman suggested adding language to ensure accessory structures
are not taller than the principal building in all cases.
Councilman Brownstein expressed concerns over making these
changes so quickly which may encourage tear downs and larger
buildings.
Councilman Chapman moved to have staff look at options for
addressing accessory structure heights and bring recommendations
back to the planning committee. Councilwoman Hyatt seconded the
motion.
The motion passed unanimously.
