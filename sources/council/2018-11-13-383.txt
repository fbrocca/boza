A. Building Permit Allocations
  First Reading: An Ordinance to implement a Building Permit Allocation
  System, Chapter 156 of the Town of Mount Pleasant Code of
  Ordinances. (Ord. No. 18097)
  Mr. Ulma stated that the Planning Committee has been focused on this
  topic and has looked at development trends and various growth
  management items at several meetings throughout this year.




  He said in August the Committee presented a proposal and requested
  that full Council authorize staff to begin preparing a draft ordinance. He
  said staff has utilized as a base the ordinance that the community was


                                                                  Page 8 of 64
familiar with and utilized in 2007. He said they built the current
ordinance from the previous one and believe they have simplified it and
from an administrative standpoint, made it more manageable for staff.
He said the Planning Committee reviewed the ordinance at their
September meeting and last week and recommended that it be
presented to Council tonight.




The main features of the program are that it is built on a five-year
approach to be able to handle the numbers and the information. Staff
uses annual allocations but distributes them twice a year. He said
whatever the number of available permits / allocations would be, there
would be a January 1st group for half of the issued permits and then
another on July 1st. He stated that it is designed to be first come, first
serve; therefore, on May 15th if someone applies and there are available
permits from the January 1st allocation, the permit would be available.
He said if it is June 20th and near the end of the first half of allocations
and the maximum for the first six-month period have been issued, their
application would roll over into the next allotment for the next 6
months.
He said another thing that was built in as part of any system, staff
believes that it is important that Council with staff, monitor and
evaluate the system to ensure it is operating and working the way


                                                                  Page 9 of 64
Council and the community would expect. He said there is also the
ability to adjust as necessary.




He said the ordinance as drafted uses a fixed number of permits rather
than a percentage growth rate. He said the way it is designed, it
excludes those residential developments covered by a legal
development agreement already in place, which would be Liberty Hill
Farms and Carolina Park and by way of reference, those two
developments would be estimated, based on the Carolina Park average
and the takedown schedule in the development for Liberty Hill Farms,
would generate approximately 220 residential permits per year. He said
the system is designed to allocate 600 additional residential units for
the rest of the community. He stated that based on his estimation that
there will be approximately 40,000 dwelling units by the end of the
year, this translates to 2.1% over the duration of the program and by
comparison, the past five years was 3.54% average growth rate in
building permit issuance.
He stated that there are two tables and only a few categories that were
created on the single-family category, which are the single family
detached units, single family attached, and accessory dwelling units
(ADU’s). He said by utilizing these numbers, the Town would end up
dealing with single family at 500 permits per year. He said in table 2,
100 multi-family per year would be added and over the five-year period,


                                                             Page 10 of 64
would be equivalent to 500 total multi-family units. He said this is how
the division of the allocation is presented in the ordinance.




He said there are some exemptions, recognizing that those residents
who remove a structure and desire to replace it with another, is not
having a negative impact generating additional impacts on the
community. Therefore, this would be over and above the set
allocations. He said they built in, as requested by the Planning
Committee, a Low-Income Affordable Housing project exemption,
which would be for low-income residential development projects
where the occupants would earn 80% or less of the area median income
within our region.




                                                             Page 11 of 64
Mr. Ulma stated that if the annual allotment is not reached, those
permits would be rolled over into subsequent half-year or entire year
allotments. He said if there are valid applications for permits that
exceed the number of allotments, they would be put into the queue and
held, working with the applicant to process that permit at the next
available opportunity, when there are available units that can be
permitted.




Mr. Ulma stated that all permit applications must be complete, and
construction must begin within six months, otherwise that approval will



                                                             Page 12 of 64
go back into the system. He said completely changing a single-family
home permit will not be allowed.




He stated that this ordinance is currently in front of Council for first
reading. As part of the Zoning Code, the Planning Commission will
review the ordinance and make a recommendation to Council and
conduct a Public Hearing. He said this may be accomplished at the
Planning Commission’s next regular meeting on December 19, 2018.
