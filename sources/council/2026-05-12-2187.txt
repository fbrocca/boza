Director of Planning, Land Use, and Neighborhoods Michele Reed
presented the proposed Ten Mile Community Overlay Zoning District. She
explained that the overlay is modeled closely on the Charleston County
zoning overlay, which was the product of approximately three years of
community engagement and was designed to implement standards
reflecting the area's historic district character.
Ms. Reed outlined the key elements of the county overlay that the town's
proposal mirrors, including standards for density, intensity and dimensional
requirements, fences, signage, pedestrian walkways, storage of vehicles,
street names, and stormwater requirements. She noted that differences
between the county and town versions are minimal, with most changes
attributable to references to county-specific ordinances that required
updating.
Ms. Reed highlighted several policy points that the committee deliberated
upon before arriving at its recommendations:
Regarding accessory dwelling units (ADUs), the county overlay does not
set a specific ADU size standard, relying instead on overall building
coverage limits. The town followed this approach, given that the 25 percent
building coverage limit — which encompasses all structures — effectively
constrains ADU size.
                                                             Town Council Meeting
                                                                    May 12, 2026
                                                                    Page 9 of 19


Regarding signage and street names, coordination with the Ten Mile
Neighborhood Association would be required.
Regarding affordable and workforce housing, the committee and
Planning Commission were firm in retaining the town's existing density
standards rather than adopting the county's density bonus provisions,
which can allow up to approximately 18 units per acre in some cases.
However, the overlay does permit duplexes, triplexes, and quadplexes as
workforce housing subject to a special exception from the Board of Zoning
Appeals, consistent with the county overlay.
Regarding stormwater, the county's special stormwater requirement was
replaced with a reference to the town's own stormwater standards, which
Ms. Reed described as equally or more strict.
Regarding the district boundary, Ms. Reed explained that because some
properties had been annexed into the town between the time the county
established its historic boundaries and the time it established its zoning
overlay, the town chose to base its boundary on the original historic district
boundary rather than the county's zoning overlay boundary. This resulted in
a few areas where the town's overlay differs, including several properties
pulled back into the town's overlay and one section excluded due to the
existence of an existing community mixed-use overlay.
Council Member Brownstein expressed support for the overlay, noting it
was a positive step toward ensuring uniform treatment of settlement
communities regardless of whether they fall within the town or the county.
He raised three questions:
He inquired whether manufactured and mobile homes would remain
permitted within the overlay. Ms. Reed confirmed they are permitted as a
use in the overlay's use table, and noted that anything not addressed by
the overlay defaults to existing town code.
He asked about the prohibition on community docks and boat ramps,
suggesting such amenities could benefit the community. Ms. Reed
acknowledged the question but stated that the rationale was not entirely
clear, noting that the prohibition had been carried over from the county
overlay following the county's own community engagement process, and
that the town chose to honor that determination.
He asked how the overlay would be enforced in the absence of a dedicated
historic district review board, referencing ongoing discussions about

confirmed that enforcement would be handled by the Planning Department

Member Hyatt. All present voted in favor.

               (1) Accommodations Tax Advisory Commission
                       Funding Recommendations

        9.1. Accommodations Tax Advisory Commission




Council Member Chapman moved to approve the Accommodations Tax
Advisory Committee funding recommendations, seconded by Council
Member Rambo.
Council Member Daniel Brownstein raised a concern regarding the
recommended funding of $5,000 for the Turkey Day Run, noting that the
race takes place in Charleston on Thanksgiving Day and, in his view,
primarily serves residents who are already present or visitors traveling for
other purposes, rather than generating new hotel stays in Mount Pleasant.
Council Member Brownstein moved to amend the primary motion to
remove the Turkey Day Run funding, seconded by Council Member
Rambo.
Council Member John Iacofano expressed agreement with the amendment.
He noted his prior service on the Accommodations Tax Advisory
Commission and stated that he did not see the Turkey Day Run as
generating vacation travel. He further inquired whether the Turkey Day Run
had received funding from the City of Charleston's accommodations tax.
                                                             Town Council Meeting
                                                                    May 12, 2026
                                                                   Page 15 of 19


Economic Development Manager Matt Brady confirmed that the
organization had applied for and received funds from the city, noting it was
an easier case to make on that side.
Council Member Chapman, who had been present at the ATAC meeting,
provided context. He noted that he had encouraged all applicants —
including the Turkey Day Run — to coordinate with Explore Charleston for
advertising purposes. He explained that Explore Charleston had agreed to
assist the Turkey Day Run by making race applications available at the
town's visitor’s center and by encouraging participants to stay at hotels in
Mount Pleasant, which are located near the packet pickup site. He
acknowledged that the actual number of Mount Pleasant hotel nights
generated could not yet be quantified but noted it was the first time such a
partnership arrangement had been established.
Mayor Haynie noted that the ATAC committee had recommended the
Turkey Day Run funding for this cycle, and that the organization had
improved its application over the past several years, though the Council
had declined to fund it in previous cycles. He stated he would support the
ATAC recommendation for this year.
Mayor Haynie called for the vote on the amendment to remove the Turkey
Run funding. Council Members Brownstein, Rambo, Iacofano and
Chapman voted in favor with Council Members Hyatt, Tinkey, Crosby and
Haynie voting opposed. The motion failed with a 4-4 vote.
Mayor Haynie called for the vote on the original motion to approve the
funding as recommended and all present voted in favor.
