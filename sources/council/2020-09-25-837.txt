III.      Action to establish a Millage Rate and Sales Tax Credit
       Factor
       Ms. Marcy Cotov, Chief Finance Officer, stated that every year Town
       Council is required to establish and certify to Charleston County, the
       Town’s millage rate and Local Option Sales Tax Credit Factor, once
       the Town’s property appraisals and assessments are received in
       September. This year is slightly different, because it is a
reassessment year. She stated that property is reassessed every five
(5) years. She stated that new construction and growth are not
factored in; however, for reassessed properties it needs to be
revenue neutral.

    Council continued discussions.
    Ms. Whitley moved to lower the total millage rate to .04 with an
operating millage of 0.0347 and a debt millage of .0053; seconded by
Mr. O’Rourke.
     Mr. Chapman stated that as Council reviews the debt millage and
the motion that is currently on the floor, to encourage the Town
Administrator to get the study underway so this may also help
residents who may not feel they are receiving the full rollback in the
millage.
Mr. O’Rourke stated that he agrees with Mr. Chapman and believes
there is merit in what was stated. He said if Council votes for what
was stated, he believes there will be funding available for those
projects stated by Ms. Whitley and Mr. Chapman, and likely some of
the items Mr. Santos stated.
Mr. Rambo stated that we are in the middle of a pandemic that has
created an economic crisis for many. He said businesses and people
are hurting. He stated that he cannot support raising anyone’s taxes.
He stated to receive a higher tax bill this year than the previous year
is not right.
Mayor Haynie asked what the average house is in Mount Pleasant.
He said what would account for a higher tax rate is the appreciation
of their property.
Ms. Cotov stated that she calculated those homes $250,000 to $1
million and also put in a recommendation for the tax credit factor.

Mayor Haynie stated that he is trying to figure out what the difference
is on a $400,000 house from what was being paid and what the
resident will be paying now.
Ms. Cotov showed Town Council the difference. She stated that this
is without the LOST factor.




Mayor Haynie asked when the property owners would receive their
tax bill and be required to pay the tax.
Ms. Cotov stated that it would be due in January 2021.
Mr. O’Rourke stated that he believes the Town needs this funding in
order to continue servicing the residents. He added that the Town is
not taking more money, because the residents will be the recipients
of these projects. He said there are projects that must be done.
Mr. Chapman discussed the issues with drainage in the Town that
must be addressed due to rising tides.
Mr. Santos stated that he was in favor of Ms. Whitley’s comments
regarding the referendum on Recreation and asked if Ms. Whitley
would include that in her motion.
Ms. Whitley asked if Mr. Santos’ issue can be addressed at the
Recreation Committee meeting in October.

Mayor Haynie stated that when money is put into the debt millage
and then funding goes into the operating millage, how does the Town
assure the taxpayers that it is being used for those projects.
Mr. DeMoura stated that funding captured on the debt millage side
must be used for capital.
Ms. Cotov stated that the debt millage is sent to a debt fund and must
abide by all the criteria and may only be used for capital.
Mayor Haynie stated that he would like to keep the operating millage
the same and increasing the debt millage by something less than the
0.0053. He said most of our citizens are benefitting from rising
property values and they are primarily those experiencing flooding.
He can justify capturing a small portion of this increase to bond and
fix the Town’s major flooding issues where those residents are not
doing as well in terms of appreciation. He believes the 0.0053 is too
much during a pandemic and if it is lowered, he would be able to
support this.
Ms. Cotov stated that with the .0062, this will fully fund the $2.1
million.
Mr. O’Rourke stated that he believes it is dangerous to decrease
operating millage or any millage. He said there is a list of capital
projects that the Town needs operating funding for and believes there
may even be a list of operational projects. He stated that there is also
the unknown. He stated that he would be in favor of a compromise in
the total, not decreasing any millage, and the increase going towards
the debt.
Ms. Whitley stated that she would agree to take it from 41.0 to 39.3
which would be 1.7 and would be 0.0046, so the debt millage would
go up to .0046 instead of .0053 and the operating millage would stay
the same.
    Ms. Whitley amended her motion for the operating millage of 34.7
and a debt of 4.6 for a total of 39.3; seconded by Mr. Chapman.
Mayor Haynie stated that there is a motion that would put the
operations millage at 0.0347 which is the revenue neutral number,
the debt at 0.0046 for a total of 39.3 mills which is less than 2 mills
total of what would be totally revenue neutral. He said this would go
into effect immediately and reflected in the outgoing property tax bills.
