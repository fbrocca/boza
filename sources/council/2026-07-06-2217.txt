1. Call to Order
Mayor Haynie called the Special Town Council Meeting to order on
Monday, July 6, 2026, at 9:00 a.m. Council Members Daniel Brownstein,
Kathryn Whitaker, Laura Hyatt, Howard Chapman, Jake Rambo, Alex
Crosby and Mike Tinkey were present. Council Member John Iacofano
was present remotely via electronic means. The Mayor opened the
meeting by acknowledging and expressing gratitude to town staff,
including the Town Administrator Mr. DeMoura, the Fire Chief, the Police
Chief, and traffic personnel for their response to a serious accident that
occurred over the holiday weekend, noting that the situation was
handled as well as could be expected under the circumstances.
2. Public Comments
The Mayor opened the floor to public comment, advising that speakers
should provide their name and address and would be afforded two and a
half minutes.
Robert Woodul, a representative of the South Carolina Coastal
Conservation League, addressed the Council in support of the so-called
"road to compromise" regarding planned improvements to Highway 41.
He noted that the project represents more than nine years of careful
planning, extensive community input, and thorough environmental
review dating back to 2017, covering matters including water quality,
endangered species, floodplains, wetlands, and historic and cultural
resources.
Mr. Woodul acknowledged that encroaching upon a county park is not
ideal and that widening a country highway through historic family
neighborhoods would not ordinarily be a position the League would
advocate. Nevertheless, he urged the Council to recognize that the
compromise alternative achieves a balance between nature and
community, protects historic communities, reduces congestion,
enhances safety, and provides needed bicycle and pedestrian
improvements.

He further emphasized the fiscal and functional interdependence of the
project components, stating that the traffic model requires the
construction of the Laurel Hill Parkway for the proposed four-three-four-
lane structure on Highway 41 to function effectively. He warned that
abandonment of the Parkway component could jeopardize the entire $60
million state allocation for the integrated project, leaving Mount Pleasant
residents without relief from traffic congestion. He concluded by
acknowledging that the compromise is not a perfect solution, but
affirmed the value of the collaborative process and characterized it as
the best solution available.
No other members of the public came forward, and the Mayor closed the
public comment period.
3. Administration of Oath of Office to Municipal Judges
The Mayor administered the Oath of Office to two newly reappointed
Municipal Judges, Kelley Young and Jack Sinclaire. Both judges were
sworn in before the Council, taking the oath of office in accordance with
the Constitution of the State of South Carolina and pledging to uphold
the integrity and independence of the judiciary, to treat all persons
appearing before them with civility, fairness, and respect, and to seek
justice and justice alone.
4. Executive Session – Legal Advice Related to Charleston County
Highway 41 Improvements
The Mayor announced that the purpose of the executive session was to
allow the Town's legal counsel to provide legal advice to all Council
members simultaneously on the matter of Charleston County Highway
41 Improvements, as required to be conducted in a called meeting with a
published agenda. The Mayor noted that, consistent with the posted
agenda, no votes would be taken on executive session items following
the session.
    Motion to enter executive session for the purpose of receiving legal
    advice related to Charleston County Highway 41 Improvements was
    made by Council Member Chapman and seconded by Council
    Member Tinkey. The motion carried.
The Council adjourned to executive session at 9:08 a.m.
5. Post Executive Session
No votes shall be taken on Executive Session Items.
The Mayor reconvened the meeting at 10:02 a.m. He confirmed that no
action was taken and no votes were cast during the executive session,
