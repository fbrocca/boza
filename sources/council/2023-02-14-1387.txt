     Chris Colman, 118 Pitt Street stated what he saw on the

comment on. He said he thinks it needs to be sent back and the
community consensus package adopted. He said 65 decibels is an

the past of municipalities who adopted 55 decibels, and only a few
took 65, with a few industrial areas adopting 70-75. He said if 55
decibels is adopted, an averaging period is probably needed. He
said a one size fits all standard is what is driving the problem, and if
a single number is chosen, it will either be too loud in the residential
area, or too quiet in the commercial area. He said separate
standards are needed.

     Dr. Mark Ghegan, 206 Live Oak Drive stated he is an ear, nose
and throat physician and lives in close proximity to Shem Creek and
is affected by the noise. He said the noise is disruptive on multiple
levels. He said many people have spoken about some of the
recommendations about different decibel levels and some of the
inaccuracies of the information, such as the large range and how it
did not differentiate between commercial and residential properties.
He said the range went from 50 to roughly 80 and an arbitrary
number of 65 was chosen. He said a difference of 10 decibels
does make a difference. He said bass travels and penetrates
houses and windows at a higher level. He said if the decibel level
is increased by 10, you are making something twice as loud. He
said he hopes Council will come together and look at the
recommendations that the business owners and community have
agreed on and adopt those standards.


    Dr. Joanna Ghegan, 206 Live Oak Drive stated she had to
move from downtown Charleston to Mount Pleasant fourteen years
ago for the peace and quiet when their daughter was born. She
said things changed about three years ago when the night life
scene intensified. Bars started remaining open later, music started
being played louder and it attracted a different kind of crowd than
we had before. She said we had our first shooting, cars started
being stolen and vandalized, and one night she said she found a
girl tragically passed out in the middle of Live Oak Drive. She said
with respect to the effect on sleep, they have tried white noise
makers, fans, earplugs, and the truth is, greater than 60 decibels
and even lower levels with bass penetrates the house. She said
there are a lot of young children and newborns that live in the area,
and cognitive development and concentration focus are affected
directly by quality of sleep. She asked Council to consider this and
listen to recommended levels to promote health and happiness.
