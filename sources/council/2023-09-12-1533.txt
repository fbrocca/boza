IV.           PUBLIC HEARINGS, AWARDS & PRESENTATIONS
       A. Public Hearing: A Second Public Hearing to receive input on a proposed
          amendment to the Development Agreement by and between DRP SC 2,
          LLC (the “Developer”) and the Town of Mount Pleasant, South Carolina.
          Proposed is to extend the Development Agreement for five (5) years, and
          to amend the development schedule due to unexpected delays, including
          the Covid Pandemic and Supply Chain Issues. Liberty Hill Farms is
          comprised of approximately 209.651 acres of land and whose location is
          generally described as being between Rifle Range Road and North
          Lakeshore Drive in the Six Mile Community.
          Stuart Whiteside, 510 Wando Park Boulevard stated he is
     representing the applicant in his request for an amendment due to project
     delays caused by COVID and supply chain issues. Mr. Whiteside said
     throughout the project the applicant has contributed funds and made
     improvements for the Town and surrounding area, totaling approximately
     three to four million dollars, as well as donating seven acres of land for a
     town park. He said that does not include all of the impact fees paid as well,
     and they are now asking for a three-year extension to finish the project. He
     said they are willing to contribute $25,000 to the proposed bike path on
     Rifle Range Road. Mr. Whiteside said he would be available for questions
     when the item is considered later on the agenda.
     There being no other speakers, Mayor Haynie closed the public hearing.
B.      Recognition of 1950 Moultrie High School Football State
     Champions
     Mr. Chapman stated 73 years ago this fall, the Moultrie High School
     Generals 1950 football team outscored their opponents by a combined total
     of 377 to 40 points and went undefeated to win the South Carolina High
     School League state football title. He said to this day, that football
     championship remains the only South Carolina High School state title won
     by a Mount Pleasant high school. Mr. Chapman stated we are here to
     honor that football team and their accomplishments. He said with us
     tonight is a member of that team, Dr. Charles Darby. He added that
     unfortunately another member of the team, Mr. Bob Royall, former
     Secretary of Commerce, was unable to attend due to illness.
     Mr. Chapman said that in recent years, the state championship trophy from
     the 1950 season was found and refurbished by Baker Motors owner and
     Moultrie High School alumnus Tommy Baker. Ms. Colleen Flynn, Mr.
     Baker’s executive assistant, is representing Mr. Baker. Mr. Chapman
     stated he contacted Mr. Jeff Borrowy, the Chief Operating Officer of the
     Charleston County School District, and arranged for the trophy to be
     displayed at Moultrie Middle School as it is the site where the team played.
     Mr. Chapman congratulated the Moultrie High School Generals, and
     thanked Tommy Baker and Jeff Borrowy for running interference to get the
     trophy housed in an appropriate place of honor, so as to inspire
     generations to come.
     Dr. Darby thanked the Town for the recognition and stated they all had a
     wonderful time playing that year, adding that most of the members of the
     team went on to be successful citizens.

VI.        PUBLIC COMMENT
            David Guttman, 1533 Deene Park Circle stated he wanted to
      comment on Highway 41 and Dunes West Boulevard. He said he lives
      in Dunes West, and they are surrounded by the Philips Community,
      Rivertowne, and Park West. He said the current proposal for Highway
      41 is not going to work. He said property from a park will be taken
      away to go to a highway which leads to a traffic circle that separates
      Park West and Dunes West. He asked Council to leave Highway 41
      the way it is now as they are all very happy with it. He said the best
      solution would be to build a highway just north of Carolina Park where
      there is vacant land.
            Janet Newham, 510 Pitt Street stated she found out last week
      that there is a concept proposal in place to divide up Edwards Park that
      is not in alignment with recognizing the history of this space. She said
      the original town common of Mount Pleasant does not need to be
      divided up to include a formally designated dog park. Ms. Newham
      said there is a petition being circulated against dividing Edwards Park,
      and there are over 500 signatures on it. She stated she believed the
      overarching position is to protect this historical space.
            Sarah Mitchell, 313 Royall Avenue stated she lives across from
      Edwards Park, and she does not think it is a good idea to turn it into a
      dog park. She said there are two children in the neighborhood who
      have been hit by cars, and one was seriously injured. She said there
      is so much traffic going in and out of the neighborhood, so adding more
      traffic into the neighborhood into Edwards Park is detrimental. Ms.
      Mitchell said she went to all of the homes that border the park to make
      sure they all are against turning it into a dog park. She said there are
      already three official dog parks in town that are not in neighborhoods.
            Michael Werner, 802 Balgrove Court stated he has lived in Mount
      Pleasant for seventeen years, works for a contractor and drives about
      150 miles a day around Mount Pleasant. He said what he sees with
      traffic and bike traffic is scary, and he feels this ordinance is targeting

     Peter Boatti, 1361 Heron Bay Loop stated there is going to be a

director for Liberty Senior Living. He said South Bay residents enjoy a

and a majority of their residents are from in and around Mount
Pleasant.
     James Wilson, 722 Dragoon Drive stated he is here on behalf of
South Bay. He said there is a need in the community as there is a
waiting list. He said the expansion was approved in the Central Mount
Pleasant Planned Development so this is not a new project, and the
reason it is before Council now is they are seeking approval to move
forward with it despite the existing moratorium. Mr. Wilson said they
have tailored the project so that it is a full continuing care retirement
community with a full continuum of care, licensed by the Department
of Consumer Affairs. He said the traffic impact assessment that was
done for Central Mount Pleasant included the continuing care unit. He
said he and Mr. Allen were available for questions when the item is
considered later on the agenda.
There being no additional speakers, Mayor Haynie closed the public

X.      COUNCIL BUSINESS
      A. New Business
        1.       Action to establish a Millage Rate and Sales Tax Credit
             Factor
             Ms. Cotov stated the millage rate is 34.7 which is the same as it
             currently is for operating, and 8.6 for debt millage which is an
             increase of 4 mills based on the referendum. She stated these
             numbers with the assessed and appraised values the County
             has provided support our budget. She said overall there is a
             6.4% increase over the prior year. Ms. Cotov said the other
             item is the Local Option Sales Tax (LOST) credit, and staff is
             requesting to increase it from .00045 to .0005. She said that is
             based on the amount of sales tax by which we are required to
             reduce tax bills. She said based on what we have in the
             current year budget and what we have in our liability account
             from prior years that we have not been able to reduce, we are
             requesting to reduce this. Ms. Cotov said for a $500,000
             appraised value house with the new millage, there would be an
             increase of $80. The increase of the Local Option Sales Tax
             Credit will increase the reduction to those bills by $25, so the
             net increase to a $500,000 home is $55.
             Ms. Cotov said the request is to set the millage rate at a total of
             43.3 with a Local Option Sales Tax credit of .0005.
