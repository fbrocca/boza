VI.   PUBLIC COMMENT
      Sean Barnes, 1418 Barbara Street stated he is concerned about
      Center Street and McCants Street. He said he knows the Town is
      working with the South Carolina Department of Transportation but
      would like to have flashing lights or something to slow down traffic now
      before someone is hurt.
      Josh Dix, 640 Rutledge Avenue stated he is with the Charleston
      Trident Association of Realtors, and he would like to speak in favor of
      the Towne Centre project. He stated this is a great way to adaptively
      reuse shopping centers. He said this is the type of housing our region
      needs, and he thanked Council for considering the proposal.
      Tony Beall, 921 Pine Hollow Road stated he is concerned about a
      very large dock being built potentially across Shem Creek. He said
      this is not under the purview of the Town, but rather the state, and
      wanted to bring it to everyone’s attention as the comment period for
      this closes Wednesday afternoon.
      Alan McMahon, 610 Chimney Bluff Drive stated he is speaking in
      favor of the Towne Centre Planned Development amendment as it
      would allow growth in an area with existing infrastructure. He said it is
      environmentally friendly as there would be no new impervious surface
      being added and would likely add more pervious surface. He said it
      will not allow the clearing of a large tract of land, or the removal of
      grand trees. Mr. McMahon stated it would create a walkable, high-
      energy environment, and would provide attainable housing for
      members of the professional community who need it so direly.
      Elizabeth Horton, 291 Commonwealth Road stated she is here to
      speak in support of the second farmer’s market. She would not like to
      see it located anywhere along Highway 17 due to safety concerns.
      Jody Mantie, 675 Kent Street stated she is speaking on the change in
      the home business ordinance. She sent to Council statements from
      the neighbors surrounding her home, most of who work from home,
      stating they have no issues with traffic or noise that her business, home
      swim lessons, produces. She would like to see the ordinance changed
                                                                     Page 7 of 41
to include outdoor activities. She said she teaches swim lessons to
one child at a time, and the times most in demand for lessons are
before 10:00 a.m. and after 3:00 p.m., mostly from working parents.
Ms. Mantie said the noise ordinance states the level between 8:00 a.m.
and 10:30 p.m. is 60 decibels and she feels this should help serve as
a guideline for when outdoor activities may be conducted. She asked
Council to please support homebased swim lessons that will help keep
the youngest members of our community safe.
Steven Mantie, 675 Kent Street asked that this ordinance change
please not be about 675 Kent Street. He said being an outdoor
business, there will be weather to contend with, and days where it will
be too hot or too cold. He asked how a small business would be able
to survive when being limited to four hours a day. He said the only
complaints about noise and traffic have come from one address on
Kent Street. He asked that Council amend the ordinance to allow for
outdoor homebased businesses.
Ellen Moore, 1469 Gunnison Street said she is excited about the
possibility of having a farmer’s market on the north end of town. She
said she thinks it will be well received and likes the idea of it being held
at Carolina Park where there are facilities and ample parking.
Greg Sobecki, 1469 Gunnison Street asked for the Town to address
the traffic situation at Faison and Park West Boulevard at Costco. He
said there is a left turn signal there that has never been activated. He
said this morning at 8:10, traffic was backed up about 12 cars blocking
the left-hand lane on Park West Boulevard, and it took four cycles to
get through the intersection. He said it will be a simple fix.
Sharon Scott, 630 E. Hobcaw Drive stated she is speaking for
Alhambra Garden Club. She said in September of 2023, Alhambra
Garden Club reached out to the Town to explore a new avenue to
promote the historic value of Patjen’s Post Office which was built in
1899, and was the first freestanding post office in Mount Pleasant. On
October 13, 2023, Alhambra Representatives met with Town staff
members where Alhambra was advised by James Ward that the Town
believed it owned Patjen’s Post Office. Mr. Ward advised Alhambra
Garden Club that if they disagreed, they should provide evidence. She
said they proceeded to compile such evidence and found historical
documents and information, including the Town Council minutes of
June 7, 1971, evidencing the Town of Mount Pleasant, provided

                                                                  Page 8 of 41
permission to Alhambra Garden Club to place Patjen’s Post Office in
its present location. Ms. Scott said their attorney, Lewis Horton, has
provided all this information to Mr. Pagliarini. She said Alhambra, with
the help of Town Council Member Harry Hitopolous, restored the
historic building. Since then, as the historical marker notes, and as
Mayor Haynie stated on February 12, 2019 when he presented the
Club with the Cresco Award, “since 1971, the Alhambra Garden Club
has owned, maintained and promoted Patjen’s Post Office.” Ms. Scott
said they are willing to work with the Town for the best outcome for
Patjen’s Post Office and for the people of Mount Pleasant.
Katie Egbers, 1028 Banker Court said she is here to speak in favor of
the proposed amendment regarding homebased businesses. She said
she does not know from where the four-hour restriction came. She
said weather can be an issue, and asked who would enforce the
restriction. She asked if there are any other businesses that have
these restrictive hours. She said this will also limit the number of
children who will be receiving the lessons, and there is already a wait-
list for instruction.
Sonya Boyd stated she is originally from Pittsburgh and wanted to
speak in favor of another farmer’s market which would help to enhance
and make a more sustainable community with a place for gathering. It
would give residents another opportunity to obtain organic, non-
genetically modified products.
Patrick McCleary said he is here to voice his support for a farmer’s
market in upper Mount Pleasant and it is much needed. He said the
most requested thing he has heard is that people want more options
for organic food. He said he believes Park West is the best option for
the new market. Mr. McCleary stated he would love to help secure
vendors.
Joanne Gorham, 683 Kent Street said she is the neighbor near where
the swimming lessons are taking place. She said this has been going
on for three years and she has tried to be understanding, even having
conversations. She said her bedroom is adjacent to her back yard
where the swim lessons are occuring, and she said the noise is
sometimes unbearable. Ms. Gorham said she is home sometimes with
health problems. She said she understands children need swim
lessons, but she needs to be able to rest. She asked Council to please
be considerate of both situations.

   Chapter 156, Zoning Code, of the Mount Pleasant Code of

   instructional activities, including but not limited to swimming
   lessons, with conditions. (Ord. No. 24029)
  Mr. Rambo moved for first reading, seconded by Mr.
  Brownstein.
  Mr. Rambo said he sees the value in these home-based swim
  lessons as extremely important given where we live. He said he
  believes the restriction of four hours a day are overly
  burdensome. He said he would be looking to amend the
  ordinance at final reading to give some more flexibility to the
  instructors.
  Mr. Brownstein said he wondered why we would need to

  regulations from such entities as the Department of Health and
  Environmental Control. Mr. Pagliarini said this is a change in
  the Zoning Code to allow this use, and any business would be
  required to have a business license and would need to follow
  any other state or local requirements. He said by enacting this
  ordinance, it would not change any such requirements.

4. Towne Centre Planned Development Amendment.
   Discussion regarding the proposed PD Amendment to the
   Wilson Tract Planned Development District Ordinance (Ord. No.
   95041), otherwise known as the Towne Centre Tract. Specific
   for discussion is the updated proposed height request for certain
   portions of the development and possible thresholds for
   activation of the approved additional height. Also, discussion
   and possible action regarding a request to solicit a consultant to
   conduct an economic feasibility study to help inform Town
   Council on this proposal and other similar future projects. (Ord.
   No. 24026) Planning Commission recommended approval
   with modifications; Planning Committee recommended
   approval with modifications.
   Ms. Whitley asked for confirmation of the deadline to have a final
   decision on this ordinance and Mr. Pagliarini responded
   February. Ms. Whitley asked if it could be extended and Mr.
   Pagliarini responded in the negative, stating it would be denied.
   Ms. Reed reviewed the recommended amendments and the
   option to solicit a consultant to conduct an economic feasibility
   study to help inform decisions for this project and future projects.
   Ms. Whitley said the primary reason that was given to Council
   as to why the developer is coming to us now is about the
   potential failure of Belk and Regal Cinemas and the concern
   about replacing those. She said her thought was to build
   triggers into the planned development amendment, so they

                                                            Page 17 of 41
receive the ability to redevelop those areas only after those
failures. She said any benefits given to this developer as far as
entitlement to height or density in housing is a financial boon to
that investor. She said as a benefit to our community, if they
double the park space then perhaps, they could be given
additional height in certain zones. She said she would like to
leave the one parking lot as-is. Ms. Whitley said the question is,
what should be the height. They originally asked for 75 feet, and
then came back asking for 65 feet, stating they economically
cannot make these projects happen without 65 feet of height,
which is why she suggested the consultant.
Mr. Brownstein said he agrees with bringing on a consultant. He
said he would also propose creating a family entertainment zone
should the movie theater fail, so that anything that replaces it
would be family-oriented entertainment.
Mr. Iacofano said he does not think this setting allows us the
opportunity to have a discussion and he would like to see this
go to a retreat to finalize the details rather than passing it back
and forth between Committee and Council.
Mr. Chapman said he likes 35 feet on Highway 17, and when we
ended up with the two buildings and extra open space, he asked
if we were going to suggest moving forward with that, rather than
waiting for possible failure of Belk and the theater. Ms. Whitley
said yes, that would happen immediately. Mr. Chapman asked
for the height of the parking garage on Hungryneck and Ms.
Reed stated it is allowed to be up to 42 feet but was constructed
at 35 feet. Mr. Chapman said we should keep it at 35 feet on
both Highway 17 and Hungryneck. Mr. Chapman said he wants
to use a wedding-cake design and stay with the 55 foot height.
He thanked the developer for working with Council, and also for
lowering the density to 8 housing units per acre with 2 units of
affordable housing.
Ms. Hyatt said the developer already suggested doubling the
open space, and they also agreed to adhere to the Town’s
ordinances on density, and with respect to heights, they wanted
the heights according to percentages.

Ms. Whitley asked if it is possible to put in a family entertainment
restriction into the planned development, and Mr. Pagliarini
responded in the affirmative.
Mr. Rambo said it is hard to be transparent with the public about
what is proposed because it is changing so often. He said he is
not comfortable giving out blanket entitlements and would rather
see a specific plan.
Ms. Hyatt said she agrees with Mr. Rambo.
Ms. Whitley said she is fine with limited triggers, 55 feet in height

Ms. Whitley asked for clarification if the motion is for a consultant

could possibly have someone secured within 60 days.
Mr. Rambo said these are our decisions to make, and this is why
we are elected.

       1. Discussion and possible action related to a two year extension
          to the Recreation Advisory Commission and review of the

         Committee, seconded by Mr. Ritchie.

         be written at the next Health, Recreation, and Events
         Committee that specifies the number of seats, exactly what the

         Council Member chairing the Commission.
