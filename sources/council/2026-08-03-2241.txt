3. Presentation By Friends Of Faison And Discussion Regarding
The Town Owned Property On Faison Road
Tom Biddie and Mike Elder, residents of the Riverside neighborhood in
Carolina Park, presented on behalf of Friends of Faison, a community
group formed to advocate for thoughtful development of the 38-acre
town-owned parcel on Faison Road. The group has been active for
approximately eight to nine months and received unanimous support
from the Riverside HOA board, representing approximately 550
homeowners.
The presenters provided a brief history of the property: the Town of
Mount Pleasant purchased the parcel in 2021 for approximately
$6,500,000, undertook multiple solicitation and planning efforts between
2022 and 2024, engaged Colliers to market the property, and had a
contract with a private developer that ultimately did not close. The
property is currently still town-owned and not actively being marketed.

Mr. Elder summarized survey results gathered from 31 respondents
across two campaigns. The top community priorities identified were:
community-oriented development and quality of life (65%), green space
and walking trails (45%), restaurants and outdoor dining (42%), an
amphitheater or performing arts venue (39%), and entertainment
destinations and gathering spaces (32%). Residents most strongly
opposed car dealerships, warehouse or storage facilities, and large-
scale generic commercial development.
Council discussion touched on several themes. Mayor Haynie clarified
that Carolina Park's development patterns stem from a 25-year-old
development agreement that predates current council decisions and
noted the town's significant investment in the Carolina Park Recreation
Complex. He emphasized that as long as the property remains town-
owned, it is off the tax rolls and represents a cost to taxpayers and
asked the group to clarify whether residents were comfortable with a
government-owned, nonprofit-use model. The presenters confirmed that
residents are open to a public-private partnership model, provided the
town maintains community-focused development restrictions.
Council Member Brownstein expressed support for a YMCA facility,
citing the need for aquatics, childcare, after-school programming, and a
second senior center in upper Mount Pleasant. He recommended
focusing on one or two uses done well, rather than attempting a complex
multi-use plan, noting that the prior plan's breadth contributed to its
failure. Council Member Rambo expressed a desire to preserve as much
of the property in its natural state as possible while incorporating family-
oriented amenities such as an indoor recreation facility, noting the
absence of such options in Mount Pleasant. Council Member Iacofano
suggested that future development be primarily private, with the town
restricting use and requiring a defined amount of community space and
called for re-engagement through the Economic Development
Committee.
Mayor Haynie recommended that the matter be referred back to the
Economic Development Committee of Council for further deliberation,
which council members affirmed.
