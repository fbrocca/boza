4. Public Hearings, Awards, & Presentations
A. Administration of Oath of Office to Mayor-Elect
Mayor Haynie began the meeting by recognizing veterans in attendance
for Veterans Day, asking them to stand and be honored. He then gave
special recognition to Gary Santos, noting him as "former Mayor Pro
Tem, and said he believed Council Member Santos was the longest
serving council member in the history of Mount Pleasant." Mayor Haynie
mentioned there would be more plans to honor Council Member Santos
in the future.
Judge Hesse administered the oath of office to Mayor-elect Will Haynie,
with First Lady Suzette Haynie standing by his side. The Mayor-elect
repeated the oath solemnly, swearing to uphold the constitution of the
state and United States, and to "equally, fairly, and impartially" execute
the trust reposed in him as mayor of the Town of Mount Pleasant.
After taking the oath, Mayor Haynie thanked his wife Suzette for all she
does "not only for me, but for this town." He expressed gratitude to
everyone for the privilege of serving as mayor, stating "I would not want
to be mayor anywhere else but Mount Pleasant South Carolina. And it's
just been the highlight of my leadership endeavors."




B. Administration of Oath of Office to Council Members-Elect
Mayor Haynie then administered the oath of office to the newly reelected
council members Mike Tinkey and John Iacofano, and new council
members Kathryn Whitaker and Alex Crosby. The council members-
elect raised their right hands and repeated the oath in unison, swearing
to uphold their duties and the constitutions of the state and United
States. Council Member Santos left the meeting having completed his

Eric Adams from Charleston County presented on the Transportation
Sales Tax program. He began by thanking the mayor and council for
having them, explaining they were there to discuss the future of the
program as "we as citizens of Charleston County are at a crossroads."
He noted the first sales tax from 2004 is set to expire in 2027, raising the
question of whether citizens want to extend it.
Mr. Adams explained that County Council formed a special committee in
September to discuss this issue publicly and transparently. The
committee requested listening sessions with municipalities to understand
their specific needs, recognizing that "you know your community better
                                                           Town Council Meeting

than we do, and it may be different than what a different area of the
county needs."
Key points from the presentation included:
   ● Over $4.5 billion has been committed to infrastructure projects
     over the program's life
   ● The county has leveraged about $1.2 billion from state and federal
     government due to the local option sales tax
   ● About half of the revenue comes from non-residents of Charleston
     County
   ● Over half the roads in Charleston County are state-owned
   ● The Green Belt program has been extremely successful, with 49%
     of the county's total acreage protected
   ● CARTA has upgraded their fleet to over 125 vehicles and
     improved bus stops
   ● Population has increased 33% since 2004, with vehicle
     registrations up 41.5% since 2008
Mr. Adams presented investment categories for consideration,
estimating about $4.25 billion would be raised over a 25-year program.
Categories included Green Belt programs, bike and pedestrian
infrastructure, public transit, roadways, rural safety, intersection safety,
and addressing bottlenecks.
Council Member Rambo raised pointed questions about trust, asking
"why should our citizens trust the county with more money after the main
project that was supposed to happen for the Town of Mount Pleasant
was Highway 41, and it's been completely mismanaged and botched?"
He referenced recent newspaper quotes where elected officials were
"essentially laughing at the citizens of Mount Pleasant, not saying very
nice things about this community." Rambo stated firmly that "a new sales
tax will not get passed with the issues currently out here in the Town of
Mount Pleasant," noting that the Highway 41 area had voted down the
last sales tax referendum "by an incredibly large margin."
Council Member Iacofano followed up, expressing frustration that Mount
Pleasant had submitted their top priorities and he believed number 1
was the US 17 and Houston Northcutt intersection which was ignored.
He questioned why they should spend time and resources providing
input "if we rank it number 1" as elected officials representing their
citizens' best interests, only to have it disregarded. He later suggested
having a workshop with the county to work through the issues.
                                                             Town Council Meeting

Council Member Chapman spoke passionately about the importance of
the half cent sales tax, revealing he had the privilege of working with the
state legislature to get the law passed that actually allows us to do this.
He emphasized that without the sales tax, the local option for CARTA
dries up and without local funding, there would be no federal funds.
Chapman stressed the significance for the community, noting there are a
lot of people in this community that if they don't have CARTA, don't have
a job. They can't get to work. He urged the council to work together on a
plan and support it for the sake of restaurants, hotels, and residents who
rely on CARTA.
Council Member Whitaker asked about the definition of green space and
green belt, with Eric Davis from the green belt program clarifying that the
Department of Revenue defines what it can be used for, primarily
acquisition of land or interest in land, so conservation easements as well
as fee simple purchases.
Mayor Haynie suggested increasing the green space percentage from
the proposed 13% to 20%, explaining our green infrastructure is part of
our resiliency infrastructure too, and that's less money we need for pipes
to put in the ground. He also proposed creating a checklist for green belt
spending that would allow more local control without having to go
through the green belt advisory board and council votes. The mayor
expressed interest in finding a way to include undergrounding of power
lines, calling it potentially our only shot at a pool of $4 billion for that type
of resiliency infrastructure.
Council Member Tinkey echoed the importance of stormwater
management, stating "We all know where the roads flood today, and
those are real issues for us." He emphasized the importance of the
green belt program "as far as being able to take cars off the road" and
expressed openness to working together, saying "we will work together
as a community to help the community."

with Ms. Reed confirming it's rural residential at 1 unit per 3 acres in the
county, but the county is considering changing to 1 unit per 1 acre.

Ms. Reed presented the three components of the ordinance: changes to
height and setbacks for detached accessory structures in the Old Village
Historic District (lowering maximum height to 20 feet or height of
principal structure, increasing setbacks from 6 to 10 feet); town-wide
changes for detached accessory structures (requiring 10-foot setbacks
for portions over 18 feet high, limiting height to not exceed principal

structure, and limiting size to 850 square feet or 40% of primary
structure); and extending BAR (Board of Architectural Review)
requirements to Old Mount Pleasant neighborhoods.
Mayor Haynie emphasized these are areas without HOAs where it falls
upon the town to try to protect and give these neighborhoods the
protection to maintain neighborhood character and prevent situations
where a 1,500 square foot house sits next to a 4,500 square foot house.
He noted they're trying not to be too restrictive while helping with
resiliency.
Council Member Tinkey asked about protections for houses 50 years
and older to prevent demolitions. Ms. Reed explained they had
discussed this extensively but struggled with determining review criteria
and who would conduct reviews, as this area doesn't go to the Historic
District Preservation Commission. Tinkey suggested looking at simplified
ways to provide protection, noting they are just going down one after
another.
Council Member Brownstein expressed his overarching concern that
these changes were prompted by a relatively small group of citizens
months ago, and we haven't really heard much public comment since
then. He encouraged Old Mount Pleasant residents to pay close
attention and provide feedback. While agreeing with the building area
ratio concerns, he worried the accessory structure height restrictions
might encourage somebody to demolish the home in order to get that
height on the accessory structure. He noted many longtime residents
whose kids might need accessory structures to afford living in Mount
Pleasant. Brownstein cautioned about creating a de facto historic district
and urged residents to consider the dramatic impact on property values.
Council Member Tinkey supported communicating with the
approximately 2,400 homes affected between first and second reading,
suggesting methods like Mount Pleasant Water Works uses - signs,
flyers, and emails. He referenced excellent presentations to the planning
commission showing how these things could actually work out and that
residents still could add on to the houses. He advocated for
overcommunication to ensure buy-in and avoid people saying we did not
know about it.
Council Member Iacofano questioned why town-wide changes and one
section's changes were on the same agenda item, calling it a very large
sweeping change to Mount Pleasant citizens. He expressed concern
about giving citizens only 30 days notice on changes affecting their
