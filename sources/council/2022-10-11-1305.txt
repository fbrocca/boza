      Rob Corbett – 1209 Meadow Park Lane stated live music is great
if not too loud and he believes it should be left to police discretion. He

it has seemed like the noise has gotten worse. He said no one wants

but setting the decibel level at 60 would be disastrous as even if ninety-

responsibly, it only takes one person to abuse it and ruin the most
special days of people’s lives. He asked that it be left up to the
discretion of the police.

      James Scott – 415 Church Street thanked Council for their hard
work on this issue. He said he sympathizes with the residents that live
on Shem Creek, but he is approaching this as a business owner on Pitt
Street. He said there are a handful of areas in town that have had
problems, but a lot of areas that do not, and that is where the value of
having it be subjective is beneficial. This is a solution in search of a
problem. He said there is no uniformity for businesses as some are
close to residential areas and some are farther away, so locking in
numbers could damage other businesses. He said removing leaf
blowers, construction, auto mechanics and other businesses would put
this straight on the backs of the food and beverage business and that
is not fair.

      Jaclyn Story – 2020 Proximity Drive said she owns Gala Café
and Bakery on Pitt Street, and she wants to speak about the noise
ordinance. She said she wants to make sure patrons can enjoy
outdoor dining, but she empathizes with those who have music
invading their homes. Ms. Story said she hopes Council can come up
with something reasonable that supports businesses and musicians
that depend on it so much.


                                                                Page 6 of 24
     Carmen Scott – 415 Church Street said the Town’s pilot program
was designed to see if the current ordinance needs amending, and if
so, what decibel level it needs to be at. She said during the pilot
program in May the police received over 5700 calls, with 14 of those
being noise ordinance violation calls. 9 were officer checks, where the
officers would go out on their own and check noise levels. She said of
the complaints and officer checks, the highest decibel reading was 70,
and the lowest was 49, and the average was 62. Ms. Scott said in
June there were only 8 noise complaints, and in July just 2 citizen
complaints. Ms. Scott said the current ordinance is subjective and
allows the police to issue fines, so she wanted to know what problem
there is that we are trying to solve. She said setting a decibel level
would cause more harm to businesses.

     Josh Schaap – 413 Coleman Boulevard stated he opposes noise
ordinance at 60 decibels.

      Charlie Chance – 803 Coleman Boulevard with Red Drum
restaurant stated he is here to oppose the current proposal for the
noise ordinance. He stated they have owned the restaurant for 17
years and they are also their own neighbors. He said when things may
have gotten a little loud, the police would come and ask them to close
the windows and lower the music and they would comply. He said it
seems the 60 decibel level proposed seems to be quite low.

     Bill Dietch – 216 Bennett Street said he supports the idea of a 60
decibel standard. He said people are being deprived of the quiet
enjoyment of their homes by what is a real problem. He said there are
hundreds of ordinances around the country that use 55-60 decibel
standards and it does not appear to create any real concerns on the
part of businesses. They learn to deal with those things. He said what
we are hearing from those who are in favor of the subjective ordinances
is that there is a fear which is hardly justified by the reality of what goes
on in most of the country where there are ordinances that have been
around for a long time without imposing any severe restrictions on
business owners.         He said the police do have discretion when
enforcing the ordinance.

     Ben Towill – 228 Forrest Trail, owner of the Post House thanked
Council for their time and effort on this. He said he is here to oppose
the proposed ordinance. He said as a restaurant owner, this has been
                                                                   Page 7 of 24
a divisive issue. He said there were some issues with noise when we
came out of the pandemic and the neighbors came forward and raised
the concern. They got together and had a discussion as to how to
resolve it, putting some measures in place. He said those that have
issues should call the police to have it logged and the businesses
should be fined if they are violating the existing ordinance. He said the
proposed 60 decibels will create issues between neighbors and
businesses. He said they have dealt with the pandemic, inflation and
a staffing crisis. Mr. Towill said they had spent hours and hours and
legal fees coming to meetings over the last three to six months. He
said he thinks the existing ordinance allows people to get together and
deal with the problem in their neighborhood and in their district, and
this proposed change will create tension among neighbors.

        Mike Tinkey – 208 Venning Street said the amount of
divisiveness, dissention, dissatisfaction tonight, plus over a year of
trying to amend what in many people’s opinion was a deeply flawed
process with the initial draft put forth with little public input
demonstrates that no matter what amendments may be made tonight,
few people will be happy and it will leave the community deeply divided.
He asked Council to please vote down or defer the proposed ordinance
to work through it further in order to achieve good neighborliness.

     Chris Colman – 118 Pitt Street stated the existing ordinance is
unenforceable. He said this is a different and controversial issue, and
many people are not happy with 60 decibels, but it is reasonable. 60
and 55 decibels are the most common levels in this region and in the
nation. He said 65 decibels would provide no help to the people along
the Shem Creek corridor. He is asking Council not to vote this
ordinance down, but he agrees that further discussion is needed.

      Shawn Maher – 1117 Mainland Court, the General Manager of

       Eva Suarez, 898 Ashley Avenue, and she oversees Basic
Projects Hospitality and their operation of Post House. She is here to
speak in favor of keeping the existing ordinance which has been in
effect for over 10 years without issues or conducting a three month
study to determine a more reasonable decibel level as she believes 60
decibels is too low. She said she thinks a reasonable agreement can

this standard of fairness, and it will stifle outdoor dining in the
commercial districts and enable complaints threatening the

Council either keep the existing ordinance in place or defer the vote
and work with the interested parties to host a round table discussion

      Jeremy Graves – 1710 (inaudible) Avenue, said he is one of the

to work with everyone around us, and have continually approved that
process as it relates to sound, but at 60 decibels, neighbors will be
deputized and potentially all outdoor dining could be eliminated. He
said he would ask that the vote be deferred and a workshop held.

      Anna Young - 1491 Kinloch Lane stated she is the Events
