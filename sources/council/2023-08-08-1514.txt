2.       Public Comment
             Carol Hallman, 2352 Parsonage Woods Lane stated she is
     encouraged that the Culture, Arts and Pride Commission (CAP) is taking
     seriously the Town’s need to establish adequate arts facilities, however
     she has some concerns. She asked why there has not been systematic
     public input. She said absent this input, recommendations by CAP cannot
     reflect any open or systematic assessment of Mount Pleasant’s needs.
     Ms. Hallman said a lack of transparency can give rise to speculation which
     can lead to division. She said one theory that is circulating is that the
     proposed facility would be intended to house the Village Repertory
     Company, which is a respected private theater company, founded in
     Mount Pleasant and driven out like all of our other theater companies by
     the rising price of land. She said all groups’ needs should be considered
     in the initial proposal. She said she is concerned that the temporary
     facility may become the template for what is ultimately designed. She
     said with regret that she has concluded that the CAP Commission is not
     the group to head up the community arts center project. She said they are
     very good people who are undertaking really valuable projects, but they
     are not adequately staffed, funded or organized to undertake this vital
     once-in-a-lifetime project. Ms. Hallman urged Council to establish a
     separate, temporary, single-purpose entity to undertake this important
     task.
           Ron Mendola, Musical Director of the Mount Pleasant Symphony,
     511 Two Mile Run, Johns Island stated they are grateful for
     accommodations tax support which currently funds roughly one quarter of
     their annual expenditures, and the rest is raised via donations, and
through corporate and private donors. He said their last two seasons
have ended slightly in the red. He said they pay for their rehearsal space,
and for the costs of doing concerts anywhere but Christ Church, which
they currently use free of charge. He said they cannot do concerts at Lucy
Beckham as the costs are prohibitive. He said there are many fully
operational arts organizations in Mount Pleasant that have similar
financial circumstances, and if the Town would wish to provide a structure
for collaborative fundraising, these organizations would be all-in and
would all be winners. That would require the formulation of an arts
development and fundraising strategy that could reach a specific niche of
potential donors. In regard to the theater under discussion from the site
planning to the design of temporary lease space to the ultimate goal, he
would think all of his colleagues would wish to be engaged now rather
than rely on a single planner.
      Marie-Louise Moreto, 1133 Liberty Court, President of the Mount
Pleasant Community Arts Center Committee (MPCAC) stated in March
Town Council voted to postpone a vote on a Mount Pleasant Community
Arts Facility that was originally intended to provide studio space, and
rehearsal space, but not performance space. She said there was a lot of
misinformation regarding that item, and Town Council requested that the
CAP Commission research the issue and present a report to the Town.
She said she applauded the CAP Commission for the time they spent on
this report, however there is still a lack of accurate information, details and
community and public input. She asked if the proposed facility was
intended to be a temporary or permanent arts center. Two conversations
between CAP and the MPCAC were held in May and November of 2020,
and since those conversations, the Town has not reached out to the
MPCAC or other Mount Pleasant art organizations. She said CAP seems
to be recommending issuing a Request for Proposals (RFP) for an artistic
director to take charge of the project, even though the location for a center
has not been decided. She said identifying a location should be the first
step, and then an RFP for a professional theater consultant and project
manager should be issued. She said Mount Pleasant does not need a
small inadequate facility but deserves an arts center that is an iconic
structure that honors its performing, visual and literary artists and cultural
heritage. She said the MPCAC recommends establishing a body with a
single mandate and remove the CAP Commission from the responsibility
for envisioning, planning and overseeing a community arts center. She
asked for establishment of a separate body under the Town’s oversight to
