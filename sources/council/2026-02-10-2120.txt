        A. Presentation of Alhambra Garden Club Proclamation
          Mr. DeMoura read a proclamation regarding the historic Patjens
          Post Office that was built by the Patjens family in 1899 and
          served as the first freestanding post office in Mount Pleasant
          from 1899 to 1917. The proclamation detailed how the
          Alhambra Garden Club had requested permission at the June
          7, 1971 Council meeting to move the post office to the town's
          property, now known as James B. Edwards Park. The club had
          restored the post office and maintained it since 1971, receiving
          the town's Cresco Historical Stewardship Award in February
          2019. The proclamation noted that the club had decided to
          cease its use of the post office and turn the keys over to the
  town. Mayor Haynie accepted the keys from members of the
  Alhambra Garden Club.




B. Reading of a Resolution from the South Carolina
   Legislature in honor of Rear Admiral James H. Flatley, III,
   USN (Ret.)
  Mr. DeMoura read a resolution from the South Carolina House
  of Representatives expressing profound sorrow upon the
  passing of James H. Flatley III, retired Rear Admiral of the
  United States Navy, at the age of 91 on December 12, 2025.
  The resolution highlighted Admiral Flatley's distinguished 31-
  year military career during which he logged 4,460 flight hours in
  over 30 different aircraft and completed 1,608 carrier landings.
  It noted his numerous decorations including the Distinguished

                                                         Page 2 of 42
  Service Medal, Silver Star, Legion of Merit with four gold stars,
  and many others. The resolution also recognized his post-
  military service as CEO of Patriots Point Naval and Maritime
  Museum, where he transformed the site into the state's largest
  tourist attraction, as well as his service on various boards
  including the Roper Saint Francis Health Care in Mount
  Pleasant. Admiral Flatley's widow, Nancy, was present for the
  reading of the resolution.

6. Public Comment
  Parker Smith spoke about his family's funeral home business and
  their plans to relocate within Mount Pleasant. He discussed a
  rezoning request from R-1 to R-2 for their property on Rifle Range
  Road, explaining that residential zoning would be more consistent
  with the existing character of the area than the commercial
  designation in the comprehensive plan. He emphasized their plan to
  design around existing mature trees and create a neighborhood with
  walking paths and green spaces.
  Stuart Whiteside, representing the Smith family, spoke in support of
  rezoning the Rifle Range Road property from R-1 to R-2. He
  explained that the R-2 zoning would provide more flexibility for
  building footprints while allowing them to plan around existing trees.
  He noted that the current R-1 zoning, combined with a drainage
  easement on one side of the property, severely restricts building
  options.
  Marcus McDonald spoke about Black History Month and requested
  the Council consider voting on an independent racial bias audit of the
  Mount Pleasant Police Department. He stated that many people feel
  they are not policed the same way as others and encouraged the
  Council to work with the Charleston Area Justice Ministry on this
  issue.

                                                               Page 5 of 42
Blake Hill spoke about his plans to annex his property into Mount
Pleasant to develop an upscale campground. He explained that he
and his wife had spent the past year traveling the country visiting over
40 campgrounds and wanted to bring a boutique, higher-end feel to
Mount Pleasant beyond the existing KOA campground.
Mike Van Horn requested that Council consider returning to three
readings for ordinances instead of two to give residents more time to
understand what is being voted on. He also expressed concern about
the Mayor's public response to what he called the Mount Pleasant
police cheating scandal, taking issue with comments that suggested
the officers involved were still worthy of being police officers.
David Spedden thanked Council Member Whitaker for being a judge
at his neighborhood's chili cook-off and the Mount Pleasant Fire
Department for promoting fire safety at the event. He also
commended the town's communications regarding Highway 41 and
inquired about the Council's plan to communicate their priorities for
the 2026 transportation sales tax referendum to the community.
Jamie McKee expressed concerns about artificial turf, citing research
on health risks including increased injuries for children, heat
absorption issues, and chemical runoff into groundwater. He urged
caution in selecting fields for Rifle Range Park, comparing artificial
turf to forever chemicals that initially seemed beneficial but later
proved harmful.
Robert Adams opposed proposed amendments to building area ratio
standards, arguing that while he sympathized with the desire to
prohibit overdevelopment, the proposals went too far. He questioned
whether the amendments would affect neighborhoods not shown in
the presentation and argued that the town should not adopt such an
ordinance without broad support from the affected areas.
Denise Fugo, Executive Director of Housing for All Mount Pleasant,
raised concerns about parking spots being removed from Gregory
Ferry, Mount Pleasant's only affordable housing project, due to the
expansion of Highway 41. She noted that most residents use their
garages for storage and requested to be included in discussions with
the county to address the issue. She also mentioned that according
to Zillow and Redfin, the average home value in Mount Pleasant is


                                                             Page 6 of 42
  now $950,000, making it difficult for town employees to afford
  housing.
  Perry Rourk defended artificial turf, noting it has been in use for
  nearly 60 years and extensively studied by agencies including the
  CDC and EPA with no proven adverse health effects. The speaker
  highlighted that natural grass fields require regular applications of
  fertilizers, herbicides, pesticides, and fungicides that can run off into
  local waterways. They also pointed out the cost benefits, stating that
  maintaining a natural grass athletic field can cost upwards of $50,000
  per year, while artificial turf fields can handle up to four times more
  use than natural grass.
