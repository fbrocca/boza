1. Call to Order
Mayor Will Haynie called the Special Town Council Meeting to order on
Monday, April 6, 2026 at 12:41 p.m. Council Members Brownstein,
Whitaker, Hyatt, Chapman, Rambo, Tinkey, Crosby and Iacofano were
all present. The Mayor noted that Council Members had been engaged
in departmental budget reviews throughout the day in addition to the
regular business items on the agenda.

2. Public Comments
Mayor Haynie opened the floor for public comment, requesting that
speakers provide their name and address and observe the two-and-a-
half-minute time limit.
Bob Adams stated that he had previously spoken in favor of the
wetlands ordinance at the March 10 Town Council meeting. He returned
to identify what he considered a drafting deficiency in the current
language. He explained that the Supreme Court's decision in Sackett v.
EPA limits the Clean Water Act to wetlands with a continuous surface
connection to federally regulated water bodies, and that the proposed
ordinance in Section 155.054(B)1 intends to cover areas not protected
by that Act. However, he argued that the draft's references to "vernal
pools and seasonal wet surface depressions" are limited to temporary
bodies of water, leaving permanent wetlands unprotected and potentially
subject to fill and development. He recommended amending Sections
(B)1 and (B)3 to replace the existing phrases with broader language,
such as "wetlands and other bodies of water fed by water sources not
protected by the Clean Water Act" and "buffer to wetlands and other
bodies of water defined in (B)1," respectively.

Lisa Schnipke expressed support for the ordinance, noting that wetland

Staff Presentation
Kevin Mitchell presented an overview of the ordinance and its
implementation tools. He noted that the ordinance applies to non-
jurisdictional, isolated wetlands — those not covered under Army Corps
of Engineers regulations — and that determinations of jurisdictional
status would require a wetlands professional with a minimum of five
years of field experience using the 1987 Army Corps Federal Manual
and the appropriate regional data sheet. The ordinance would apply to
all subdivisions and single-family activity. A buffer averaging 10 feet,
                                                      Special Town Council Meeting
                                                                      April 6, 2026
                                                                     Page 5 of 22

with a minimum of 5 feet and a maximum of 20 feet, would be required
around isolated wetlands, with the Planning Commission having
authority to increase the buffer width when deemed necessary for larger
or more impactful developments.
Regarding exemptions, Mr. Mitchell described the three-part criteria that
would exempt a property from requiring a wetlands professional: (1) the
owner submits an affidavit affirming no known knowledge of isolated
wetlands or previous delineations on their property; (2) the town's GIS
mapping tool shows no indication of suspected wetlands; and (3) the
proposal does not increase density on the property. He noted this
framework was modeled closely on Bluffton's ordinance.
Environmental Manager Maeve Gimbert presented the GIS mapping tool
used to identify potential isolated wetland areas. The tool layers three
data sources: the National Wetland Inventory (US Fish and Wildlife,
2011), hydric soil data (USDA National Resources Conservation
Service), and a sink/depression layer developed by the town's GIS team
using 2025 topography data. Ms. Gimbert explained that hydric soil and
depressional sinks represent two of the three primary wetland indicators
under the Army Corps manual, with the third — hydrophilic vegetation —
not yet available as a data layer. Stormwater ponds were removed from
the tool to reduce confusion, as they are not classified as wetlands. The
combined layer covers just under nine percent of the town. She noted
that the tool is used as an internal staff tool to flag properties for closer
review, not as a hard determination of wetland presence.

Several Council Members asked clarifying questions about the practical
application of the tool. Mr. Mitchell confirmed that staff would exercise
judgment — including field visits — before requiring a property owner to
hire a wetlands professional, and that a blue dot on the map alone would
not automatically trigger that requirement. He confirmed that interior
renovations not involving land disturbance would not be subject to the
ordinance. Council Member Brownstein asked about the cost of a
wetland delineation, and Mr. Mitchell estimated a range of approximately
$1,500 to $2,000 for an initial site visit and data collection.
Mr. Mitchell also presented a slide clarifying the roles and
responsibilities under the ordinance: the Planning Commission reviews
and decides on subdivision requests and holds authority to increase
buffer widths; property owners are responsible for disclosing historical
knowledge, employing wetlands professionals (consistent with existing
practices for stormwater and tree removal compliance), and ensuring
long-term protection of isolated wetlands; and staff administers permits,
uses the GIS tool, ensures design compliance, and investigates
unpermitted impacts.
Council Member Iacofano raised concerns about the visual complexity of
the GIS map, particularly regarding random or isolated data points that
may appear on residential properties, and expressed a preference for
clearer, more black-and-white guidance for residents and staff. Ms.
Gimbert clarified that data points appearing on roads or under structures
are likely the result of the parent soil material or a closed topographic

Amendment 1 — Strike Section D.2 Except for Critical
Infrastructure and Emergency Activity
Council Member Mike Tinkey introduced the first amendment, citing the
importance of the town abiding by the same rules it imposes on
residents. He proposed amending Section D.2, which provides an
exception allowing the Director of Engineering and Development
Services to authorize alterations serving an overriding public interest —
including parks, recreation facilities, capital improvement projects, critical
infrastructure, and emergency activities. His amendment would strike the
broader language while retaining the provisions for critical infrastructure
and emergency activity and would remove the single-director authority in
favor of routing such requests through the normal Planning Commission
review process.
Council Member Brownstein asked what the normal process would be
for a project that is no longer exempt. Mr. Mitchell explained that the
Planning Commission reviews subdivision requests and could also
review wetland impact applications from property owners or the town,
similar to a model used by Bluffton. He indicated he had a draft slide
outlining an alternate procedural pathway, which he briefly shared:
applicants would submit a wetland impact application, which would go
on public notice and be reviewed and decided upon by the Planning
Commission.

Council Member Rambo expressed concern about vesting a significant
amount of authority in an unelected body, particularly with respect to
council-approved projects. He raised the scenario of a council-approved
capital project being denied or substantially altered at the Planning
Commission level, noting the potential for increased costs and outcomes
contrary to Council's intent. Legal Counsel Pagliarini acknowledged that
such a scenario could create a situation in which the town is defending
or prosecuting something adverse to Council's wishes.
Town Administrator Eric DeMoura confirmed that Gary Santos Park,
currently under construction, would not be affected by the ordinance,
and that most foreseeable future town projects involve roads, existing
stormwater infrastructure, or already-built facilities that would be
exempted or unaffected.
The amendment was put to a roll call vote.
    Council Member Tinkey moved to amend the ordinance to strike
    Section D.2, retaining only the provisions for critical infrastructure
    and emergency activity, and removing the single-director authority in
    favor of the Planning Commission review process. Council Member
    Chapman seconded the motion. The motion carried 5–4, with
    Council Members Chapman, Crosby, Hyatt, Tinkey, and Mayor
    Haynie voting in favor, and Council Members Iacofano, Rambo,
    Whitaker, and Brownstein voting in opposition.
Amendment 2 — Remove "Or Adjacent To" from Buffer Language
Council Member Brownstein proposed removing the phrase "or adjacent
to" from Section C.3.b., which prohibits the introduction of invasive
species or non-native landscaping "within or adjacent to" the buffer or
wetlands. He expressed concern that as written, the phrase could be
interpreted as regulating landscaping anywhere on a property, since
essentially all areas are "adjacent to" something. Mr. Mitchell confirmed
that enforcement intent was not to be heavy-handed in this regard, but
Council Member Brownstein suggested that removing the phrase would
simplify and clarify the language. Council Member Hyatt noted the
original purpose was to protect the wetland from nearby invasive
plantings, such as kudzu, that could encroach into it.
    Council Member Brownstein moved to amend the buffer section of
    the ordinance to remove the phrase "or adjacent to" from Section
    C.3.b. Council Member Iacofano seconded the motion. The motion

Amendment 3 — Exempt Existing Single-Family Homes (Not
Adding Density)
Council Member Brownstein moved to exempt existing single-family
homes not adding density from the ordinance, expressing the view that
the ordinance should first be implemented and tested on subdivisions
and commercial projects before being extended to existing residential
properties. He stated that homeowners with single-family residences do
not have the same flexibility as developers to redesign or relocate
improvements, and that lessons learned from initial implementation
should inform any expansion of the ordinance's scope.
Council Member Hyatt opposed the amendment, stating that the
purpose of the ordinance is to protect wetlands regardless of where they
are located, and that no exceptions should be made. Council Member
Tinkey agreed, emphasizing that wetland loss is cumulative and that
incremental losses — one at a time — add up to significant damage over
time. Mayor Haynie stated that the vast majority of Mount Pleasant
citizens are asking for more sensitivity about where and how
development occurs, and that he would not support an amendment that
allows existing single-family homeowners to damage wetlands affecting
neighboring properties.
Council Member Iacofano, who had seconded the motion for discussion
purposes, ultimately voted against it, citing concern about the GIS map's
blue dots appearing on properties that may not have obvious wetlands.
    Council Member Brownstein moved to amend the ordinance to
    exempt existing single-family homes not adding density from the
    ordinance's requirements. Council Member Iacofano seconded the
    motion for discussion purposes. The motion failed 2–7. Council
    Members Brownstein and Chapman voted in favor with Council
    Members Crosby, Hyatt, Iacofano, Rambo, Tinkey, Whitaker and
    Mayor Haynie voting in opposition.
