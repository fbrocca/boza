VI.   PUBLIC COMMENT
          Mike Tinkey, 208 Venning Street stated he wanted to thank the
      members of Council, Chief Arnold and Town Staff for conducting
      the research and gathering the facts. He stated he recommends the
      maximum decibel level be 50 for nighttime with the nighttime hours
      being from 8:00 a.m. to 9:00 p.m. [sic]. He said the current
ordinance has the daytime hours ending at 9:00, and very few
complaints are happening. He said he recommends that lawn care
equipment and agricultural field equipment as well as delivery and
service trucks are servicing from 8 to 6, no nighttime hours. He
said the same for construction as it was not specifically mentioned
in the ordinance, but it should be 8 to 6, Monday through Friday and
no construction during nighttime hours, or Saturday and Sunday.
He agrees with the $500 fine for violations but think it should be
escalating, and he said it should be reconciled with other codes as
there are some conflicts.




    Gayle Smither, 419 Bayview Drive stated he appreciates that
the Committee has put together a very good plan and pilot
ordinance, especially where the ordinance addresses rhythmic
bass that can be heard. He said he also likes that it has specific
prohibitions on amplified sound heard in any residence. He is
concerned with the decibel level of 60 as it could be considered still
reasonable at 65 or 70. He asked that the evening hours be
changed to 7:00 p.m. Sunday through Thursday, and 9:00 p.m.
Friday and Saturday. He said in the new ordinance, it defines
where there is a specific prohibition for building noise after 9:00
p.m.
    Jacob Robertson, 1313 Shrimp Boat Lane stated he is the bar
manager at Vickerys. He stated he liked the fact that there has
been a time inserted as Monday afternoon cannot be the same
level and time as a Saturday night. He does not think that 10:30 is
too late on Friday and Saturday nights. He said he took a level of
64 the other day during a childs birthday party. He said he does not
expect to sit in his house in silence and not hear anything from
anyone all the time.
     Caroline LeSesne, 310 Bennett Street stated her house is
directly behind the Pitt Street Pharmacy and she owns the alley that
is directly behind the Post House restaurant. She said when she
sits on her porch she can almost hear every word the patrons sitting
in the restaurant having conversations are saying. She said she
has used the decibel reader at the property line, and it is about 55
decibels, and sometimes 60 if someone is laughing. She said she
supports 55 decibels or less, and she is comfortable with the hours
in the ordinance.
    Shannon Carter, 334 Hook Lane shared a video recording of
music coming from a venue that backs up to his property line and
plays live music every week. He said he appreciates the work done
so far on the noise ordinance but feels as if some additional
improvements can be made such as striking indoor conversation
levels. He stated with respect to items E2 and E4 that cover radio
and stereo and musical devices and amplified sound, most
municipalities have much more restrictive provisions, addressing
the impact across real property, not just inside of dwellings. He
said with respect to fines, a fine of $500 could possibly be viewed
as the cost of doing business, and he proposes that continued
violation every 15 minutes should be a separate fine and possibly
adding a nuisance provision.
Jamey Bradham, 338 Hook Lane stated Mr. Carter is his neighbor
and the video he played was his backyard and that is how it sounds
every Sunday, and sometimes Thursday and Saturday nights. He
appreciates the work that has been done to get to this point, but he
as some specific issues he would like addressed. Mr. Bradham
stated in Section 8, plainly audible and normal conversation is
ambiguous. He would like to see that stricken. Section C, the
daytime hours being defined as 8:00 a.m. to 10:30 p.m., he would
like to see the time reduced to 9:00 p.m., and under Section J, he
would like to see language added to address repeat offenders.
     Mack Cross, 1496 Goblet Avenue stated he is the applicant for
the Central Mount Pleasant Planned Development amendment. He
stated the ordinance as currently drafted prohibits a direct access
from the subject property to Hungryneck Boulevard, and he is
requesting for that to be amended to either remove it altogether or
allow a right-in, right-out access from the property onto Hungryneck
Boulevard. He said earlier today he sent out the proposed location
for the right-in, right-out curb cut which would align with the hotel
curb cut directly across the street. He said it would be centered on
the existing raised concrete median that runs down the middle of
Hungryneck Boulevard. Mr. Cross said he appreciated Councils
consideration and is available for questions.
    Tomi Dinkins, 1612 Florencia said in the past there were many
concerns with the Central Mount Pleasant project. She stated the
developer, Mack Cross, has demonstrated a willingness to try to
coexist harmoniously. She stated the changes that Mr. Cross is
requesting are a result of direct communication, compromise and
effort. She said while many residents had different wish lists, two of
the main concerns were parking and the high commercial traffic on
the residential streets. Ms. Dinkins said the parking concerns have
been alleviated and the suggested road changes have been a great
improvement. She said with a lot of communication, open-
mindedness and general good humanity on Mr. Crosss part, a way
has been found for the residents to have quality of life and Gather
Mount Pleasant to be profitable and a great improvement for the
Town. Ms. Dinkins said the residents support the revisions and ask
that Council do the same.
     Chris Colman, 118 Pitt Street stated he would like to thank
everyone for the work done on this ordinance. He said there are a
few changes that need to be made. He suggested the Town adopt
a standard that says that leaf blowers should be average or better
than average in terms of noise rating. Mr. Colman said that lawn
care equipment is exempt from the ordinance if operated during
daylight hours which is either 6:00 a.m. until 9:00 p.m. during the
summer or 8:00 a.m. until 10:30 p.m. He suggested a more
restrictive standard. Mr. Colman said a provision also needs to be
made with respect to construction noise.
     Nick Mead, 1006 Planters Curve thanked everyone for their
hard work. He said it would be disappointing for the Towns
restaurants and local musicians if this ordinance passes as
currently written. He said here tonight at the reception prior to the
meeting, the music was over 60 decibels from outside of the
building. He said local bars and restaurants will be affected by this
decision. He said a more thorough study needs to be conducted.
He said we should be able to find a way for local places to have live
music but also for their neighbors to have livability. He says for all
of the public speakers this evening, all but one have been over 60
decibels.
     Pam Gabriel, 860 Springwood Circle stated she is here to ask
for a Mount Pleasant museum. She said she has traveled the state
and every little town seems to have one. Ms. Gabriel said the Town
has such a wealth of history in this area, and the new residents who
have moved in have no idea how special the place is that they now
call home. She said history is being lost.
    Pearl Ascue, 977 Gadsenville Road, Awendaw said regarding
the growth in the upper part of Mount Pleasant in the 10 Mile
community, they have been fortunate to have a turn lane on
Gadsdenville Road, and also Bee Hive. She said the Bees Landing
project also consumes Gadsdenville Road as well as they come out
from the Bees Landing project onto Theodore Road. She said a
turn lane is needed there as the traffic from the recently opened
service station across the road is rapidly increasing. She said there
have been incidents there, and some of the area on Theodore
Road is in the Town. She is asking for a traffic light and turn lane to
be considered in that area. Ms. Ascue said there have been
instances where developers have come into the 10 Mile community
and made promises to help the community and they have not been
kept, which is devastating. She said the community center is in
desperate need.
    George Freeman, 1450 Bowman Road stated he is following
up on what Ms. Ascue just stated. He said they have discovered
that promises broken by developers are not just happening at 10
Mile. He said he understands that is not a responsibility of the
Town, but one suggestion they have is that possibly if an
agreement is made and a developer makes a promise to do
something, it can be recorded so there is a paper trail within the
Town.
    Angie Anderson, 396 Tidal Reef Circle stated she is here
regarding the 526 Longpoint interchange. She said she and her
neighbors are asking for the Towns help navigating the procedures.
Ms. Anderson said she has been attending the meetings since
2018 and at the meeting on August 2, they brought two options to
       the public that were favored. They are a raised semi by-pass that
       would end in front of Grassy Creek. She said it is visually
       disturbing, but what is worse is that the presenters could not talk
       about the impact that these options would bring to the
       neighborhood. She said there are traffic safety issues and
       increased noise which would impact the neighborhood. She said
       there are a lot of questions that need to be answered, and impact
       studies done before an option is chosen.
            Jenna Stephens, 302 River Oak Drive said she is here tonight
       to ask for assistance on mitigating the potential devastating affects
       to individuals in our community. She said she supports reducing
       the traffic on Longpoint as the impact the trucks have on
       businesses and families that live there is devastating. She said
       creating a port access road and moving traffic to the other side of
       526 is not the answer, it is just creating another problem. She said
       the port access road will directly impact several neighborhoods and
       at least two homes, it could have other devastating affects to the
       rest of the homes in these communities. She said the port access
       road would put trucks physically closer to Grassy Creek and Tidal
       walk, and the reduced distance poses risks on various levels, but
       especially the children who often play, bike, run and walk in those
       neighborhoods along Seacoast Parkway and in the woods. She
       said the fumes from the trucks would result in a significant increase
       in exposure to pollutants and potential health problems. She said
       this is an important community issue.
            Paul Trouche, 320 Bennett Street thanked everyone for their
       hard work and said the proposed noise ordinance is a great
       improvement. He stated the extension of the daylight hours from
       9:00 p.m. to 10:30 p.m. seems a bit late, and asked that it be
       returned to 9:00 p.m. He also said the 60 decibel level is a whole
       lot more than 50 decibels. He does think this is a positive step and
       appreciates the efforts.
