VI.          PUBLIC COMMENT
            Troy Hopkins, 1288 Old Colony Road, stated he owns a tree
      service and finds many landscaping and tree companies are putting
                                                                      Page 3 of 11
trees on the street for local pick-up. He says according to the Town
Code, contractors are supposed to haul these items off at their own
expense. He says it makes it harder to compete when they do not have
to pay the extra expense of removal. Mr. Hopkins said certain species
of trees require permits for removal, and he is losing business to
companies who are not obtaining permits. He asked for there to be
more enforcement for those who are not following the rules on both
issues.
      Gary Davis, 849 Bulls Bay Boulevard, stated he hopes Council
will give serious consideration to leasing the space at East Cooper
Plaza as an arts and community center, rather than the smaller building
being considered.
      Marie-Louise Moreto, 1133 Liberty Court stated she wanted to
thank and commend Town Council members for realizing the need for
a community arts center and trying to make it happen. She said there
is a lot of confusion, misunderstanding and misinformation going
around and she asked Council to make an arts center a reality by
gathering input from the artistic community, the business community,
the residents and officials. She asked that the information be accurate
and have public input before coming to a decision.
       Carol Hallman, 2352 Parsonage Woods Lane stated she would
like to commend Council for their attention to the problem of lack of
facilities and support for arts organizations. The problem that many
groups run into is that if they can find a space to rehearse, such as a
school or church, their rehearsal times and dates may be bumped for
a church or school function which would have priority. She asked that
Council evaluate the needs beyond the performance space that will
have to go into any kind of useable community arts center so that one
group will not monopolize the space.
     Pam Hrisik, 945 Pine Hollow Road stated she is in support of an
arts center and is in favor of the larger space being considered.
     Abby Hrisik, 945 Pine Hollow Road stated she is in support of an
arts center with a performance space.
      Mark Chestnut, 155 Wingo Way stated he is in support of a
performing arts venue. He said a large, flexible space is needed.



                                                             Page 4 of 11
            William Hamilton, 32 Sowell stated the location near Aldi is the
       one that make sense for an arts center. He said there are good transit
       stops and businesses nearby for people to spend their money.
             Katie Zimmerman, Executive Director of Charleston Moves
       stated she is here in support of the Target Zero resolution being
       considered later this evening.
            Thomas Donaldson, 226 Molasses Lane stated he was there to
       speak in opposition to the proposed changes of short-term rental
       ordinance changes, and specifically in opposition of the minimum night
       requirement in order to keep a permit.
           Mike Tinkey, 208 Venning Street stated he is encouraging Council
       to vote yes on the recommendations of the March 6 Police, Judicial
       and Legal Committee meeting. He thanked Council for working

             Josh Dix, 5006 Wetland Crossing stated he is the Government
       Affairs Director for the Charleston Trident Association of Realtors and
       he is speaking in opposition of final reading of the multi-family
       moratorium.
             Chris Colman,118 Pitt Street stated the plainly audible standard
       still has the dreaded above normal conversations standard, which
       renders it unenforceable. He said the receiving property must receive
       noise above the plainly audible standard and that needs to be
       corrected. He said bass standard needs to be addressed and
       regulated, as well as averaging provisions. He said lawn equipment
       and the regulation of construction equipment needs to be addressed
       as well.
           Kyle Downes, 1481 Center Street Extension stated he is there to
       advocate for a theater and multi-use community arts space.

         Mr. Iacofano amended his motion to say six of nine must

     Ms. Whitley asked if Mr. Iacofano would separate his motion

B.        Old Business
     Final Reading: An Ordinance to repeal and replace Section
     132.03 titled Noise of the Mount Pleasant Code of Ordinances.
     (Ord. No. 22049)
          Mr. Ritchie moved for final reading, seconded by Ms.
     Whitley.
          Mr. Ritchie moved to amend the ordinance to include
     Fridays as part of the weekend, both for daytime and evening
     hours, the maximum allowable decibel levels as provided, a
     reasonable average of sustained noise as measured by our
     meters, the decibel level for base noise be consistent with the
     decibel levels of 60 during daytime and 55 during evenings as
     consistent with what is in the ordinance currently, and for
     business hours for lawncare equipment from 7:00 a.m. to 7:00
     p.m., seconded by Ms. Whitley.
     After further discussion, Mayor Haynie called for the vote on the
     amendment and all present voted in favor. The motion carried 8-
     0.
         Mayor Haynie called for a vote on the amended ordinance
     and all present voted in favor. The motion carried 8-0.
     This Ordinance SIGNED, SEALED and DELIVERED this date.
