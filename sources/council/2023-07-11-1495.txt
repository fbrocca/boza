VI.       PUBLIC COMMENT
              Stuart Whiteside, Seamon Whiteside and Associates, 501
      Wando Park Boulevard stated he is representing the Charleston
      Harbor Resort and Marina for their Patriots Point Planned
      Development amendment request. He stated Council has worked very
      diligently to determine in the Comprehensive Plan which areas are
      suitable for hotels, of which Patriots Point was one area. He said the
      Charleston Harbor Resort and Marina has had an extremely large
      economic development impact on the Town for over twenty years,
      paying hospitality and accommodation taxes of over $1 Million a year
      and they pay fees to the Patriots Point Development Authority which
      help for the long-term maintenance of the Yorktown and other features
      which add up to $1.8 Million currently. He said there has been some
      confusion over the last several meetings over the history of the zoning
      on this site. He said the original zoning was approved in June of 1997,
      and has been amended several times. Mr. Whiteside said the March
      2000 amendment allowed for the original hotel as well as the following
      uses: 100,000 square feet of retail, 30,000 square feet of restaurants,
      50,000 square feet of entertainment, 25,000 square feet of office, 175
      residential units and the marina as well as another hotel and meeting
      spaces. He said they did participate in the widening of Patriots Point
      Road based on those proposed improvements. He stated the last PD
      amendment was in June of 2011, which was for the last 92 room hotel,
      but there was still room for development at that time. Mr. Whiteside
      said they are now looking to add 133 rooms to diversify their offerings
      on the site, and they will manage the new rooms on the existing hotel
      facilities. He said they have coordinated with staff and sufficient
      parking is available and Patriots Point Development Authority has
      indicated their support for the project. Mr. Whiteside said they are
      requesting Council’s approval for the amendment, and he is available
      to answer any questions.

Approval to establish Juneteenth and Veteran’s Day as Town
holidays
June 30, 2023 Police, Judicial & Legal Committee Minutes
Mr. Ritchie moved to establish Juneteenth and Veteran’s Day
as Town holidays, seconded by Mr. Chapman.
Mr. Rambo asked for confirmation that all staff would be off
except for police and fire and Town facilities would be closed.
Mr. DeMoura responded in the affirmative.
Mr. Ritchie said that was discussed and most of our
neighboring municipalities and jurisdictions already observe
those holidays and are closed which makes it difficult for our
Town staff to do work on those days.
Mr. Rambo said this topic came up last year at Mount Pleasant
Waterworks and there are a couple of different holidays that
meant things to different people, so what the Commission did
was, rather than establishing holidays and closing the facilities,
they established a floating holiday so each member could
choose a day that was important to them. He said he would be
more in favor of something like that and is not in favor of the
Town being closed for two additional days.
Mr. Ritchie said the Town does currently offer a personal
holiday, in addition to leave that is accrued. He said many
government entities recognize Veteran’s Day and Veterans are
the reason we are sitting here today with the freedoms we
have.
Ms. Corley said she agrees with Mr. Rambo and asked how
many holidays the Town recognizes. Mr. DeMoura stated not
including the personal day, there are 11.
Ms. Whitley said she thinks that recognizing these two days as
holidays is a wonderful thing, and should be done, but she does
not want to close the Town for two additional days. She would
be in favor of removing one of the existing holidays.
Mr. DeMoura said of all of the holidays we have, the least
traditional one that we observe is President’s Day.

       1.       First Reading: An ordinance to repeal and replace
            Chapter 73, titled Bicycles, of the Mount Pleasant Code of
            Ordinances. (Ord. No. 23023)
            Ms. Whitley moved for first reading, seconded by Mr. Ritchie.



                                                                       Page 8 of 10
     Mayor Haynie said the bicycle ordinance had not been updated
     since 1963, and we must be mindful of state laws. He also
     stated amendments cannot be made until final reading.
     Mr. Pagliarini stated there are definitions in the ordinance,
     which have been defined by the state legislature, as well as
     rules and regulations applicable to each. He said the way this
     will work is that e-bikes are not allowed on sidewalks, but are
     permitted on roadways with helmet and hand signaling
     requirements. He said the Police, Judicial and Legal
     Committee believe that children under the age of 12 and under
     adult supervision, children under 7 may ride bicycles on
     sidewalks. Mr. Pagliarini stated the Police, Judicial and Legal
     or any other committee may elect to discuss this further before
     final reading.
     Mr. Ritchie stated any time there are changes in laws, there is a
     robust education component before enforcement will take
     place.
     Mr. Iacofano clarified that a bicycle is allowed on a sidewalk on
     a road with speeds of 35 miles per hour or more, and not an e-
     bike. Mr. Pagliarini responded in the affirmative.
     Mr. Rambo said he had some concerns that this may be too
     complex. After expressing some of his concerns he stated he
     would submit them to the committee to be addressed. Mr.
     Rambo stated he would be voting no for this Ordinance in its
     present form.
         After further discussion, Mayor Haynie called for the vote.
     Mr. Rambo voted no with all others present voting in favor. The
     motion carried 8-1.
