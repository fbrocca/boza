    Howard Chapman – 824 Natchez Circle stated he is speaking
about an ordinance that was passed last month to identify where
hotels should go within the Town. He said he wanted to state that
the vote was not about any particular hotel or any individual who
might have wanted to build a hotel in Mount Pleasant. He stated
the individual builds wonderful hotels, but it was the wrong place,
and he hopes in the future the individual might look at some of the
places designated for a nice hotel. He reiterated the ordinance was
not about any hotel or individual, but about where the Town and the
Comprehensive Plan identified as appropriate locations.
    Richard Taylor – 1137 Louise Terrace stated he is in support
of the annexation and rezoning of the property in Louise Terrace.
He said their area is surrounded by commercial buildings, and the
homes in Louise Terrace are mostly rental homes. He said if he
thought this rezoning would hurt the neighborhood he would not
consider it, but he believes it will enhance the neighborhood and
make life a lot better for the neighbors in there with the green space
and park that will be provided and he hoped Council would vote in
favor of it.
     Russ Seamon – Seamon Whiteside, 501 Wando Park
Boulevard state he is the applicant for the Louise Terrace
townhomes. He said the Comprehensive Plan recommends future
land use of mixed neighborhood, which is what is being requested,
and the townhouse zoning is supported by the Comprehensive
Plan. He said this is a great opportunity to annex a donut hole into
the Town, it provides an opportunity to improve the drainage, and
an opportunity to provide a more attainable product for the Town
versus another large single family house. Mr. Seamon said this
mixed neighborhood recommendation is the right one when you
look at the context around it. Mr. Seamon said the project is two
townhouse buildings that face an existing apartment development,
and are about half the size of the apartments, which is a logical
stepdown from multi-family to townhouse to single family. He asked
that Council please support the project.
       Michael Harper – Gramling Brothers, 502 King Street,
Charleston stated he would like to make three quick points. The
first is that this project is only dealing with Louise Terrace, as the
site plan is completely on Louise Terrace facing the condo complex
across the street. He said the Louise Terrace properties they are
working on for some type of high density residential, everything
within the block for Louse Terrace, all but one property are six
percent taxpaying properties. He said they feel that is relevant. He
said the project will create a significant amount of green space, and

     Chad McPeters – 1147 Carter Avenue thanked Council for
their service. He said the Planning Committees [Commission] on
January 19th and 16th were presented a petition signed by twenty-
two residents of Louise Terrace that oppose the rezoning and
annexation of 1131 and 1137 Louise Terrace. He stated they are a
small neighborhood of 18 single family homes and an
overwhelming majority, twelve out of fifteen occupied homes, are
opposed to the rezoning to townhomes. He stated several
speakers spoke against the project, and at those meetings the
Commission members agreed that this development would have a
negative impact on the residents of Louise Terrace and the
surrounding neighborhoods. He said this development would be
twenty feet away from single-story, single- family homes, and it
would cast a shadow on all of those neighbors. He said they are a
growing community and they would like to grow on their own,
asking Council to please follow the existing zoning regulations.
    Danny Webb – 1175 Carter Avenue stated he is opposed to
the townhouses. He said one of the speakers mentioned the six
percent taxpayers. Mr. Webb stated he is an owner resident,
however has not filed for the homestead tax break, so you cannot
look at that information and tell which properties are owner-
occupied. Mr. Webb stated the drainage system in the
neighborhood would not accept the type of development that they
are proposing, and he does not think they will make it adequate if it
is approved. Mr. Webb said they are pitting neighbor against
neighbor. He said this is a small neighborhood and the
overwhelming majority of the people that live there and intend to
live there do not want the townhouses.
    Craig Cole, 1133 Carter Avenue stated he lives in his home
with his 87-year-old mother and they built their home themselves in
1986. He said when they first moved in, there was nothing behind
them but some old houses, chickens and a lake. He said then
Walmart was built, but it was behind them and they do not see it as
there is a privacy fence and a buffer. He said condominiums were
built on Louise Terrace but are set back from the street so it does
not bother them. He said they picked that place as they thought it
would remain a nice, small area. He said one of the trees that will
be taken out has an owl living in it, and when the townhouses are
built, he will not be able to see the moon rise. He said this project
will completely change the complexity of their small neighborhood.
He was also concerned with being able to afford to tie into the
sewer if the annexation was approved. He said he hopes the
neighborhood can remain as it is.
    Allison McAdams (did not give address) stated she is against
this rezoning as her house is next to where the project would be
and it would tower over her house, cutting her off from the
neighborhood. She said the people that live there are not asking
for much but want to have a home and a yard like the American
Dream. Ms. McAdams said that of the things about this project that
bothers her the most, other than the height and density, is that
twelve units would be there on three lots, and that would mean
twenty-four cars on one block, and the neighborhood is only two
blocks. She said asked Council to hold to the zoning as it is.
    Layla Luna, Founder of Just Bee thanked Council for the
proclamation. Ms. Luna said Just Bee was stated because she is
the mother of a of a thirteen-year-old non-verbal boy. She said they
were asked to leave a restaurant because he was having a
meltdown. She said one out of every forty-four children is
diagnosed with autism spectrum disorder, and Just Bees goal is to
teach businesses how to become sensory-friendly environments.
Ms. Luna said that most families that have autistic children or adults
do not leave home and spend money in their local communities as
they are afraid of how they will be disrespected or discriminated
against. She stated what everyone experienced during Covid-19 by
being isolated in their homes can be the life of autism. Ms. Luna
said she is here to spread the word and ask businesses to take the
pledge to learn how to become sensory-friendly environments. She
thanked Council and said that her son, being non-verbal, has no
voice. She said she is his voice and she is the voice for millions of
families that feel just like she does. She said she is grateful, but
this is not about changing the child to fit the environment, but about
changing the environment to fit the child.
     Perry Rourke – 2004 Brick Kiln Parkway asked where the

He said he understands there is a Recreation Committee

there could be up to five appointed to that committee. Mr. Rourke
said it would be great if Council would consider creating a
recreation advisory committee, something similar in structure to the

could collaborate with the heads of the Recreation Department. Mr.
Rourke said he was a little disappointed that there are still only two
minutes allowed for public comment. He brought it up last month
that up to three minutes is allowed and the Chair stated that a
motion could be made prior to the meeting. He said at the next
meeting he would love to see any member of Council make a

    Rev. Rob Dewey – 598 E. Hobcaw Drive stated he would like

    Ms. Whitley moved for final reading, seconded by Mr.
Iacofano.
Mr. Chapman said there is a plan for access into the property
from the rear, and that was part of the plan as it was
developed for the entire Carolina Park project. He stated the
developer planning this in their present location continues to
have vehicles parked in the buffer zone as late as this
weekend, and extensively. He would like to ask if it is
possible to have a clause for this amendment if they do the
same thing on this site where they are parking vehicles in the
buffer zone. He asked if it is possible to do this to force them
to keep the vehicles out of the buffer zone.
Ms. Reed said the reason this was not on the consent agenda
is because Mr. Chapman stated he would like to make some
amendments such as no parking in the buffer and no future
plans for a left turn where the median is located. She stated
because it is a planned development amendment, we could
attach those conditions, and asked Mr. Pagliarini to confirm.
Mr. Pagliarini stated that is correct.
    Mr. Chapman moved to add those two amendments to
make sure we do not get left turns in or out, and to require no
parking in the buffer zone, and if they do park there, that
entrance closes.
Mayor Haynie asked if no parking in the buffer zone is already
covered by an ordinance, and perhaps it is just an
enforcement issue.
Ms. Reed said if they violate this condition or this zoning
provision, she does not know if that would change anything in
the approved planned development. She said it would be a
code enforcement issue. Mr. Pagliarini stated the Town would
have the right to enforce within the context of the planned
development so he feels it is relevant if Mr. Chapman would
like to include it.
              Mr. Chapman moved to amend the ordinance so that in
          the future, no left turns would be made in or out of this
          development at location F, and second, in the event we have
          the same problem in the current location, that the direct
          access to 17 be closed.
          Mayor asked for clarification if Mr. Chapman was referring to
          the right turn in, and Mr. Chapman concurred.
          Mr. Chapman said enforcement had a very difficult time trying
          to get the vehicles out of the buffer area, and he is putting this
          in here because they worked for over two years to try and get
          the vehicles out of there. He said other dealerships were
          staying outside the buffer so there is no reason for this.
             Ms. Whitley seconded the motion.
          Mr. Pagliarini stated for clarification that we cannot close
          access for a violation.
          Mayor Haynie said that would affect the second part of Mr.
          Chapmans amendment and asked Mr. Chapman if he would
          like to strike that part.
              Mr. Chapman amended his motion to strike the closing of
          the access for violations. Ms. Whitley amended her second.
              After further discussion, Mayor Haynie called for the vote
          on the amendment and Mayor Haynie, Mr. Chapman, Ms.
          Hyatt, Mr. Ritchie and Ms. Whitley voted in favor with Ms.
          Corley, Mr. Santos, Mr. Rambo and Mr. Iacofano voting
