6. Public Comment
Several citizens addressed the council:
David Spedden spoke about the Charleston County traffic crisis,
particularly focusing on Highway 41. He criticized the "road to
compromise" plan and urged council to form a committee to build a
unified response against it, including Smart Growth 41, local HOAs, and
residents.
Mike Van Horn announced a candidate forum scheduled for October 16
from 6:30 to 9:00 PM at town hall. He also urged the council to redirect
budgeted funds to support the police force in light of a recent home
invasion.
Kevin Cunnane criticized Mayor Haynie regarding public safety staffing
levels for both police and fire departments, arguing that both
departments need better staffing, pay, and equipment but have not been
made a priority.
Curt Thomas introduced himself as a candidate for mayor. He expressed
concerns that citizens' voices weren't being heard on issues including
public safety, growth, and infrastructure, and emphasized the need to
restore trust in town leadership.
Ellen Moore questioned the council's approval process for the
$60,000,000 bond fund from Mount Pleasant Water Works, arguing that
there was insufficient public input before final approval.
Alex Skatell thanked the council and spoke about a recent armed home
invasion at his residence. He acknowledged the leadership of the city,
especially Town Administrator Eric DeMoura and Police Chief Arnold, in
responding to the incident. Mr. Skatell emphasized that while the
incident was frightening, his family would not live in fear and expressed
confidence that the perpetrator would be brought to justice.
Dana Coleman from the 10 Mile community spoke against annexing
properties located in the 10 Mile Historic District, arguing that annexation
would weaken the historic district and allow investors and developers to
avoid the Charleston County historic district process.
Paul Franklin spoke on behalf of the Mount Pleasant Exchange Club, a
community service organization that focuses on preventing child abuse,
Americanism, youth academic scholarships, and community service. He
invited everyone to attend their new member open house on September
18.
Larry Kobrovsky, representing East Cooper on the Charleston County
Council, spoke in his personal capacity to thank the council for their
leadership on Highway 41 and apologized to Mayor Haynie and Council
Member Iacofano for how they were treated at a county council meeting.
He also voiced opposition to the proposed annexations, arguing they
would change protections for historic settlement communities.
Waring Howe discussed the upcoming elections, emphasizing the
importance of vetting candidates through forums and questioning a
candidate who owns vaping businesses despite the town having an
ordinance banning vaping in most places.
Craig Ascue from 10 Mile spoke about preserving the Gullah Geechee
community and asked council to consider the impact of zoning changes
on preserving cultural heritage.
Jeremy Manchester identified himself as the person seeking annexation
of his property into Mount Pleasant. He clarified that he was willing to
accept more restrictive zoning than what was being characterized by
some speakers, stating he just wanted to be part of Mount Pleasant and
receive town services.

7. Consent Agenda Items
A. Final Reading: An Ordinance providing for the issuance and sale
of not exceeding $60,000,000 Town of Mount Pleasant, South
Carolina, Waterworks and Sewer System Revenue Bonds, Series
2025, and other matters relating thereto. (Ord. No. 25045) (Ord. No.
25045 FINAL)
August 4, 2025 Finance Committee Minutes
August 4, 2025 Water Supply & Resources Minutes
Mayor Haynie noted that representatives from Water Works were
present, including the general manager, chief financial officer, and legal
counsel, to answer any questions.
Council Member Iacofano asked about the purpose of bringing the bond
to council, current water rate comparisons to other municipalities, and
plans for the fleet maintenance facility. Mount Pleasant Waterworks
General Manager Allan Clum explained that by state law, town council
must approve bond issues, and that Mount Pleasant water rates fall in
the middle compared to regional peers. He noted that approximately
$30,000 per year would be saved by constructing the new maintenance
facility.
Mayor Haynie asked about the bond rating. The CFO, Mark Coffin,
reported that Mount Pleasant Water Works had been upgraded to a AAA
bond rating, making it one of the smallest utilities in the nation with such
a rating and only the fourth in South Carolina to achieve this status.
Council Member Tinkey asked about the benefits of having a deep well.
Mr. Clum explained that the new deep well would provide a source of
drinking water for the next hundred years, with Mount Pleasant being the
only entity with access to that aquifer. He noted that permits for their
current aquifer had been reduced by 43 percent, creating concern for
future water supply.
Council Member Brownstein asked about assistance programs for
residents with fixed incomes and how personnel costs were being
controlled. Mr. Clum mentioned they have a customer care fund to assist
those who can't afford their bills and stated that staff growth has been
approximately 2 percent per year.
A motion was made to approve the final reading of Ordinance No. 25045
regarding the issuance and sale of not exceeding $60,000,000 Town of
Mount Pleasant, South Carolina, Waterworks and Sewer System
Revenue Bonds by Council Member Chapman. The motion was
seconded by Council Member Santos and passed unanimously.
This Ordinance SIGNED, SEALED and DELIVERED this date.

Council Member Whitley reported that the Planning Committee was split
2-2 on both items and could not come to an agreement on
recommendations.
Ms. Reed explained the Planning Commission's recommendations. For
the Old Village Historic District, they proposed returning to the previous
20-foot height limit (down from 25 feet) and adding a 10-foot setback for
side and rear. For the rest of the town, they recommended keeping the
25-foot height limit but requiring a 10-foot side and rear setback for any
portion over 18 feet. They also proposed adding a 40 percent maximum
size relative to the principal structure, in addition to the existing 850
square foot maximum.
Council Member Chapman made a motion to approve the Planning
Commission's recommendations with the addition that in the
Neighborhood Preservation Overlay District and the rest of the town,
new construction could not be higher than the existing property height.
For example, if the existing house was 20 feet tall, an accessory
structure could only go to 20 feet.
Discussion followed about whether this restriction would encourage
people to tear down ranch homes to build larger structures. Council
Member Whitley argued that the town had just spent two years revising
the zoning code, which was passed in May, and changing it now would
be disruptive. She also expressed concern that the proposed setbacks
would prevent some property owners from building ADUs at all.
Council Member Brownstein agreed that limiting accessory structures to
the height of the home could have unintended consequences, potentially
encouraging demolition of existing homes. He also noted that ADUs
often serve important purposes for extended families, particularly aging
parents.
Council Member Chapman clarified that his proposal would not require
tearing down ranch homes, as homeowners could add on to existing
structures or add a story to them.
Council Member Chapman made a motion to approve what the Planning
Commission recommended with the exception that in the Neighborhood
Preservation Overlay District and the rest of the town, new construction
would not be higher than the existing property height. The motion was
seconded by Council Member Santos and passed with a 5-3 vote, with
Council Members Brownstein, Iacofano and Whitley voting opposed.

Ms. Reed explained Building Area Ratio (BAR), which is currently
applied in the Old Village Historic District and controls the intensity,
mass, and scale of buildings. The Planning Commission recommended
expanding BAR to the Old Mount Pleasant area.
BAR limits the total building area to 50 percent of the lot size, with a
minimum allowable size of 2,500 square feet for small lots and a
maximum of 5,500 square feet regardless of lot size. Ms. Hyatt provided
examples of how this would work and showed a real example of a
project under construction in Old Mount Pleasant that exceeds the 50
percent BAR and would have needed to reduce its footprint by about
540 square feet if BAR were applied.
Council Member Whitley expressed concern about the financial impact
on property owners, calculating that a reduction of 542 square feet could
represent a $260,000 loss in property value based on current real estate
prices.
Council Member Tinkey suggested looking at lot sizes and typical
structures in Old Mount Pleasant to determine if the BAR percentages
should be adjusted from those used in the Old Village.
Council Member Iacofano questioned why only Old Mount Pleasant was
being targeted when neighborhoods like Cooper Estates had similar
character. Ms. Hyatt explained that the lots are larger in those other
neighborhoods, so large houses have less impact on neighbors.
A motion was made by Council Member Brownstein to approve the
Building Area Ratio concept for Old Mount Pleasant. The motion was
seconded by Council Member Hyatt and passed by a 6-2 vote with
Council Members Whitley and Iacofano voting opposed.
