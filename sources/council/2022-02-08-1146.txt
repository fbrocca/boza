      and the concern has been that at certain locations at Town
      businesses are starting to charge for parking, so the question
      becomes if the business is charging for parking, does that
      parking still count toward their required parking. She asked
      what we can do to either encourage businesses to not charge
      for parking or if they do, then should we require that they be
      reimbursed if they are a customer of that business. Ms.
      Whitley said the Committee wanted to bring it to full Council to
      get feedback on parking as there have been complaints from
      citizens about paying $20 for parking, difficulty with the
      parking apps, getting the parking system to work, worrying
      about getting ticketed and towed.


      Mr. Iacofano said his understanding is the issue is the parking
      at Waters Edge, which customers should be reimbursed. He
      said he does not believe they are trying to charge for parking
      in their spaces, but simply trying to reserve them for their
      customers, as they are being reimbursed when they go in.
      Mr. Iacofano said he believes that is a right as a business
      owner, to try to preserve the parking spaces. He said as far
      as complaints, he has not heard anyone make public
      comments nor has he seen one e-mail about it. He said he
      does not think it is out of control right now.


      Mr. Santos said he had an issue with the parking app but
      when he went inside they helped him with it. He said one of
      the things that bothered him is that people are parking at
Shem Creek Park, so that the residents that want to go down
to the boardwalk do not have a place to park. He said if
people park by the restaurant, they risk getting towed
because they do not go inside and eat. Mr. Santos said if the
park is open to the residents, there should not be restaurant
workers or people going to eat in the restaurant parking at the
park area. He said something needs to be done to keep that
area open for the people that want to enjoy the park.


Mr. Chapman said he and family members have had issues
with the parking app as well. He said they do not have the
spaces they are supposed to have for their customers
immediately going in and it is driving away business. He said
there are several other businesses there that have put this
program in place and anyone can park there and pay the $20
and go on a raft or paddleboard. He said you do not have to
go to the restaurants, you can pay to park and go anywhere.
Mr. Chapman said the spaces the restaurant is supposed to
have available for their customers by ordinance are not
always available and that is the problem.


Ms. Corley said the restaurants have the spaces, but other
people are using them for other purposes, and how are we
going to ask the business owners to monitor the parking. She
said that is an undue burden and maybe they need to
significantly increase the parking fee to make sure the
customers are going in to eat and getting the ticket validated.
She said those restaurant owners would much rather have
four people sitting at a table than to get $20 for parking.


Mr. Rambo stated he understands Mr. Santos point about
public parking, but what this ordinance is talking about is
private property and private businesses. If someone does not
want to deal with the parking app, then they can go to a
restaurant that does not have that, which are the vast majority
of restaurants in town. This is an issue in one area, and the
citizen has a right to not visit the business if they do not like
the way they do business.


Ms. Hyatt said we have ordinances for a reason and we need
to enforce the ordinances, so the question is, does this
prevent the ordinance of required parking from being
enforced. She asked if there is anything in the ordinance that
states the required parking must be free. Mr. Pagliarini stated
there is no current language related to charging for parking.
Ms. Hyatt said it mentioned that monitoring the parking lot
would be a burden, but they would still have to monitor the
parking anyway. She said she feels they should be marking
the spaces and letting the public know that the spaces are
only for their business.


Mr. Ritchie said we have ordinances about requirements for
parking and he does not think that changes. They still have
the spaces for the restaurant, but it is up to the restaurant to
monitor who is parking there. He said he thinks in the spirit of
the ordinance they still have the spaces for the restaurant.


Ms. Whitley said it seems as if there are those on Council who
feel this is not an issue, and then there are those that feel it is
a violation of our ordinances. She asked if Council wanted to
regulate a business so closely when it comes to their parking.


Mr. Iacofano said he likes the idea of marking the parking
spaces for the restaurant, but he thinks Waters Edge is trying
to do their best to keep their parking for their customers as it
is free parking if they dine in the restaurant. He said at a time
when it is very difficult to find employees, it would probably be
difficult to put an employee out there to monitor the parking
lot.
