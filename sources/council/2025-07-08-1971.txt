4. Public Hearings, Awards, & Presentations
   A. A Public Hearing to receive comments on a 5-Year update of
      the 2019-2029 Comprehensive Plan
Staff noted that this was the first public hearing and potentially first
reading for the 5-year update to the 2019-2029 Comprehensive Plan.
The plan was adopted in 2020, and after conducting public input
meetings and taking it to the Planning Commission, it was presented
with a recommendation for approval.
Public comments were received from:
  ● George Freeman, President of the Community Action Group for
      Encouragement (CAGE), requested removal of Rifle Range Road
      from the Mount Pleasant Way in the comprehensive plan. He
      stated that the existing sidewalk is sufficient for pedestrians and
     cyclists currently using it, and a wider sidewalk would only
     encourage illegal use by electric bicycles and golf carts.
  ● Anna Kimmelblatt, Communities and Transportation Program
    Director for the South Carolina Coastal Conservation League,
    expressed appreciation for staff's work but felt that sections on
    settlement communities, the cultural core component, and the
    resilience element could be improved. She requested an
    opportunity to connect with staff to discuss potential
    improvements.
  ● Brianna Harmon suggested settlement communities might want to
    incorporate themselves to better self-govern between county and
    town jurisdictions. She also noted that residents in Charleston
    County properties cannot vote for town council members.
Following public comments, Mayor Haynie closed the public hearing.
    B. Blessing of the Fleet Check Presentations
Nicole Harvey presented on behalf of the Blessing of the Fleet event.
She began by honoring the memory of TJ Phillips, who had helped with
live broadcasts of the Blessing Boat Parade and recently passed away.
Ms. Harvey recognized the Magwood family's role in starting the festival
nearly four decades ago and thanked Anne, Tressie, Jennifer, Melissa,
and Tara for their continued work in producing the festival with town
staff. She also acknowledged the Wayne Magwood Fisherman's
Memorial statue that was recently installed.
She reported that eleven vessels were blessed this year, including the
lead Coast Guard boat, and each registered Shem Creek trawler
received $500, for a total of $4,500 in direct support of the local
shrimping and fishing fleet.
Harvey introduced representatives from the two beneficiary
organizations:
  ● Meow Dog Animal Rescue, which works with the Lucy Beckham
    High School Veterinary Sciences Program to provide shelter,
    safety, and medical care to animals in need.
  ● Rotary Club of Mount Pleasant, which has been serving the
      community for 47 years through programs such as Backpack
      Buddies, Happy Feet, dictionary distribution, and scholarships.
Each organization was presented with more than $13,000 to help
continue their missions.

(1) First Reading: An Ordinance providing for an amendment to the
Watermark Planned Development District Ordinance (Ord. 05052) by
amending the Zoning Map to incorporate an approximately 1-acre parcel
located on Bowman Road to allow for AB, Areawide Business uses with
the addition of outdoor storage in association with Car Rental Office use.
(Ord. 25026)
April 23, 2025 Planning Commission Minutes
May 5, 2025 Planning Committee Minutes
Ms. Reed presented the request to incorporate a 1-acre tract of land into
the Watermark Planned Development. The property, currently zoned AB
(Areawide Business), fronts Bowman Road just off Chuck Dawley
Boulevard. The applicant wanted to allow for car rental with outdoor
display/storage of rental vehicles. Rather than rezoning to AB-2, which
would allow more intense uses, the applicant proposed to join the
Planned Development with limited conditions allowing just the car rental
use.
The Planning Commission had recommended approval with an
enhanced buffer—a full 25-foot buffer with an 8-foot masonry wall—
along the residential portion of the property line. The Planning
Committee made no recommendation.
A motion to deny was made by Councilwoman Hyatt and seconded by
Councilman Chapman.
During discussion, Councilman Rambo noted that the property would be
developed regardless, and the current proposal with enhanced buffers
would be less intensive than many other allowed uses under the current
zoning.
Councilwoman Hyatt expressed concerns about deed restrictions that
had been promised when the property was originally rezoned from R-1
to AB-1, which appeared to prohibit vehicle rental with outdoor storage.




                                                                 Page 7 of 15
Mr. Pagliarini clarified that the town could not enforce private deed
restrictions, though residents could seek enforcement through civil
courts.
Mayor Haynie called for a vote and the motion to deny failed with a 4-4
vote. Councilwoman Hyatt and Councilmen Chapman, Iacofano and
Santos voted in favor of denial, with Councilmen Brownstein, Rambo,
Tinkey and Mayor Haynie voting opposed.

10. Council Business
     A. New Business
(1) Adoption: A Resolution expressing support for Alternative Highway
41 Corridor Improvements to avoid Laurel Hill Park impacts. (R.25071)
(R.25071 FINAL)
June 30, 2025 Transportation Committee Minutes
A motion to approve was made by Councilman Brownstein and
seconded by Councilman Iacofano.
Councilman Chapman proposed an amendment to modify language in
the resolution, removing the phrase "however town council opposes the
destruction of Laurel Hill Park for the construction of roadways" while
retaining language opposing the specific Southern Parkway
construction. He argued this would make the resolution less antagonistic
toward the county. Mayor Haynie seconded the motion.
After discussion, the amendment failed in a 3-5 vote. Councilmen
Chapman and Tinkey along with Mayor Haynie voted in favor, with
Councilwoman Hyatt and Councilmen Brownstein, Iacofano, Rambo and
Santos voting opposed.
During discussion on the main motion, several council members
expressed concerns about the Laurel Hill Parkway:
Councilman Rambo questioned how the "compromise" plan came to be,
describing it as "longer, slower, and more expensive" than the original
option.



                                                              Page 12 of 15
Councilman Iacofano cited John Mueller's vision that Laurel Hill would
be "an oasis in a sea of development," which guided his opposition to
the parkway.
Councilman Tinkey emphasized the need to protect green spaces while
looking for other ways to improve traffic flow.
Councilman Brownstein noted that while they had just passed a
moratorium on major subdivisions in settlement communities to work
with the county, they needed the County to work with them on this issue
as well.
Councilman Chapman cautioned against language that might
antagonize County Council and potentially jeopardize $160 million in
available funding for improvements.
The resolution was approved with Councilman Chapman voting opposed
and all others present voting in favor.
This Resolution SIGNED, SEALED, and DELIVERED this date.
