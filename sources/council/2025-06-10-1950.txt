7. Items Moved from Planning New Business and Planning
   Committee Report
   A. First Reading: An Ordinance to provide for a temporary
      moratorium on the acceptance and processing of major
      subdivision applications in Charleston County Historic Districts.
      (Ord. No. 25035) Planning Committee recommended approval.
Planning Director Michele Reed explained that the proposed moratorium
would prevent subdivision of land that would create more than 4 lots in
designated historic districts. The moratorium aligns with Charleston
                                                              Page 3 of 10
County's efforts and would allow time for developing more specific
criteria for these areas.
Mayor Haynie noted that the council had received a letter of support for
the moratorium from seven members of Charleston County Council.
Councilman Iacofano expressed concerns about the duration of the
moratorium and requested more specific deadlines for action.
Motion: To approve the first reading of Ordinance No. 25035 and invoke
the pending ordinance doctrine. Made by: Councilman Brownstein
Seconded by: Councilwoman Hyatt
The motion passed unanimously.
   B. Discussion and possible action regarding regulations for
      demolitions and rebuilds/major additions in older
      neighborhoods.
Mayor Haynie explained that this item was moved to be discussed
alongside the historic district moratorium due to similar concerns about
protecting neighborhoods from redevelopment.
Planning Director Michele Reed presented information on demolitions,
new builds, and additions in older neighborhoods. She outlined potential
options for incentivizing preservation and regulating size and scale of
new construction, including:
  ● Offering additional square footage allowances for maintaining
    existing houses
  ● Implementing a Building Area Ratio (BAR) similar to the Old
    Village
  ● Further limiting development standards such as height, lot
    coverage, and setbacks
Councilmembers discussed various aspects of the issue, including:
  ● The need to balance property rights with neighborhood
    preservation
  ● Concerns about stormwater runoff from larger structures
  ● The potential for a historic survey to identify significant structures
  ● The effectiveness of the existing 40% impervious surface rule
Motion: To send this issue to the Planning Commission for further review
and recommendations. Made by: Councilman Howard Chapman.
Seconded by: Councilman Gary Santos
The motion passed unanimously.

Motion: To approve the first reading of ordinances 25033 and 25034 for

Planning Director Michele Reed explained that the property, owned by a

by the family.
Council members discussed the potential for future development, noting
that under Community Conservation zoning, the property could
potentially accommodate 22-25 housing units. It was clarified that the
property would be subject to the town's newly adopted moratorium on
major subdivisions in historic districts once annexed.
The motion passed unanimously.
      (6) Request for first one-year extension of vested rights period
          under Patriots Annex Development Impact Assessment.
          Planning Committee recommended approval.
Motion: To approve the one-year extension of vested rights for the
Patriots Annex Development Impact Assessment. Made by:
Councilman Chapman Seconded by: Councilman Iacofano
Legal counsel David Pagliarini explained that state law narrowly defines
the circumstances under which vested rights extensions can be denied,
and those circumstances were not present in this case. He clarified that
the extension only applies to rights approved in 2019 and does not allow
for changes that would increase traffic or alter the approved density and
intensity of use.
Council members discussed the implications of the extension, including
its effect on development standards and the town's ability to negotiate. It
was noted that the extension maintains the status quo rather than
granting additional rights to the developer.
The motion passed unanimously.
