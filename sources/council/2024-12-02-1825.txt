     Mount Pleasant Code of Ordinances to impose a local option tourism

         Mr. Santos moved for first reading to enact Chapter 40.22 of the
     Mount Pleasant Code of Ordinances to impose a local option tourism
     development fee as discussed by Council, seconded by Mr.
     Chapman.
     Mayor Haynie asked Mr. Pagliarini if the Latin term mutatis mutandis
     refers to change, and Mr. Pagliarini responded in the affirmative.
     Mayor Haynie said that means that further identification of this point-
     of-sale jurisdiction mutatis mutandis, those can change. Mr.
     Pagliarini responded in the affirmative.


                                                                   Page 18 of 20
     Mr. Rambo said the fee would be imposed sixty days after it is
     finalized, but the project and conservation easements are not worked
     out. He asked what the approximate timeline would be the residents
     and businesses would start seeing this fee. Mr. DeMoura said when
     final reading occurs, that does not mean it will be imposed
     immediately. He said there is time given to the local government to
     work out more details, and time for businesses to configure such an
     imposition of a fee. He said based on our estimates, it could take six
     to eight months to be in a position to start charging this fee, so it
     would be around summertime that would be the earliest he imagines
     the fee would likely be imposed if everything went according to plan.
     Mr. Rambo said we would have first and final reading of imposing the
     fee, and asked if Council would have a further vote months from now
     to decide the start date. Mr. DeMoura said after second reading, the
     imposition of the fee is allowed by Council, so as we get closer to
     working out some of the details in the agreements, we would be in a
     better position sometime in the summer. He said it would not take
     an additional Council vote, but the progress would be communicated
     with Council every two weeks, so Council would know well in
     advance how things are moving, and whether we would be able to
     impose the fee in the summertime.
     Mayor Haynie said by state law, it takes a two-thirds majority of
     Council, and we are short a Council Member, which means we would
     need six votes in the affirmative to pass first reading. He said it is
     anticipated that second reading would be in early January.
         Mayor Haynie called for the vote and all present voted in favor.
     The measure passes with a vote of 8-0.
