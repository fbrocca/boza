3. First Reading: An Ordinance to amend Section 30.46,
   entitled Procedure for Enactment of Town Ordinances of


                                                 Page 56 of 66
the Mount Pleasant Code of Ordinances. (Ord. No.
25008 - Council Member Brownstein)




                                           Page 57 of 66
Mr. Pagliarini stated this was brought to the Police,
Judicial and Legal Committee, there was no
recommendation from the committee, but it was
requested by Mr. Brownstein to be placed on this
agenda.
Mr. Pagliarini stated our system is set up so that first
reading is set up for notice only and for discussion as
well as consideration of future amendments, but the idea
is to place it for notice to the public and to Council. He
stated at second reading, amendments may be made.
Mr. Pagliarini said state law requires at least two
readings, with six days between each reading, which are
the minimum standards. He said what other
municipalities do was reviewed.
Mayor Haynie asked if this states that Charleston,
Columbia and North Charleston have first reading by
title only, so they do not amend at first reading. Mr.
Pagliarini stated generally not, though there can be a full
reading requested by a member of Council, and they
handle their ordinances similar to the way the Town
handles our.
Mr. Brownstein moved for first reading, seconded by Mr.
Iacofano.
Mr. Brownstein said this ordinance would allow us to
make amendments at either first or second reading. He
                                                Page 58 of 66
said the rationale behind it is with our current system, a
member of the public has no idea what the final vote is
going to be on. He said for example with the zoning
code re-write the public was not sure when it would take
effect, whether pine trees would be protected, what the
tree protection zones would be and whether cottage
courts would be allowed. He said there are certain
issues that can be ironed out early on in the process to
give the public notice of what will likely be voted on at
second reading. He said in those cases we should
make those amendments early and give the public time
to research and absorb the information, but this does not
preclude us from making amendments at second
reading.
Ms. Hyatt said this sounds confusing that we can amend
at both times. She said the first reading is what comes
to Council as a whole for the first time, and if Council
Members were not at a particular meeting where it was
discussed, it is the first time they are hearing it. Ms.
Hyatt said she likes that Council and the public see the
ordinance for the first time, and have a month to think
about it, and any changes they would like to see, and
the public has time to make their comments as well.
She said she does not know how we would know what
changes we would like to make when seeing the
ordinance for the first time. Ms. Hyatt said she likes the
process as it is currently.
Mayor Haynie said he concurred with her comments.
Mr. Iacofano said it will be very helpful to be able to go
back and watch the committee meetings since they are
going to be live-streamed and we will be very educated
for first reading. He said with the City of Charleston, has
first reading by title only, and second reading for
amendments and third reading for a vote. He said the
public has zero response time when we make
amendments on second reading, and then we vote. He

                                                Page 59 of 66
said if we pay attention and follow committee meetings,
it allows us the chance to make those amendments on
first reading and truly gives thirty days for the public to
respond and it gives two chances for amendments.
Mayor Haynie asked Mr. Pagliarini how long the Town
has done it this way. Mr. Pagliarini stated it appears that
this was codified in 1981.
Ms. Whitley said her thought as to why this could
potentially be a good thing is that sometimes at our
meetings, we are cautioning the public that it is first
reading, and things will be changed before final reading,
so why not change it at first reading. She said
sometimes the thought for a change can come up during
discussion at final reading and then there is no chance
for the public to respond. She said there are benefits to
both ways.
Mayor reminded everyone that Greenville is two thirds
the size of Mount Pleasant and they are the only ones
who amend on first reading. He said there will be
amendments after first reading, and then if there are no
minutes published between then and the next meeting,
the public will not know, and how will they know to go
look for changes.
Mr. Rambo asked if the agenda for the next meeting
would have the amended ordinance linked to it, and Mr.
Pagliarini responded in the affirmative. Mr. Rambo said
it seems as if that would be more clear as he does not
like amending on final reading.
Ms. Hyatt asked when the Council agenda is published,
and Mr. Pagliarini said it is usually posted on the Friday
before the meeting.
Mr. Santos said the process has been working well, but
he would not mind trying something new.

room nights sold, they would meet the state
requirements to receive the accommodations tax dollars
to use for advertising to bring people in. He said the
current by-laws do state non-profit, which has been the

Mr. Iacofano said he believes this came up because of

marketing is $20,000, but they can get $20,000 from the

people who create them. She said she does not think
we should put taxpayer dollars to boost the fungible
money of someone’s advertising dollars to make a profit.
Mr. Chapman said Accommodations Tax funds were
specifically voted for in the state legislature to bring
tourism and heads in beds into a community. He said
hopefully some of our non-profits can do that, and some

numbers up. Mr. Chapman said we have an institution

with their attorneys that allowed for this, and he would
hope that we can still do that to help our hotels,
restaurants and to help bring events that create heads in
