VI.   PUBLIC COMMENT
           Scott Barheight stated he is the Senior Vice President for
      Government Relations for the Charleston Metro Chamber of
      Commerce. He stated the Metro Chamber respectfully requests
      that Council defer the decision on Ordinance 23005 regarding
      residential density in commercial districts which is up for first
      reading. He stated the members are very concerned that reducing
      potential additional housing will do more harm than good.
            Josh Dix, Government Affairs Director for the Charleston
      Trident Association of Realtors stated he is here to speak about the
      potential downzoning Council will be considering this evening. He
      said the reduction of twelve market-rate units in the commercial
      districts would be a detriment not only to the housing cause, but
      also to the Town in general. He said those types of units are
      typically renters, and they are a benefit as they do not traditionally
      have children in the school system. He said they are also
      contributing to the school system without adding to the school
      population. Renters also tend to have less vehicles. He asked
      Council to consider deferring this item as we need smart growth
      solutions and the removal of twelve units in the commercial district
      is not a smart growth policy.
          Rebecca Williamson, 606 Detyens Court stated she wanted to
      thank everyone who supported referendum. She said she and her
      husband have been playing tennis since they moved here in 1993.
      She said they did not have trouble finding a court until the last few
      years. She said we need tennis courts badly and asked Council to
      vote for final reading so we can have more courts.
           Jennifer McCool, Lowcountry Tennis Association (LCTA)
      overall coordinator for both 18 and over and 40 and over mens
      spring and fall leagues, as well as a member of the LCTA Board.
      She said the main facilities are close to capacity and this spring
      there are 389 teams playing in the Lowcountry league tennis. She
      said she hopes more courts can be found so that others can enjoy
the sport as much as she does.
    Dan Doyle, The Beach Company, 320 Broad Street stated he is
here to speak on Ordinance 23005, requesting that Council pause
and defer action on approving this item. He said it is important to
get feedback from property owners. He said the reduction in units
and the incentive to add four additional units if attainable housing is
included is not realistic. He said he is offering himself as a
resource to provide feedback to show and help Council understand
just how density and affordability in private sector development
works. He said if the Town wants to get serious about attainable
housing, look at what is happening in Greenville County. He said
they recently adopted a new affordable housing plan where they
work one-on-one with developers, and the affordability is tailored to
the mix. He encourages the Town to reach out to them.
     Michael Harley, 1600 Long Grove Drive stated he is here to
speak on the citizens commission on greenspace. He stated he
would like to know who the members will be, how large will the
commission be, would it be funded with town resources, what is
their operating budget, who will approve the members and their
qualifications. He asked how they will be selected and will they be
selected by someone qualified to make those selections. He asked
what authority the commission would be granted and by whom. He
asked what future impact this would have on prospective
developers and investors for Mount Pleasant. He asked if it will
have a positive or adverse effect, and how this concept came to be.
Mr. Harley said in his opinion this citizens commission is not in the
overall best interest of the town.
     David Quick, 1502 Village Square said he is astounded at the
loss of trees and unnecessary cutting and a lot of beautiful places
that have been paved over. Mr. Quick stated he is in total support
of green or resiliency commission in order to make our town a
greener, cleaner place. He said the City of Charleston has had a
resiliency and sustainability committee for years and have put many
great things into practice. He said he would like to see some
electric vehicles put into place as a result of this commission as
over a million dollars a year are spent on diesel and gas for town
vehicles. He said we are facing a crisis on the planet and we need
to have local and global solutions.
     Nanci Shipman – 1174 Freelock Drive stated this community is
special to her as it birthed a non-profit from the loss of her son to
the opioid epidemic in 2016. She said the state was awarded $363
million dollars over eighteen years allocated per percentage for
different counties and towns. Ms. Shipman said the Town has been
unprecedented in the state of leading the charge through
collaborative partnerships, first responders, medical providers and
other community civic organizations. She stated the goal of Wake-
Up Carolina is to provide awareness and education with hope and
recovery for young adults individuals and families through
community partnerships, as well as resources and support services.
Ms. Shipman said she just left a mothers group, the only one in the
state that provides a group for mothers who lost a child to a
substance, a child in recovery, or a child that they no longer have
access to. Ms. Shipman said she is begging Council to be diligent
on how the money is spent. She said her organization is on
Coleman Boulevard in a tiny space and they have served over a
half a million people in the past two years, during Covid when all of
the fundraisers had to be cancelled. She stated they served 64,000
people through their eleven programs and services who have
walked through their door. She is asking for support from the
Town.
     Kevin Cunnane stated he would like to thank the Council
Members who supported it, but stated someone is trying to stop the
World Trade Center memorial in town. He has asked for
communication as to why that happened and he has received
nothing. He said the memorial was started by a group of citizens
who managed to get a piece of the World Trade Center, and Mr.
Ritchie and Mr. Santos have been very helpful with trying to get it
placed in town. Mr. Cunnane said he thought it was time to place
the names of the individuals who requested items placed on the
agenda next to the item. He said regarding the push to maintain
and increase density in commercial areas, we do not have the fire
protection for density in this town. He said there is an engine
company off Coleman Boulevard with three firefighters. He said the
regulations require thirty-five to forty-five firefighters for a high rise
fire, and it has to do with the distance of the hose stretch. He said
that is enough reason to not have density like that.
     Blair Martin – 7341 Awendaw Landing Road stated for almost
twenty-six years he had the privilege of protecting our community
as a police officer, and in that time, he had a good view of the
threats to public safety in this town. Mr. Martin said his professional
opinion is that the number one threat is drugs. He said at the end
of December, the Charleston County numbers for fatal overdoses
for the year of 2022 were 225 with several dozen others still
pending toxicology. He said for the Town, eleven citizens were
confirmed dead from drug overdoses, with three other probables.
He said this is not an over-the-bridge problem. The number of
people struggling with an addiction is exponentially higher. He said
if you do not know someone who is personally affected by this, you
will. Mr. Martin said the town has been very helpful in supporting
those fighting addiction in our community. He is asking for the
Towns help one more time, so when it is time to allocate the funds
to please look very carefully to Wake-Up Carolina as there is no
one else who is providing these services for treatment and
prevention.
     Linda Brady – 226 Middle Street stated she is a resident of
Mount Pleasant and a Surf Soccer Club board member and she
would like to commend and thank the Recreation Department,
Public Services, Town staff, the Recreation Committee, the
Recreation Commission and Council for their efforts to improve and
support recreation, the arts, greenspace, and for moving forward
with Phases 3 and 4 of Carolina Park, and hopefully with the
passing of the recreation referendum. She said after discussions
with the Mount Pleasant Chamber of Commerce and the
Convention and Visitors Bureau, the South Carolina Surf
organization made decisions to reschedule the Charleston
Challenge Cup Soccer Tournament which has traditionally
conflicted with the Southeastern Wildlife Exposition and Valentines
Day weekend which is an already busy time for the Town. She said
it has been rescheduled to the second weekend in February where
hotel occupancy is significantly lower. She said they look forward
to continued collaboration with the Recreation Commission,
Committee, the Towns economic development team and the
business community to have a positive relationship and financial
impact with the Town.
     Ty Weatherby, 563 Creole Retreat stated the company he
started is called Rebellion Roads and he would not have heard of it
had Ms. Susannah Smith Miles not written an article about it in the
Moultrie News, describing a safe anchorage out in Charleston
Harbor where pirates used to blockade the city. He said he wanted
to support the development of the citizen group, and regarding the
Isle of Palms Connector bike lane, concepts five and three put two
bike lanes from Mount Pleasant to Isle of Palms and he personally
thinks the Town should have as much of a stake in it as the Isle of
Palms.
     Christine Von Kolniz, 1071 Cottingham Drive stated she is in
support of the greenspace commission. She said she is on the City
of Charlestons resilience and sustainability committee, and she has
also helped with the Countys efforts. She said they have done
fabulous work. Ms. Von Kolniz also stated she goes to the senior
center, and there are not a lot of programs and classes for people
in their 50s who would need before and after work times.
    Al Bradshaw Whittemore said he is one of the USA Pickleball
Ambassadors for Charleston County, and he is also an instructor.
He thanked Council for all they have done for pickleball. He said he
has introduced over 1,000 people to pickleball, and it has exploded.
He thanked Council for the recreation referendum that passed, and
he appreciates the eight courts that are proposed on Rifle Range.
    Perry Roark, 2004 Brick Kiln Parkway stated there were many
people that were there in support of final reading of the recreation
referendum. He thanked the members of Council who put the
referendum on the ballot for the residents to decide.
    Jay Thrower stated he is with the Wake-Up Carolina Board, and
he implores Council to consider them to leverage the dollars the
Town receives to impact lives. He said this organization fills a void.
