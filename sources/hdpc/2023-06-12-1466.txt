   Mr. Keith Harris, applicant, stated that he was available for questions.

    Ms. Linda Balzac, 422 Hobcaw Drive, stated that she worked previously on
this home. She suggested that since there was already asbestos that is original,
they should be allowed to be replaced with cementitious material. She also
suggested that allowing a composite for the windows to reduce rot.

     Mr. David Creech, 212 Center St, suggested that the guidelines should be
followed, particularly with historic homes. He suggested that if there is a
deviation to allow the cementitious siding, it should be a minimum of 5/8 inches
to allow the correct channel line.

   There being no further comments, Mr. Crosland moved to Commission
questions.

    Mr. Keith Harris, applicant, answered questions from the Commission. He
asked that cementitious material be allowed to match the newer existing
material. He stated that the siding would match the existing color.

Ms. Louisa Montgomery, 404 Church St, suggested that clad windows have been
previously approved for historic homes. She suggested that the thicker
cementitious siding would be appropriate. She suggested that given the humid
climate, the deviation from wood should be considered.

    There being no further comments, Mr. Crosland closed public comment.

     Mr. Barber moved for approval with the condition that the windows be a
wood simulated divided light product and that the siding profile be thicker than
the typical track home cementitious siding with final approval by staff. Mr.
Crosland seconded the motion. All in favor.

D. 448 Church Street – Survey 6190 / Historic (TMS# 5320100091) Final Review
   for partial demolition, addition, and new accessory structure

   Ms. Browder reviewed staff comments with the Commission.

   Ms. Linda Balzac, architect, reviewed the request with the Commission.

    Mr. David Creech, 212 Center St, expressed concern with the proposed
design. He suggested that the staff comments at the previous meeting are the
same as those presented at this meeting. He suggested that the proposed
design is not consistent with the historic district. He suggested that because of
the home being historic, it should have the “strictest scrutiny” as indicated in
the historic district guidelines. He asked the Commission to defer this project.

     Mr. Dennis O’Neal, 308 Venning St, expressed concern with the proposed
construction as this is a prominent location in the historic district. He suggested
that the length of the home should not be more than ½ the size and suggested
that this is not the case. He suggested that the rear setbacks are not in
compliance. He suggested that if it is raised two feet, it would cause a drainage
issue. He suggested that the screened porch is not consistent with other homes
in the area.

    Ms. Emily Hyder, 436 Church St, expressed concern with the proposed
construction and the scale of the home on this prominent corner lot. She
suggested that the new construction appears to be higher when looking at it by
the “naked eye”. She expressed concern with the chimney removal and that
there is a screened in porch. She suggested that the BAR is not correctly
calculated. She expressed concern that the shed and driveway would be located
                                            Historic District Preservation Commission
                                                                         June 12, 2023
                                                                            Page 4 of 6

in close proximity to her front door. She expressed concern with drainage and
that if additional fill is brought in, it would cause a drainage issue.

     Mr. Mike Tinkey, 208 Venning, expressed concern with the proposed
construction and agrees with staff comments. He stated that there are other
neighbors in the neighborhood are opposed to the project. He suggested that
the new construction is not subordinate to the historic structure and there are
no screened porches in the vicinity. He suggested that the fabric of the historic
home should be maintained and not changed. He suggested that the project
should be reconsidered to be in keeping with the character of the historic
district.

There being no further questions, Mr. Crosland moved to Commission questions.

   Mr. Crosland moved for denial Mr. Gallogly seconded the motion.

    Mr. Barber moved to amend the motion to add the findings that the denial is
based on the streetscape and proportion of the new construction to the existing
historic home. Mr. Crosland seconded the motion.

Mr. Barber withdrew his amendment and Mr. Crosland withdrew his second for
the amendment. Mr. Crosland withdrew his motion. Mr. Gallogly withdrew his
second.

    Ms. Louisa Montgomery, 404 Church St, suggested that the garage is too
“bulky” and the proposed construction too massive. She suggested that the
addition should be more in keeping with the historic district.

   There being no further comments, Mr. Crosland closed public comment.

    Mr. Barber moved for denial based on staff’s recommendation and with the
findings that the garage is to large and bulky and not in keeping with the
streetscape. Mr. Crosland seconded the motion. All in favor.

E. 605 Pitt Street – Survey 6097 / Non-Historic (TMS # 5320500029) Final
   Review for home addition, fences, and outdoor kitchen.

   Ms. Browder reviewed staff comments with the Commission.
                                             Historic District Preservation Commission

   Ms. Balzac, architect, reviewed the request with the Commission.

    Ms. Julia Shealy, 217 Queen St, asked that the request be denied as what has
already been accomplished has had a significant negative effect on the historic
district, the streetscape, and surrounding property values. She suggested that
the outdoor shower and sink would encroach into the setback and would be in
close proximity to their home. She asked that the outdoor kitchen be denied as
it would cause visual and noise pollution.

     Mr. Andy Shealy, 217 Queen St, expressed concern with the proposed
construction. He expressed concern with previous approvals causing significant
impact to their home. He suggested that the outdoor shower and sink would
cause negative impact into their way of living. He suggested that the outdoor
kitchen and shower have already been plumbed and suggested that this is
therefore an “after-the-fact” approval. He asked that the request be denied.

    Ms. Susan Harris, 224 Queen St, is in opposition of the proposed balcony as
it would impact the existing tree, would encroach into the setback and would
impede their home. She suggested that the size and mass should be considered
for this lot and if the proposed construction is appropriate.

   Mr. Tinkey, 208 Venning, expressed concern with the previous approvals and
how it impacts the historic district. He agreed with the Shealys and Ms. Harris
that the proposed construction is not in keeping with the historic district and the
character of the neighborhood. He asked that this request be denied.

    Ms. Jennifer Gestnor, 1600 Longhorn Drive, expressed concern with the
changes that have been approved and the negative impact it has had on the
character of the historic district. She suggested that the proposed construction
is not in keeping with the character of the neighborhood. She suggested that
the proposed changes should not be allowed and the request be denied.

   There being no further comments, Mr. Crosland closed public comment.

   Mr. Barber moved for approval of the request with denial of the outside
shower and sink based on the impact to the streetscape. Mr. Crosland seconded
