VI. Parking Permit/Time Limit Discussion

   Mr. Rutherford introduced Mr. James Aton, Transportation Department
Operations Division Chief.

Mr. Aton stated the Transportation Department has been tasked with reviewing
this issue to determine the feasibility of parking passes, how they would be
administered, associated fees, etc. He stated that most of the roads in the Old
Village Historic District (OVHD) are state roads and any proposal would have to be
reviewed and approved by South Carolina Department of Transportation (SCDOT).
He stated that staff would also contact surrounding municipalities, such as the City
of Charleston to see how they administer parking passes.

Mr. Hirshorn added this issue would be handled by Town staff and not the
Commission.

Ms. Wilson asked if portions of the OVHD could require parking passes, while other
areas would not. Mr. Rutherford answered that there is concern that if the entire
OVHD were not included that there could be a “leapfrog” to other areas. Ms.
Wilson asked if existing parking pads located in the right-of-way in front of homes
would have to be removed. Mr. Aton answered that this was a possibility and
suggested that if they remained, they would be available for the public as they are
located in the right of way.

Mr. Jim Berg, 402 Patjens Lane, suggested that parking passes could be easily
implemented. He suggested that there should be a time limit assessed as well. He
suggested an actual pass might not be needed but enforcement could be
complaint-based.

Mr. Miles Martschink, 134 Toomer Lane, suggested that the issue is bigger than
vehicle parking, it’s boat parking, parking pads in the right-of-way, etc. He
                                                Old Village Historic District Commission
                                                                     November 12, 2018
                                                                              Page 4 of 12

suggested that the character and aesthetics of the OVHD is being adversely
affected by these issues. He suggested that a citizen committee could be appointed
to complete a study on the issue.

Ms. Ruth Brennan, 617 Pitt Street, mentioned another issue is vehicles parking in
the right-of-way and damaging utilities. She stated that this could mean costly
repairs that are the responsibility of the homeowner.

Mr. Hirshorn suggested that there could be an informal committee to study the
needs of the neighborhoods. Mr. Rutherford stated that Town Council would make
the decision on whether to appoint a special committee.

Ms. Wilson noted that Mr. Aton indicated that Transportation staff would be
reviewing this issue.

Ms. Montgomery asked if there would be an opportunity for public input. Mr. Aton
answered in the affirmative.
