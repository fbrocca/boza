VIII. Discussion of Future Road Name Sign Design in Old Village Per Federal and
      State Regulations

    Mr. Aton updated the Commission on the new federal mandates regarding
street signage.

Ms. Joanna Keegan, 206 Live Oak Drive, suggested they investigate the type of signs
Palmetto Bluff has installed in their neighborhood as well as maybe adding some
lighting.

Ms. Carol Tinkey, 208 Venning Street, expressed concern with the new signage and
suggested that the signage should be consistent. She asked for clarification on the
signage required above the obelisks.

Mr. Mike Tinkey, 208 Vennng Street, suggested that the Town research what other
municipalities are doing, such as the City of Charleston and if there are exceptions
allowed for historic areas.

There being no further comments, Ms. Rowe closed public comment.

The Commission suggested that the signage should show that it is part of the
historic district and should be consistent with the obelisk signage in color; white
with black lettering. Mr. Anton stated that federal guidelines mandates that only
those areas on the national historic registry can be identified as such, so he is not
sure that the entire historic district could be identified. He stated that if a
decorative post is desired, it would require identifying funding for these posts.

Mr. Hackenberg asked is powder coated posts have been considered. Mr. Anton
answered in the affirmative, but the Town opted for the aluminum posts.

The Commission asked that images of the white sign with black lettering be brought
back to the Committee in addition to images showing a powder coated post.

IX. Update on Proposed Residential Parking Permit Study

Mr. Anton reviewed the results of the Parking Permit Study with the Commission.
He stated that based on the study, no parking permit program would be
considered.

Ms. Carol Tinkey, 208 Venning Street, suggested that the historic district has been
significantly impacted due to the overlay district. She stated that many of the
streets were laid out before cars were invented. She expressed concern with the
proposed construction of the inn on Hibben and Whilden streets.

Ms. Joanna Keegan, 206 Live Oak Drive, expressed concern with parking near Shem
Creek and patrons parking in front of homes causing a disturbance as well as
abandoning cars on her property. She suggested that residential parking permits
would be a good fit for the community. She suggested that there should be some
way to limit parking in the neighborhood for restaurants and other commercial uses
that are nearby. She suggested that there should be some way to enforce “no
parking” areas in the Old Village.

Mr. Mike Tinkey, 208 Venning Street, suggested that there is a parking issue
affecting the neighborhoods from the Shem Creek area. He suggested that this is
affecting the quality of life for the neighborhood. He suggested that there is also
noise affecting the neighborhood. Another factor is that the resident and child
safety is being affected. He suggested that a parking permit program needs to be
revisited.

Mr. Chris Coleman, 118 Pitt Street, stated that he agrees with Mr. Tinkey’s
comments.

Ms. Allison Daley, 204 Live Oak Drive, stated that she was not able to attend the
public meeting due to illness, but did email the consultant her concerns. She
suggested that there is not enough off-street parking and would appreciate more
investigation into this issue.

There being no further comments, Ms. Rowe closed public comments.

Mr. Rutherford stated that all comments regarding Earl’s Court should be directed
to the Legal Department. He stated that issues regarding intoxication, littering, etc.
should be forwarded to the Police Department.

Ms. Montgomery suggested that limiting parking times could be an option and that
signage indicating time limits could be installed that might deter someone from
parking in those areas. Mr. Aton answered that this could be considered, however,
                                                   Old Village Historic District Commission

there is not sufficient staff to enforce those parking time limits and it would require
amendments to the Town Code of Ordinances. Ms. Montgomery suggested the
restaurants could make arrangements with the parking garage at Shem Creek. Mr.
Anton answered that this would be a civil agreement between the parking garage
owner and restaurant owners.

Ms. Rowe asked the reason for the parking permit study. Mr. Anton answered that
it was requested in order to determine the main causes and issues as well as
solutions for those issues. He stated that another consideration would be that
those existing parking pads in the right-of-way would have to be removed.

Ms. Rowe asked is a homeowner could block off the parking pad with posts and
chains. Mr. Anton answered that because the parking pad is in the right-of-way it
should not be allowed.

Mr. Hackenberg asked if this program has ever been activated. Mr. Anton
answered the study was conducted not only for the historic district, but whether
this could be implemented for areas Town-wide. He stated that the Transportation
Committee agreed not to move forward with the parking permit program. Mr.
Hackenberg suggested that this is a program that is needed for the Town.

Ms. Rowe asked if there are other municipalities experiencing this same issue. Mr.
Anton answered that the Town did meet with the City of Charleston on this issue
to see what remedies they have implemented for their historic areas and other
areas throughout their city.
