B.   124 Mary St. A, Survey Card 6253/Historic – TMS 5171500075,
     Preliminary Review for the construction of a new primary structure.

     Mr. Hankinson reviewed staff comments with the Commission.

    Mr. Ryan Smith, architect, reviewed the request with the Commission. He
stated that the property has been subdivided and has its own address of 122
Mary Street.

    Mr. Scott McCormick, 141 Toomer Lane, asked that the encroachment into
the setback not be approved. He suggested that while the architecture meets
the character of the neighborhood, the encroachment into the front setback
is not a minor encroachment and is a major departure from the established
building line. He suggested that there should be a major justification for
approval of this significant encroachment. He suggested that traffic, parking
and safety on this right-of-way is a major concern as there is not sufficient
space for two vehicles to pass each other. He stated that over the last eight
years, some homeowners have “commandeered” portions of the right-of-way
for extension of their front yards, decreasing the width of the right-of-way. He
asked that the Commission deny the request.

     There being no further comments, Ms. Gravitt closed public comments.

    Mr. Player asked the reason for the varying window types and suggested
that it is “too much”. He suggested that the architecture looks like modular
sections put together and that the encroachment into the setback should be
restudied. He suggested that dimensions should be added to the plans.

    Ms. Gravitt asked the overall height. Mr. Smith answered that he is not
sure, but it is under the maximum 35 feet allowed.
                                           Historic District Preservation Commission
                                                                      August 10, 2026

   Mr. Craft stated that the location of the home mirrors the same as the
house located to the right.

    Ms. Gravitt asked the reason for the encroachment. Mr. Smith answered
that they were trying to keep as much space in the back as possible. He stated
that the modular feel was to mirror a historic home with additions added. He
stated another reason for pulling the home forward was to avoid the tree
protection zones as much as possible. He stated that the neighboring homes
are also pulled forward and meeting the setback would be out of character
with the surrounding properties. Ms. Gravitt asked how the footings and
foundation would avoid the tree protection zones. Mr. Smith answered that
the porch has piers and was designed in keeping with the arborist report. He
stated that the home was situated to take 124 Mary and the angle of 120 Mary
into consideration.

    Mr. Craft asked what the material is for the dormers on the front. Mr.
Smith answered that there is a singular piece of trim at the dormers. He stated
that full details would be noted in the final drawings.

    Mr. Foxworth asked if the shutters are double back. Mr. Smith answered
that the larger windows would have double-back shutters.

     Mr. Player asked if the small window on the northeast elevation is the
stairwell. Mr. Smith answered in the affirmative. Mr. Player asked if the entire
roof would be a metal roof. Mr. Smith answered in the negative and stated
that the front of the home would be a shingled roof.

   Mr. Hankinson asked the Commission to determine if they are comfortable
with the setback encroachment.

   Mr. Craft suggested that the historic context of the house placement
should be taken into consideration and not just the newer “suburban setback”.

    Ms. Gravitt suggested that it is a significant encroachment and that the
home should be located more in keeping with the neighboring homes. She
asked that the dimensions from the home to the property line should be
noted.

D.    320 Morrison St., Survey Card 6159/Non-Historic – TMS 5320100051,
      Preliminary Review for the demolition of an existing primary structure
      and construction of a new primary structure.

     Mr. Hankinson reviewed staff comments with the Commission.

     Mr. Joel Adrian, architect, reviewed the request with the Commission.

    Ms. Heather Evans, 318 Morrison, expressed concern with privacy and the
proximity of the house to the neighboring properties. She stated that the two
story porches could mean being able to see into their bedrooms.

    Mr. Chuck Bennett, 202 Pitt Street, expressed concern with cut-through
traffic and asked that the construction vehicles be parked on site as opposed
onto the street.

     There being no more comments, Ms. Gravitt closed public comment.

    Mr. Player noted that there are many golf carts that come down the street
as well.

    Mr. Craft stated he has no objection to the demolition. He suggested that
the 3-D models help in showing the massing. He suggested that the gables
could be simplified and asked what is driving the second gable on the front
elevation. He suggested that the window configuration could be simplified as
well.

    Ms. Edwards agreed with Mr. Craft about the gables and asked the reason
for it. Mr. Adrian answered that it was to show a step down in the massing.

   Mr. Craft suggested that the extra gable detail was not needed.

    Ms. Gravitt asked the reason for the positioning of the home. Mr. Adrian
answered that they were trying to avoid further encroachment into the tree
protection zone for the 36 inch willow oak. He stated that the arborist
provided guidance on the amount of encroachment into the tree protection
could be done without impacting the tree. Ms. Gravitt asked for details on
how the home would affect the canopy of the willow oak. Ms. Gravitt asked if
a variance would be required. Mr. Hankinson answered that the tree can be
pruned according to ANSI standards, but if it was desired to be removed, it
would require approval from the Board of Zoning Appeals (BOZA).
    Mr. Craft asked if the trees on the neighboring lot could be removed. Mr.
Adrian answered that they are diseased and they are working with the
neighbor and staff on approval for removal of those trees.

   Ms. Gravitt asked about the second story porch and the reason for it. Mr.
Adrian answered that this is the master bedroom and the owner thought it
would be a nice feature. He suggested that it could be re-studied.

   Mr. Craft suggested flipping the porch to be in the front might be an option.
Mr. Player suggested that having the second story porch would be no different
than having windows in that same location.

   Mr. Foxworth asked if a more traditional roof color could be considered.
Mr. Adrian answered that this could be re-studied.
