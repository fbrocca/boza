   a. Proposal to amend the Vested Rights division of Chapter 156 of the Mount
      Pleasant Code of Ordinances, Zoning Code Section 156.049, to amend
      provisions related to extensions of vested rights and prohibitions for
      approval thereof.

  Mr. Ulma reviewed the request with the Committee. He stated that the
  Planning Commission recommended approval on a 7 to 1 vote.

  Mr. Pagliarini stated that the fundamental requirements of state law are not
  being changed. He stated that this amendment is to better clarify when the
  extensions can be granted and to clarify that one Board, Commission or body,
  cannot approve extension of a vested right of an approved plan under the
  purview of another Board, Commission, or other body.

  Mr. Brimmer asked what protection the developer has when a change has been
  made that would “void the vested right”. Mr. Pagliarini answered that the
  developer has the initial two-year period to exercise their vested right. He
  stated that zoning and land development regulation changes are done on a
  broad basis and neighborhood centric as opposed to an individual parcel centric.

6. Discuss referendum to generate a Preservation Fund for the Town of Mount
   Pleasant

Ms. Whitley stated that she asked for this item be put on the agenda for discussion.
She suggested that there are properties that could potentially be bought by the
Town for preservation and improvement as open space or green space. She asked
that this could be sent forward as a referendum in November 2019 to generate
funds.

Mr. Bustos asked if this would be a yearly fee similar to a stormwater fee. Ms.
Whitley answered in the affirmative and stated that it would be specifically
earmarked for preservation.

Mr. DeMoura stated that it typically takes approximately five months to have a
referendum on a ballot, so if desired, the process would need to start in June 2019.
He stated that the content of the referendum question would be critical and would
need to be include specific details such as how the funds would be utilized, the
exact fee, impact to the operating budget, etc.

Mr. Brimmer suggested that this is not the appropriate Committee to review this
issue. He suggested that this should be considered by the Finance Committee and
through the upcoming budget process. He stated that he is not comfortable with
this Committee bringing this forward as it is a finance issue and not a planning issue.

Mr. Bustos suggested that this go to full Town Council for discussion and
consideration.

Ms. Whitley moved to send this issue to Town Council for further discussion. Mr.
Bustos seconded the motion.

7. Continued discussion of regulations related to Short-Term Rentals

Mr. Patrick Arnold, Executive Director of the Charleston Homebuilders Association,
stated that he has some expertise on this issue and is available as a resource in
developing an ordinance on this issue.

Ms. Maddie Donaldson, 1649 Lauda Drive, stated that she is opposed to regulations
that limits the citizens’ ability to utilize their property for short-term rentals. She
asked that townhomes and multi-family units should be allowed to have short-term
rentals. She suggested that each neighborhood should determine if short-term
rentals should be allowed.

Mr. Robert Donaldson, 322 Lapwing Lane, asked the Committee to allow short-
term rentals in townhome districts. He suggested that short-term rentals provide
additional income. He suggested that the homeowner’s association (HOA) should
have the authority to dictate whether short-term rentals are allowed. He
suggested that having short-term rentals would not affect the number of
affordable housing units in the Town.

Ms. Alicia Lockwood, 316 Lapwing Lane, expressed concern with prohibiting
townhome for short-term rentals. She stated that she has a fee simple lot and
suggested that these types of homes should be allowed to have short-term rentals.
She suggested that it could be considered discriminatory if not allowed. She stated
that she would like to opportunity to supplement her income by having short-term
rentals.

Ms. Sarah Jacob, 1152 Village Creek Lane, stated that she owns a condominium and
suggested that short-term rentals should not be allowed to have short-term
rentals. She suggested that there is not enough affordable housing in the Town.
She suggested that she would like to know who her neighbors are.
                                                                 Planning Committee
                                                                   December 3, 2018

Mr. Mark Sanford, 509 King Street, suggested that there is legitimacy in both sides
of the equation. He suggested that there should regulations, but those regulations
should allow as much opportunity as possible. He suggested that often, a short-
term renter is a better tenant than a long-term renter. He agreed that there should
be regulations to prevent entire streets or neighborhoods to be bought by
investment companies for short-term rentals.

Ms. Canon reviewed the request with the Committee.

Mr. Bustos asked if those currently having short-term rentals could be
“grandfathered” in. Ms. Canon answered that she was not sure, particularly since
the Town currently does not regulate whole house short term rentals. She stated
that more research and review by legal counsel would be required.

Mr. Brimmer stated that he would be comfortable with regulations similar to the
City of Charleston.

Ms. Whitley stated that off-street parking requirements should be included in the
ordinance. She suggested that 20% of the fee could be earmarked for affordable
housing. She suggested that a 1% maximum of the number of total dwelling units
should be considered. She also suggested that there should be annual safety
inspections and the administrative costs should be included in the annual fee. She
suggested that there should be resources to adequately enforce the ordinance.

Mr. Bustos asked about fire inspections and how much of a burden would this be
for the Fire Department. Chief Mixon answered that short-term rental inspections
are not included in the National Fire Code. He stated that approximately 5,000
inspections are completed by the Fire Department each year. He stated that these
inspections would need to be coordinated with the Inspections Division. He stated
that this could mean additional staff. He stated that it would need to be
determined if an affidavit would be sufficient or if a physical inspection would be
required. He noted that currently for commercial properties, they only inspect the
common areas. Mr. Bustos asked if guidelines could be developed that would be
included with the affidavit and then have spot inspections. Chief Mixon answered
in the affirmative and stated that this could be done if desired. He suggested that
it might be beneficial to have an initial inspection required.

Mr. Brimmer stated that as discussed at the November Town Council, the specifics
on inspections would be further discussed as this process continues.

Mr. Bustos suggested that there should be an annual inspection with a business
license required. He suggested that there should be a fire inspection. He suggested
that townhome and multi-family units should be required to be owner-occupied,
while single family units might not be required to be owner-occupied. He
suggested that parking should be considered as well. He asked how the
accommodations taxes are handled. Mr. Ulma answered that this would be
handled by Finance.

Ms. Whitley suggested that a percentage of the fee should go to affordable housing
and there should be a maximum number of short-term rentals allowed throughout
the Town.

Mr. Brimmer suggested that short-term rentals of accessory dwelling units (ADUs)
should be owner-occupied. He suggested that there should be regular inspections.
He suggested that there should be some protection against investment properties.
He suggested that there should be compliance with American Disabilities Act (ADA)
requirements. He suggested that the Town should not rely on affidavits, but there
should be regular inspections. He agreed with Mr. Sanford that the neighborhoods
should be protected from investment companies.

Mr. Bustos expressed concern with having a percentage maximum and that this
could become a burden for areas that do not prohibit short-term rentals.

Mr. Arnold suggested that an option to having a maximum number on short-term
rentals is to have a de-clustering ordinance that limits the number allowed within
an area.

Mr. Bustos suggested that the de-clustering should be researched and included in
the ordinance.

Ms. Whitley expressed concern with de-clustering as it might be difficult to
administer. She suggested that a numerical maximum might be a better option.

Mr. Sanford suggested that the sharing community is a different concept and
operates differently, so this should be kept in mind going forward. He suggested
that the property rights should be kept in mind as well when going forward. He

Mr. George Bixler, 144 3rd Avenue, asked that the PowerPoint presentation be
