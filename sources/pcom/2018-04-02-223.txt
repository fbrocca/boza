4. Request to amend the Patriots Point PD, Planned Development District
   Ordinance, establishing development guidelines and permitted uses for the
   National Medal of Honor Museum, an approximately 8.827 acre portion of
   TMS No. 517-00-00-001, located on Patriots Point Road, depicted on a plat
   recorded by the Charleston County ROD Office in Plat Book W, Page 025. Also
   request approval of an Impact Assessment & Conceptual Plan.

Ms. Kathy Smith, 239 Cooper River Drive, stated that the Planning Commission
recommended denial of the project. She clarified the reason for the denial was not
because of the design or if it was appropriate in the area, but because there were
significant variances that would be needed regarding the height, critical line buffer,
impervious surface setback and parking requirements. She suggested that the
project was rushed to be submitted for approval, in part due to potential changes
with the critical line buffer. She suggested that the project should not have been
presented to the Commission. She stated that the maximum height for this
property is 50 feet and not 80 feet as has been mistakenly reported.

Ms. Pat Sullivan, 1002 Plantation Court, stated that this is a southeastern coastal
town where residents value history, character and environment. She suggested
that the proposed project along the coastline is not appropriate at the presented
height. She suggested that the project is ostentatious and not in character with the
lowcountry. She suggested that the height should not be compared to the
Yorktown control tower. She suggested that there should have been a
                                                                  Planning Committee
                                                                         April 2, 2018
                                                                          Page 3 of 10

collaborative effort with the Town and the community on this project to ensure
that the project would meet the Town’s requirements.

Mr. Gary Santos, 1690 Greystone Blvd, stated that after seeing the design of the
project and the intended uses, it seems that there could be an opportunity to
partner with Patriots Point and not duplicate efforts between the museum and the
Yorktown that would mean the ability to lower the height. He stated that he is in
favor of the Medal of Honor Museum in Mount Pleasant and looks forward to
having a project that meets the requirements of both the Town and the developer.

Mr. Ulma reviewed the request with the Committee (attachment 1).

Mr. Morrison reviewed transportation comments with the Committee (attachment
1).

Mr. Bustos asked about the road relocation and if the Town would pay for $1.2
million for a portion of the relocation with the project responsible for the
remainder of the relocation and parking. Mr. Morrison answered in the affirmative
and stated that the displaced parking of approximately 112 spaces would be the
responsibility of the project with additional parking being paid for by the Town for
a total number of parking spaces of approximately 170 spaces.

Mr. Bustos asked if there are any changes to the developer’s presentation from
what was presented to the Planning Commission. Mr. Stuart Whiteside answered
in the negative.

Mr. O’Rourke stated that his understanding is that the applicant is willing to work
with the Town on revising the project to better meet the requirements of the Town.
He suggested that this should go back to the Planning Commission to review the
revised proposal. He stated that he would like to see this project come to fruition
and suggested that this project should go back to the Planning Commission to
receive a recommendation on the revised project.

Mr. Brimmer asked how many variances are included in the request. Mr. Ulma
answered that there are some considerations such as the proposed changes to the
Impervious surface setback from 30 feet to 15 feet, with an average of 30 feet;
maintenance of the critical line buffer at 15 feet; and the extended height to 140
feet. He stated that some other minor variances involve parking lot landscaping.
                                                                  Planning Committee

He stated that there has been a preliminary Design Review Board (DRB) staff review
as well to provide some insight into the proposed design guidelines. Mr. Brimmer
agreed with Mr. O’Rourke’s comments. He stated that as a former Planning
Commission member and now Town Council member, he has been reviewing plans
for over ten years. He suggested that the item should be deferred or withdrawn in
order to work with the Town and have more community outreach so that there is
a better consensus and approval on the project. He agreed that more work should
be done to ensure a better project. He suggested that deciding now would not be
beneficial for the Town or the project.

Mr. Bustos stated that there are a lot of “moving parts” with this project including
the relocation of the road, which requires a signed agreement that is currently not
in place. He agreed with the previous comments where all the variables are not
currently known. He suggested that the project should be completely vetted
before a decision is made to ensure that the citizens, Town Council, and the
developer are comfortable moving forward.

Mr. Joe Daniels, Board CEO, thanked the Committee for this opportunity and the
privilege to work on this project. He agreed that the only way this project comes
to fruition is through an engaged partnership with the community and the Town.
He stated that he understands the impact a project as this could have on the quality
of life. He stated that there should be opportunity for the community to provide
input on this project.

Mr. Brimmer asked about the procedure with either a deferral or withdrawal. Mr.
Ulma suggested that if changes are anticipated, then the project should be
withdrawn and resubmitted. He stated that Town Council would need to approve
a waiver to submit within the one year waiting period.

Mr. Bustos suggested that the request could be deferred for 60 days with the
project to come back to the Committee. He stated that then a decision could be
made on whether it would need to go back to the Planning Commission for a public
hearing.

Mr. Brimmer agreed and suggested that the applicant should determine if it should
be deferred or withdrawn. Mr. O’Rourke agreed and stated that once deferred,
the applicant could choose to withdraw. Mr. Daniels agreed.

   Areawide Business District, an approximately 1.00 acre tract of land
   comprised of one parcel in its entirety and portions of two parcels, located

Ms. Canon reviewed the request with the Committee (attachment 1).

Ms. Mamie Rudy, 1232 Melvin Bennett, lives adjacent to the requested property
and expressed concern with commercial property being adjacent to her back
yard. She stated that this is a historical neighborhood and suggested that to
keep the neighborhood character intact, the residential uses should be
maintained for the entire property.

Mr. John Burn, 1411 Breckon Rd, lives across Bowman Road from the proposed
property. He suggested that the request would be consistent with the
commercial property along Bowman Rd.

Mr. Larry Haig, 1318 Bluebird Dr, suggested that if zoned commercial the
commercial uses would further the “creep” along Melvin Bennett Road. He
stated that another concern is that the type of commercial use is currently not
known. He suggested that the property should remain residential.

Mr. Bustos asked if there is a bank located on the corner of Melvin Bennett. Ms.
Canon answered in the affirmative.

Ms. Canon stated that the applicant submitted a rendering of the proposed
office building and showed this for the Committee (attachment 1).

Mr. Tim Askins, applicant, stated that he has been working with the
neighborhood to have a development that would be amenable to the
neighborhood. He distributed a list of restriction of uses that would be included
with the deed to the property. He stated that an eight-foot fence would be
included around the commercial portion of the property. He suggested that this
would be a separation of the neighborhood and not “creep”. He stated that he
has coordination letters from Mount Pleasant Waterworks (MPW) and SCDOT.

6. Continued discussion regarding the Urban Corridor Overlay District (UCOD)
   and potential removal of Ben Sawyer Boulevard from the district

Mr. Ulma reviewed the request with the Committee (attachment 1).

Mr. Bustos asked if the UC-OD is removed, would the existing residential be
considered non-conforming. Mr. Ulma answered in the affirmative and stated that
the only residential allowed in AB zoning is multi-family and as a conditional use.

Mr. Brimmer asked about Sawyer’s Landing and if there was damage due to a storm
event, could the property be re-built. Mr. Ulma answered that if the UC-OD were
removed, the property would have to develop under the AB zoning and the
permitted and conditional use requirements. He stated that there are currently 15
units and under the AB zoning only 12 units would be allowed as a condition of
multi-family uses. He stated that the current height would also exceed the height
                                                                 Planning Committee
                                                                         April 2, 2018
                                                                        Page 10 of 10

limit under AB zoning. Mr. Brimmer asked if this could be considered a taking. Mr.
Pagliarini answered that it depends on the circumstances, but down-zoning in itself
would not constitute a taking.

Mr. Bustos suggested that this should be further discussed. He stated that he sees
some benefits, but would like to make sure that this is completely vetted. He asked
that this information be sent to the entire Town Council as well.

Mr. Bustos suggested that this be deferred to the next Committee meeting. The
Committee agreed to defer this issue to the May Committee meeting for further
discussion.

7. Report on Comprehensive Plan Update

Ms. Boyles updated the Committee on the Comprehensive Plan update
(attachment 1).

Mr. Bustos asked about golf carts and their increasing use as transportation and if
allowing them on roadways could be included in the transportation network. Ms.
Boyles answered that this could be researched to determine viability and legality.

Mr. Brimmer asked about the Long Range Transportation Plan (LRTP). Ms. Boyles
answered this typically comes after the land planning discussions. She stated that
there have been some technical issues with the model that needed to be worked
out. She stated that it is anticipated that there would be some major work on this
at the May 1st Comprehensive Plan Forum meeting. She stated that in addition, an
additional open house is scheduled for June with the LRTP being a large component
of the open house.

       RESTRICTIONS AND ALLOWANCES IN THE UC-OD
An overlay expands or limits land uses. The UC-OD as it relates to Ben Sawyer Blvd
expands uses/standards as follows:
▪ Base/underlying zoning determines permitted uses (residential vs.
   commercial/mixed use):
   ▪    Residentially-zoned properties – can utilize UC-OD density for detached single-family, duplexes,
        townhouses, & multi-family; public institutional/ civic uses are permitted
   ▪    Commercially-zoned properties – greatest flexibility; permitted to have any residential use
        listed above AND all permitted uses provided in the OP, NC, AB (residential is a conditional
        use, ground floor residential not permitted), and PI-1 Districts. Any combination of the above on
        same site, in same building, and/or on same floor; no special exception required for outdoor or
        streetside dining.
•UC-OD expands to allow density of 16 units/acre and 20 units/acre for mixed-use
•Design Requirements: 2 Story building on Blvd., wider sidewalks for Activity Zone, ability to
 have Reduced Parking
AB (AREAWIDE BUSINESS) ZONING
AB, Areawide Business District. It is the purpose of this section that
the AB District be utilized for certain types of businesses, related to
those in the NC and OP Districts, which need a location adjacent to
arterial roads. The regulations that apply within this district are
designed to encourage an economically healthy environment,
particularly for those businesses that have a market area beyond just
one or a few neighborhoods. They are also designed to discourage
the encroachment of other uses capable of adversely affecting the
basic commercial character of the district.
