stated that a school use requires a special exception and is not a use by right

     Ms. Reed stated that the Planning Commission reviewed possible options and
questioned what problem was trying to be resolved. She stated that the Planning
Commission expressed concerns with rezoning all of the properties to PI zoning.
She stated that before the adoption of the new zoning code, density was
established in the Comprehensive Plan, so if a property was to be developed, the
recommended zoning and corresponding density in the Comprehensive Plan was

current proposal, a request to change the Comprehensive Plan would have to be
submitted to establish density. If that Comprehensive Plan change request was

and discussed by the Commission, but this is not a viable option.

    Ms. Whitaker suggested that the current issue is that Town Council was taken
out of the review process. Ms. Reed stated that moving the density to the Zoning

risk of denial of the Comprehensive Plan amendment could be considered

7. Discuss Options for Reviewing Proposed Development in the Ten Mile and
   other Settlement Communities that are designated as historic.

    Ms. Reed reviewed this item with the Committee. She stated that further
discussion is needed to determine how to move forward in reviewing development
of properties in the historic settlement communities. She reviewed possible
options with the Commission as follows:
   ➢ Rely on Zoning Overlay District only
   ➢ Coordinate with Charleston County on a Joint Commission
   ➢ Expand Existing Historic District Preservation Commission (HDPC)
   ➢ Create Separate Historic Commission specific to Settlement Communities
        East of the Cooper River
   ➢ Deny Annexations for properties in a County Historic District
    Ms. Whitaker suggested that expanding HDPC would be her preference as this
creates a unified commission within the Town; however, not having the area
character appraisals completed and not knowing when they would be available is
a concern. She suggested that without the area character appraisals, the timelier
option might be to see if a joint commission with Charleston County is legally
viable.

    Mr. Tinkey suggested that working with Charleston County on a joint
commission is a logical option. He agreed that the area character appraisals are
needed to have further discussion on expanding HDPC and how that could be
accomplished or if a separate historic commission specific to the settlement
communities needs to be established. He suggested that until these could be
determined, denying annexation would be the best option.

    Ms. Crosby agreed that it could be problematic in expanding HDPC and
suggested that a joint commission is currently the best option.

    Ms. Hyatt suggested that the overlay district could govern the review and
preservation of the neighborhood, like how the Design Review Board operates.
Ms. Boyles answered that once the area character appraisals were completed, the
overlay district could be amended to incorporate those design standards /
guidelines, which staff could then use for review and approvals; however, this
would be a staff level approval as opposed to having public input.

      Ms. Reed suggested that there are more issues that would come into play that
  would not be related to zoning. Ms. Pigott explained the County’s process for
  historically designated settlement communities. She stated that this includes the
  area character appraisal and identifying contributing historic resources. She stated
  that they also follow the Secretary of Interior standards for treatment of historic
  properties. She stated that some of the issues addressed were the placement of
  street trees due to the height of the proposed structure, elevation of buildings
  beyond the required flood zone, etc.

     Ms. Whitaker suggested that the historic communities voiced the desire to
  have guidelines for those reviewing the requests, so it is less subjective.

      Mr. Tinkey suggested that having the guidelines included in the Zoning Code is
  a benefit and suggested that the area character appraisal would be a good source
  of those guidelines. He suggested that it would be important to have the historic
  commission made up of residents within the community.

      Ms. Crosby asked if the joint commission would be adding members to the
  existing historic commission or an entirely new joint commission. Mr. Pagliarini
  answered that as proposed it would be a joint commission made up of members
  from the Town and County. He suggested that he is not certain that this could be
  legally established and would have to confer with the County attorneys. He
  suggested that this was very premature as what is desired to be enforced needs to
  be determined before having discussion on how to establish the governing
  commission.

      The Committee agreed that the legality of having a joint commission with the
  county should be explored as the first option with the possibility of expanding the
  historic district preservation commission in the future.
