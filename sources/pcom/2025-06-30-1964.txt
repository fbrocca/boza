3.   Public Comments

    Mr. Bill Maccio, 1319 Lake Mallard Blvd, owner of Mount Pleasant Magazine,
stated that the magazine sheds light on positive aspects of Mount Pleasant. He
stated that over the years the growth of the Town has been an issue. He stated
that Continental Realty is looking to do well for the Town with the proposed
changes for Towne Centre. He stated that Towne Centre is approximately 53 acres
of property in the Town and suggested that the best for this area and for the Town
should be considered.

    Mr. Alan McMahon, representative for Towne Centre, stated that the proposed
plan for Towne Centre is a culmination of several years of work with staff, public
meetings and other agencies. He stated that no additional infrastructure is
proposed, no grand trees impacted, and a reduction in stormwater impacts due to
the amount of paved surfaces being reduced. He stated that the proposal would
provide needed attainable housing. He stated that in addition there is 60,000
square feet of family entertainment uses that would be maintained, the addition
of open space, and needed transportation stops. He stated that this would provide
live/work options within the Town. He asked for the Committee’s support.

    Mr. George Freeman, 1450 Bowman Rd, spoke regarding the Highway 17 North
rezoning. He stated that Highway 17 Community Task Force outlined conditions
for rezoning and commercial uses for this area. He asked that those conditions be
upheld to maintain the area. He also suggested that Mount Pleasant Way could
benefit from the purchase of property in the 2-mile community.

    Ms. Pam Ireland, 820 Fiddlers Point Way, spoke regarding Towne Centre, and
suggested that instead of allowing a ratio of 6 market rate residential units and 2
attainable housing units, a ratio of 3 market rate to 1 attainable housing unit or a
ratio of 4 market rate unit to 1 attainable housing unit would be a better option
that would make the attainable housing more viable for the developer and lower
the overall number of residential units allowed.

    Ms. Kathy Herrmann, 1524 Cypress Pointe, marketing director of Towne Centre,
stated that the number of events, services, and charitable events has grown over
the years. She stated that because of this growth, more green space is needed to
accommodate additional events. She suggested that the proposed plan would
allow for more green space. She also suggested that there are successful mixed-
use areas such as Avalon, Summit, and Fenton that attract high quality retail,
restaurants, and entertainment because of the built-in population of office workers
and residents. She stated that many of the tenants have expressed their support
of the proposed plan.

    Ms. Myra Richardson, 2755 Earl Johnson Lane, read a quote for the Committee
regarding the tree canopy and asked that the study from 2019 report be provided
for the public. She stated that the Hamlin Beach community has a significant
amount of tree canopy and this should be preserved. She expressed concern with
rezoning in the Hamlin Beach and Ten Mile Communities that threatens the
character of the community and the quality of life for the residents. She suggested
that the continued growth cannot be sustained without increasing and improving
the infrastructure. She suggested that the character of the existing communities,
the wildlife habitat, and the trees throughout the Town need protection and
preservation.

     Mr. Sean Barnes, 1418 Barbara St, stated that he agrees with Ms. Richardsons
comments. He expressed concern with the height of structures and the engineered
fill of properties. He suggested that the engineered fill starts out pervious but

    Mr. Andy Lowe, 2795 Waterpoint Circle, property manager of Towne Centre,
