3.   Public Comments

    Mr. Richard Habersham, 2938 Bennett Charles Road, spoke regarding the
Charleston County moratorium on zoning changes to settlement community
properties. He stated that they are working on their character appraisals and asked
that the Committee recommend extension of the Town’s moratorium on
annexations and zoning of these historic properties as well.

     Mr. Hacker Byrd, Trident Academy, spoke regarding the proposal to rezone
church and school properties to Public Institutional (PI) zoning. He gave a history
of the school, noting that the school provides learning for students with learning
differences. He stated that they anticipate providing learning for well over the next
100 years and having the property remain as currently zoned is a part of that plan.

    Mr. Jason Taylor, Trident Academy, spoke regarding the proposal to rezone
church and school properties to PI zoning. He stated that they have a strategic plan
outlining their goals and plans for the property in the future. He asked that the
Committee and Town Council consider the ramifications of changing the zoning and
the possible detrimental effects.

      b.    R-16-26 Gregorie Ferry Towns. Request to amend the Gregorie Ferry
            Planned Development District Ordinance, to modify the AMI, Area

     Median Income Limits, for Gregorie Ferry Towns Attainable Housing
     development.

    Mr. Stone reviewed the request with the Committee. He stated that the
Planning Commission recommended approval.

    Mr. Tinkey stated that he reviewed this with the Planning Commission
chair and thanked staff for their review of the request including the scenarios.

    Mr. Tinkey moved to recommend to Town Council approval. Ms. Crosby
seconded the motion.

     Ms. Whitaker asked the maximum sell threshold. Mr. Stone answered that
it is 3% not including the approved qualified improvements. He stated that
Origin SC, a non-profit organization, handles the calculations for the resale.
Ms. Whitaker stated that she understands that the homeowner’s goal would
be to sell the home for the maximum amount to have funds to put toward
their next home purchase; however, the Town’s goal is to have housing
available for first-time buyers. She expressed concern with increasing the AMI
for this neighborhood as it would affect the amount of affordable housing
available in the Town. She stated that she is aware of the different mortgage
qualification criteria and that it can be difficult to secure a mortgage while still
meeting the 80% AMI, but the intent is to have affordable housing at this price
point within the Town.

    Ms. Crosby agreed with Ms. Whitaker and asked if increasing the AMI
would exclude a potential buyer. Mr. Stone answered in the negative and
stated that once a home is purchased it does not have to be vacated if the
household income increases above the 80% AMI. He stated that increasing
the AMI would expand the number of potential buyers for the homes. Ms.
Crosby expressed concern that increasing the AMI could mean that the homes
would eventually reach fair market value, which is against the intent of having
the affordable housing.

    Ms. Whitaker agreed and suggested that it shifts the demographic from
low income to middle income.

    Mr. Tinkey asked if the potential buyer is required to report all household
income. Mr. Stone answered in the affirmative.

    Ms. Hyatt asked the ranges for the different tiers. Mr. Stone stated that
these ranges for affordable and workforce housing are recognized by HUD; the
attainable housing is a newer category. He reviewed the difference between
affordable, workforce, and attainable housing AMIs with the Committee. She
expressed concern with increasing the AMI to the attainable housing range.
She suggested that while it would increase the number of qualified buyers, it
would mean fewer opportunities for those at the lower income level.

     Mr. Tinkey withdrew his motion. Ms. Crosby withdrew her second.

    Ms. Crosby moved to recommend to Town Council approval of changing
the AMI for Gregory Ferry Towns up to 120%. Mr. Tinkey seconded the motion.

    Mr. Tinkey asked how this would affect the neighborhood and have any
detrimental effects. Ms. Reed answered that up to 120% AMI is a typical
standard. She suggested that the AMI for Carolina Park is outside the norm.
She suggested that this being a more conservative and restrictive amendment,
it would not require an additional public hearing. Mr. Pagliarini concurred.

   Ms. Hyatt called for a vote on the motion. Motion failed on a 2 to 2 vote
with Ms. Crosby and Mr. Tinkey in favor; Ms. Hyatt and Ms. Whitaker opposed.

Ms. Hyatt stated that this would move forward to Town Council with no
recommendation.
