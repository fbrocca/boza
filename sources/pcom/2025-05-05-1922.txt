3.   Public Comments

    Mr. Larry Kobrovsky, County Councilman, spoke regarding the annexations on
the agenda. He asked that the historic settlement community properties should not
be annexed into the Town as it could negatively affect the character of those
communities. He stated that there currently is a county moratorium on subdivisions.
He expressed concern with development causing more traffic. He suggested that
there is a responsibility to preserve the historic settlement communities. He asked
that the annexations be denied.

    Mr. Sean Barnes, 1418 Barber St, suggested that development should not be
allowed unless the infrastructure is in place. He stated that the new zoning code
now allows accessory structures to be 25 feet in height. He expressed concern that
allowing the additional height is not in character for many of the neighborhoods in
the Town, especially those that have been in existence for many years. He asked
that additional height not be allowed for accessory structures.

    Mr. Brett Bennett, 131 Hibben St, expressed concern with the additional height
of the accessory structures and that it negatively affects the character of historic
neighborhoods. He also expressed concern with subdivision of lots and that this
should only occur if it is consistent with the surrounding lots. He also spoke

    Ms. Lisa Nuzum, 761 Adluh, expressed concern with the height of the accessory

    Mr. Adam Ferrara, 1660 Rivertowne Country Club, spoke regarding the issue of
assessing impact fees in regard to imminent domain and condemnation. He
suggested that this is not a voluntary action. He suggested that assessing impact
fees for relocation seems to be unjust as it is not a new impact but merely moving
that impact to a new location. He suggested that other municipalities allow an
exemption in these cases and asked that this be considered.

   Mr. David Quick, 1502 Village Square, thanked Mr. Kobrovsky for his comments
and for trying to preserve the character of the Town. He suggested that the main
theme of these comments is over development. He suggested that some hard
choices need to be made to preserve the character of these historic areas and the
Town. He suggested that preservation of the historic settlement communities,
preservation of the tree canopy, and the number of larger homes being developed
where smaller homes were located all affect the character of the Town.

    Ms. Cassandra Davis, 1148 Huro Rd, spoke regarding the historic settlement
communities. She asked that guidelines for development of these settlement
communities should be considered to preserve the character of these historic
communities. She suggested that there should be members from the settlement
communities able to provide input on these issues. She also suggested that the
zoning request for Boston Grill should be denied and the property not required to
annex into the Town.

4.   Discussion and possible action on creating a process parallel to the county's
     efforts to preserve historic settlement communities

     Ms. Reed reviewed the request with the Committee.

   The Committee discussed how the settlement communities could be preserved
and if a similar moratorium as established for the county should be considered.

    Mr. Chapman moved to recommend to Town Council approval to begin dialogue
with Charleston County to have similar requirements and development guidelines in
place, look at annexation requirements for water and sewer service, and to consider
a possible moratorium in place that mirrors the moratorium currently in place by
Charleston County. Ms. Hyatt seconded the motion. All in favor.
