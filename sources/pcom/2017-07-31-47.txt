Mr. Smith asked the setback requirements for cell towers. Ms. Farrell answered
that the request is to amend the PD for this to be a permitted use. She stated
that the closest residential property line is approximately 104 feet. She stated
that as a special exception, the cell tower would have to be the same distance
from the residence as the height of the tower.

Mr. Carrier asked the difference in this request from other requests such as the
cell tower near Candlewood. Ms. Farrell answered that the regulations in place
today differ from the regulations in place when the Candlewood cell tower was
approved. She stated that the current regulations were enacted as a result of
the concerns with the cell tower near Candlewood.

Mr. Bustos asked about cell coverage. Mr. Cessa answered that if he cannot get
good cell service, he uses his landline. He stated that his neighbor frequently
travels to a location where he can get sufficient cell service.

Mr. Brimmer asked about consideration of different locations for the cell tower.
Mr. Yates answered that there is seldom a location where there is not some
opposition. He stated that they typically look for locations that will provide the
best and largest coverage area that are in commercial areas or other
appropriate locations. He stated that the proposed area would be located near
the golf course maintenance area. He stated that the tower would be visible as
this is necessary to get sufficient coverage. Mr. Brimmer asked if there is a
report on what other locations were considered. Mr. Keith Powell, Optima
Towers, answered that they did consider other locations. He stated that the
requested location was considered the best location to provide the coverage
needed. Mr. Brimmer asked for clarification if there is written justification on
the other locations considered. Mr. Powell answered in the affirmative and
stated that this could be provided. Mr. Hill stated that this information was not
provided as it is not required to be submitted with the request and there were
locations that were not viable because of not being able to lease the property.
Mr. Brimmer asked the amount of land needed for the cell tower. Mr. Hill
answered that typically a 100x100 foot area is needed. Mr. Brimmer asked if
any other locations on the golf course were considered. Mr. Hill answered that
the cell tower needs to be in proximity to the residential neighborhoods to
provide the service needed.

   Clearing and Field Inspections

Ms. Farrell stated that an in-depth review of the tree removal process was
presented to the Commission and briefly reviewed this with the Committee

Mr. Brimmer noted that the presentation reinforced the fact that staff is following
the ordinance requirements. He suggested that the ordinances might need to be
reviewed and possible amendments considered.

Ms. Farrell reviewed the Indigo Square project tree removal with the Committee.
She stated that approximately 500 inches of trees would have to be replanted on
the site. Mr. Brimmer suggested that the amount of mitigation should be more
than 500 inches. Ms. Farrell answered that they receive credit for the trees that
would be remaining on the property.

Mr. Carrier asked about the buffer along US17. Ms. Farrell reviewed the buffer
requirements with the Committee.

better protect the trees.
                                                                 Planning Committee

Mr. Cunnane suggested that the Town is more concerned with property rights than
quality of life.

Mr. Brimmer stated that there was a good discussion with the Planning Commission
on tree removal. He asked the purpose of the point system. Mr. Bernard answered
that the point system is based on the American National Standards Institute (ANSI)
system and is used to categorize the desirability, longevity, and species of trees.
Mr. Brimmer asked how the desirability is determined. Mr. Bernard answered that
it can be somewhat subjective, but is categorized based on the national standard.

Mr. Brimmer moved to recommend to Town Council that the issue of tree removal
be discussed with the Planning Commission and brought back to the Planning
Committee within 90 days, including the consideration of the following—
    explore how to maintain more trees on property to create more of a natural
      environment as opposed to a manicured environment;
    review species of protected trees and determine if changes are necessary and
      if other species should be protected;
    review roadside buffers and how they can be strengthened or increased;
    determine different means of mitigation and if more mitigation should be
      required for unprotected or class IV trees;
    Consider stronger penalties for illegal removal and violations;
    simplify ordinance where appropriate.
Mr. Bustos seconded the motion.

Mr. Smith asked if 90 days is a sufficient timeframe given the amount of work staff
is currently working on. Mr. Brimmer answered in the affirmative.

Mr. Brimmer called for a vote on the motion. Motion passed on a 3 to 1 vote with
Mr. Brimmer, Mr. Carrier, and Mr. Smith in favor; Mr. Bustos opposed.

                                                               CLASS IV TREES
 Class IV trees are not protected trees and may be removed without replacement within new residential or commercial developments or from existing
  residential or commercial lots
    - Exception: Pines and Sweet gum are protected within the Natural Roadside, Mathis Ferry, and Critical Line Buffers. Invasive species are not
        protected in these buffers.
 Class IV trees are not protected because of one of the following:
    - They are invasive species.
    - The tree is short lived, the structure of the tree is multi-stem resulting in significant mitigation.
    - The trees were added to the list of non- protected species through an ordinance amendment.
 Invasive Species
    - Callery Pear varieties
    - Mimosa
    - Chinaberry
    - Chinese Tallow (very prevalent in town)
    - Camphor tree (rare in town)
    - White poplar (rare in town)
    - Mulberry (not listed but also invasive)
 Native Species
    - River Birch (on list because they are short lived and usually multi-stem with significant mitigation as a result). Generally not native to Mount
        Pleasant as closest are around some inland rivers in Francis Marion.
    - Pine and Sweet Gum (added to list in early 2000’s)

                                                               CLASS IV TREES
 Class IV trees are not protected trees and may be removed without replacement within new residential or commercial developments or from existing
  residential or commercial lots
    - Exception: Pines and Sweet gum are protected within the Natural Roadside, Mathis Ferry, and Critical Line Buffers. Invasive species are not
        protected in these buffers.
 Class IV trees are not protected because of one of the following:
    - They are invasive species.
    - The tree is short lived, the structure of the tree is multi-stem resulting in significant mitigation.
    - The trees were added to the list of non- protected species through an ordinance amendment.
 Invasive Species
    - Callery Pear varieties
    - Mimosa
    - Chinaberry
    - Chinese Tallow (very prevalent in town)
    - Camphor tree (rare in town)
    - White poplar (rare in town)
    - Mulberry (not listed but also invasive)
 Native Species
    - River Birch (on list because they are short lived and usually multi-stem with significant mitigation as a result). Generally not native to Mount
        Pleasant as closest are around some inland rivers in Francis Marion.
    - Pine and Sweet Gum (added to list in early 2000’s)
