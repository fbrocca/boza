3.   Public Comment

    Mr. Zachary Holcamp, 275 Alexander Drive, spoke regarding the moratorium
on data centers. He suggested that there are negative impacts to the natural water
resources and ecosystem diversity. He stated that data centers can impact
migratory patterns due to noise pollution. He stated that community livability is
another aspect and the number of commercial uses in an area can negatively
impact the aesthetics in residential neighborhoods. He stated that another concern
is energy efficiency as they require a significant amount of energy and water; more
than residential uses.

    Mr. Daniel Brownstein, 1391 Downsbury Drive, spoke regarding data centers.
He suggested that the Town should be proactive and have a public review process.
He suggested that they could be a special exception in some zoning cases and
require an impact assessment that would be reviewed by the Planning Commission
and Town Council. He also suggested that the definition should be clearly defined
to provide protection while not impeding necessary micro centers.

     Mr. Brett Williams, Rifle Range Road, spoke regarding data centers and that he

    Ms. Pam Altman, 3085 Caspian Court, suggested that data centers should be

5.   Discussion of Data Center land use and zoning considerations

     Ms. Reed reviewed this issue with the Committee.

    Ms. Crosby asked where the properties are located that would allow this use.
Ms. Reed answered that the use is allowed in AB-2, ED and LI, and there are not a
lot of properties zoned this that are vacant to develop these centers.

    Ms. Whitaker asked if there is a pending request. Ms. Reed answered in the
negative. Ms. Whitaker asked if the impact assessment would outline required
mitigation. Ms. Reed answered in the affirmative and stated that Town Council
would have to review and approve the impact assessment.

    Mr. Tinkey asked what would be needed to prohibit AI data centers. Mr.
Pagliarini answered that the definition would be critical to ensure that smaller
centers that might be appropriate were not automatically prohibited. He stated
that amendments to the zoning code would require a public hearing by the
Planning Commission and approval by Town Council. Mr. Tinkey agreed that the
definition should be clearly outlined.
                                                                   Planning Committee
                                                                       August 31, 2026
                                                                            Page 4 of 9

    Ms. Hyatt asked if the data centers could be “banned” or prohibited. Mr.
Pagliarini answered that the zoning code could be crafted to define those uses that
would be prohibited. Ms. Hyatt suggested that data centers should only be
permitted in areas zoned Light Industrial. Ms. Reed stated that any changes could
be recommended for the public hearing. Mr. Pagliarini agreed and stated that staff
would propose a draft amendment that would outline the parameters desired by
Town Council that would then proceed to a public hearing. Ms. Hyatt asked about
micro centers. Ms. Reed stated that definitions and parameters that specifically
outline the criteria and thresholds could be accomplished.

    Ms. Crosby asked if they could regulate water consumption, noise, etc. Mr.
Pagliarini answered in the affirmative and stated that this could be included in the
text. He stated that Town Council should also identify those zoning classifications
where these types of data centers would be allowed.

    Mr. Tinkey asked about Mount Pleasant Waterworks’ desire for a micro grid
and storage. Mr. Pagliarini answered that this would be addressed through the
definition and how the ordinance is crafted.

     Ms. Whitaker suggested that the Planning Commission should review the
zoning classifications and provide a recommendation on which zoning
classifications should allow these centers as an allowed use.

    Ms. Whitaker moved to recommend to Town Council approval of having the
Planning commission evaluate the special exception definition and evaluate the
current zoning classifications that allow this as a principal use. Ms. Crosby seconded
the motion.

    Mr. Tinkey stated that he is opposed to the motion as a special exception is
approved by the Board of Zoning Appeals and not Town Council and would be a
“loophole”.

    Ms. Crosby asked if this could be deferred for a period. Ms. Hyatt answered
that this would be discussed with the next agenda item. Mr. Pagliarini answered
that a moratorium could be put in place with a definitive expiration date.

    Mr. Tinkey suggested that a pending ordinance or moratorium should be
considered as well.

B.   R-22.26. Oyster Park PD, Planned Development Amendment. Request
     to amend the Oyster Park PD, Planned Development District Ordinance,
     to increase the maximum building height to 48 feet for certain areas in
     the master plan.

    Mr. Stone reviewed the request with the Committee. He stated that the
Planning Commission recommended approval.

   Ms. Hyatt moved to recommend to Town Council approval of the
amendment. Ms. Crosby seconded the motion.

     Mr. Tinkey asked about the current building and if it would be demolished.
Mr. Stone answered in the affirmative. Mr. Tinkey asked about setbacks and
if a “wedding cake” option should be considered. Mr. Stone answered that
the existing building would be demolished, and a 60-foot buffer would be
required through the 2021 PD amendment. He noted that there would be
parking between the building and the buffer which would essentially provide
a 120-foot buffer between the building and the residential neighborhood. He
stated that there is currently a ten-foot buffer.

   Ms. Hyatt called for a vote on the motion. Motion passed on a 3 to 1 vote
with Ms. Crosby, Ms. Hyatt, and Mr. Tinkey in favor; Ms. Whitaker opposed.
