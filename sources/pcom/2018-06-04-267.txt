2.   Public Comments

Ms. Laurie Bixler, 144 3rd Avenue, expressed concern with proposed changes to
short-term rental regulations. She suggested that there should be an advisory
committee to work on this issue. She stated that she has had short-term rentals
since 2009 before AirBnB was established and has paid the appropriate taxes the
entire time. She agreed that there should be reasonable legislation regarding
short-term rentals. She suggested that those existing short-term rental properties
like hers should be grandfathered in and allowed to remain.

Mr. George Bixler, 144 3rd Avenue, stated that short-term rental regulations should
be accomplished in a sensible and logical manner. He stated that there can be
controlled regulated rentals and asked that short-term rentals be allowed to
continue.

Mr. Kevin Haim, Walk-a-way Stays, stated that he has several short-term rental
properties in the Town. He stated that there are approximately fifty people a day
that are moving to the area as well as events such as weddings occurring on a daily
basis that need temporary housing. He suggested that the short-term rentals
should be allowed but regulated. He stated that he pays all appropriate taxes
associated with the short-term rentals. He suggested that regulations such as
requiring business licenses, taxes, etc. should be considered and implemented for
                                                                  Planning Committee
                                                                         June 4, 2018
                                                                           Page 2 of 9

all short-term rentals. He suggested that the regulation should include
consideration for those that are legally existing.

Ms. Laurie Bauer, 506 Live Oak Dr, stated that she owns a duplex that is rented as
a short-term rental and has done well. She suggested that requiring the owner to
live on the property is too onerous and should not be a requirement. She stated
that she pays all the appropriate sales and accommodation taxes. She expressed
concern with the loss of income if she cannot continue with her short-term rental.
She suggested that food service should not be a requirement to have short-term
rental. She suggested that a different zoning classification could be considered for
short-term rentals.

Ms. Chavonne Wren, 3607 Colonel Vanderhorst, stated that there is a stigma with
having short-term rentals in single family neighborhoods as it would mean more
transients and there would be loud parties. She suggested that short-term rentals
are ideal for those wanting to stay longer than a few days in a hotel such as
grandparents visiting family, stays associated with events such as golf tournaments,
etc. She suggested that limiting short-term rentals for stays of a month or more
might be more appropriate.

7.   SE Spine Institute - Tree Removal adjacent to Mathis Ferry Road (State scenic
     highway) [Per State Law, SECTION 57-23-520, Article 11: Removal of certain
     trees prohibited. No trees in excess of a six-inch caliper located within the
     highway right-of-way of this scenic highway may be removed without the
     permission of the Department of Transportation upon the recommendation
     of the governing council of the Town of Mount Pleasant.]

Mr. Bernard reviewed the request with the Committee.

Mr. Bustos asked the consequence if not approved. Mr. Bernard answered that
Mathis Ferry Road would have to be realigned and the water line being relocated.
Mr. Bustos expressed concern that approval could set a precedent for additional
tree removals on Mathis Ferry Road.

Ms. Whitley asked if these improvements are required. Mr. Bernard answered that
it is required for traffic improvements.

Mr. DeMoura suggested that this could be an opportunity to restore a portion of
the scenic highway. He stated that there are invasive vines and plantings that are
threatening the trees and suggested that this could be an opportunity for
restoration of the buffer in that area.

Mr. Bustos asked how the restoration would be funded. Mr. DeMoura answered
that there are Tree Bank Funds that could be utilized. Mr. Bustos asked what
                                                                  Planning Committee
                                                                         June 4, 2018
                                                                           Page 5 of 9

portion SE Spine would be responsible for. Mr. DeMoura answered that he was
unsure, but suggested that this could be discussed.

Mr. Ulma stated that this would be a recommendation to SCDOT. He stated that
while mitigation could not be planted in the same area, there is an opportunity to
provide some restoration to the area.

Mr. Bustos stated that he would be interested in seeing what the project would
entail.

Mr. Brimmer asked the reason for the request and if a different use on the property
would trigger the same result. Mr. Bernard answered that the transportation
improvements were required due to the development of this site as outlined in the
planned development (PD) document. Mr. DeMoura added that any development
of the site would require the improvements.

Mr. Bustos asked if this could be deferred to next month.

Mr. Ron Crump and Mr. Jerry Trainor, reviewed the request. Mr. Crump stated that
any improvements to the hospital site would trigger these transportation
improvements. He stated that the utilities for the area must be relocated as well.

Mr. Bustos asked if the utility improvements would help correct the drainage issues
in the area. Mr. Crump answered in the affirmative.

Mr. Brimmer asked if different improvements were considered. Mr. Crump
answered in the affirmative and stated that other alignments would further impact
trees in the buffer.

Ms. Whitley asked if there would be mitigation required. Mr. Crump answered that
no mitigation is currently required. Mr. DeMoura answered that some mitigation
would be required; however, removal of the vines would be necessary to protect
the area. Mr. Trainor stated that they are willing to work with staff. He asked that
the approval be given with working with staff on mitigation and restoration.

Mr. O’Rourke agreed that the restoration should be done in conjunction with the
tree removal. Mr. DeMoura suggested that a mitigation plan could possibly be
drafted before Town Council.

NEIGHBORHOOD STANDARDS
Yard
1. Buildings shall be set on the lot relative to the build to zones and setbacks specified for each
   building type.
2. Stoops, chimneys, balconies and bay windows may encroach within the setback or build-to zone.
3. Street walls shall be built on frontage lines as shown.
Porches
1. Shopfront buildings shall have an arcade that extends along 100% of the street façade.
2. Side-yard and All-yard buildings shall have a covered porch a minimum of 8 feet in depth.
3. The porch shall extend a minimum of 50% of the length of the home in the case of Side-yard
   buildings and a minimum of 40% of the street façade in the case of All-yard buildings.
4. The street façade of All-yard buildings shall extend along the front yard a minimum of 40% of the
   lot width.
Outbuildings
1. Outbuildings shall have a maximum footprint of 625 square feet and building height of 18 feet.
2. The walls of the outbuildings at interior property lines shall be left windowless and shall be two-hour
   fire rated.
3. Outbuildings may have less than 3’ setbacks from adjoining property line.
