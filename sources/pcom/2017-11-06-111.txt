b. Request to zone TH, Townhouse District, an approximately 11.99 tract of
   land comprised of 77 parcels, located along Dingle Road, identified by TMS
   No.’s 578-00-00-162, 578-00-00-538 through -559, 578-00-00-595 through -

   Townhomes, Christ Church Subdivision, as depicted on plats recorded by
   the RMC Office in Book L12, Pages 0114 – 0115 and Book L13, Pages 0405 –
   0406. Development is not proposed to be included in the SB-OD,
   Sweetgrass Basket Overlay District.

Ms. Canon reviewed the request with the Committee (attachment 1). She
stated that the Planning Commission recommended approval.

Mr. Brimmer asked the reason for not being included in the SB-OD. Ms. Canon
answered that this is an existing development that was constructed in
Charleston County that is not consistent with the intent nor the required
development patterns of the Overlay.

Mr. Bustos asked if this an existing developed neighborhood. Ms. Canon
answered in the affirmative and stated that the development was started
approximately 2007.

Mr. Carrier noted that this property had previously requested water and sewer
service, but did not wish to annex into the Town at that time.

Mr. Gary Santos, 1690 Greystone Blvd, stated that he is in favor of annexation
of the property, but suggested that the development should be included in the
SB-OD and that he is not in favor of the “75 percent method” of annexation.

Mr. Carrier moved to recommend to Town Council approval of the zoning
request. Mr. Smith seconded the motion. All in favor.

Ms. Farrell reviewed the request with the Committee (attachment 1). She

d. Review of Planning Commission recommendations regarding proposed
   amendments to the Tree and Buffer ordinances.

Ms. Farrell and Ms. Canon reviewed the request with the Committee
(attachment 1).

Mr. Brimmer suggested that this be received as information as it would need to
be presented to Town Council following the election. He stated that he received
correspondence as well from other interested parties wanting to weigh in on
the recommendations.

Mr. Carrier asked about the classification change for pine trees. Ms. Canon
answered that it would mean that a pine tree would be protected and would be
subject to mitigation if removed.
                                                Planning and Development Committee
                                                                   November 6, 2017
                                                                         Page 4 of 6



Mr. Smith asked if the same would be the case for sweet gums. Ms. Canon
answered in the affirmative. Mr. Smith asked how this recommendation was
determined. Mr. Bernard answered that the categorization is based on the ISA
standards and ratings for the Southeast region. He stated that the point system
is based on the ISA recommendations. He stated that this was recommended
to eliminate the potential of an entire lot being “stripped” of all trees. He stated
that the ISA recommends pine trees as a Class I, but this was thought to be too
burdensome and based on local expertise, it should be placed in Category III.
He stated that Sweet Gum are classified by ISA as a Class III tree.

Ms. Pat Sullivan, 1002 Plantation Court, stated that trees provide water
retention on properties and suggested that this should be included in the
formula and for consideration.

Mr. Stuart Whiteside, Seamon, Whiteside, and Associates, stated that the
engineering community would like to have some input on the revisions and
would like use some new developments as test cases for the revisions to
determine if any additional recommendations would be needed.

Mr. Ian Scott, Charleston Metro Chamber, stated that the planning and
engineering resource management community would like to work with Town
Council and staff to help refine and work through the details of the ordinance
revisions.

Mr. Brimmer stated that this issue was brought forward to improve the
developments in the community and preserve trees throughout the Town and
was not intended as a means to control growth.

There being no other comments, Mr. Brimmer stated that this would be
received as information.

Mr. Bustos asked how this issue would proceed. Ms. Farrell stated that direction
would be needed from the Committee on how staff should proceed.

Mr. DeMoura stated that his understanding is that this would be brought back
to the Committee next month for further review and a recommendation. Mr.
Brimmer and the Committee agreed.

4. Review and update from the Housing Committee on the implementation of
   the Housing Task Force recommendations.

Ms. Farrell reviewed the request with the Committee and stated that Mr. Little
would provide an update on the progress of the Workforce Housing Committee.

Mr. Little updated the Committee on the progress of the Workforce Housing
Committee (attachment 1).

Mr. Bustos stated that there is a need for affordable housing, but suggested that
there might not be a need to focus on educating the public on the need for
affordable housing. He suggested that the focus should be on establishing the
501c3 with the education component working in conjunction. Mr. Little stated that
until the 501c3 is implemented, there would be little the Workforce Housing
Committee to do in moving forward with additional affordable housing.

Mr. DeMoura stated that Town Council’s direction was to move forward with the
501c3 and this should be the main focus of the Workforce Housing Committee.
Once the 501c3 is established, then the Workforce Housing Committee could
proceed with other goals and activities such as advocacy.

Mr. Brimmer suggested that the history aspect should not be the focus of the
Workforce Housing Committee. He suggested that there are other entities of the
Town than can work on this aspect.

Dr. Sarah Jacob, 1152 Village Creek Lane, stated that they have discussed that it
should be the Workforce and Senior Housing Committee. She suggested that there
is some confusion between affordable housing and public housing and the
Committee sees this as a significant perception issue that needs to be addressed.

Ms. Sullivan suggested that there is a “huge misconception” of what affordable
housing is and this should be addressed through an advocacy or education
program. She stated that the City of Charleston recently approved a $20 million
bond for affordable housing.

Mr. O’Sullivan suggested that this is more about promotion and not on having
affordable housing in Mount Pleasant. He suggested that in order to have
                                                Planning and Development Committee
                                                                   November 6, 2017
                                                                         Page 6 of 6

affordable housing, there needs to be affordable land. He suggested that
affordable transportation should be a consideration in conjunction with affordable
housing as well.

Mr. Brimmer stated that this would be received as information.

5. Staff Update on the Comprehensive Plan process.

Ms. Boyles updated that Committee on the Comprehensive Plan process
(attachment 1).

Mr. Brimmer thanked the Planning staff for their work over the past two years and
in supporting this Committee. Mr. Smith agreed.

Mr. Smith stated that the Comprehensive Plan process is moving forward and
commended staff for the openness and transparency of the process. He stated that
the engagement from the community and the stakeholders has been exceptional.

Mr. Carrier suggested that residents in the Highway 41 area should attend the
Highway 41 Corridor Improvement Seminar scheduled for November 13th.

There being no further business, the meeting adjourned at 12:17 pm.
