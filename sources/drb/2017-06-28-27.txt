1. 349 Coleman/Kickin Chicken, 349 Coleman, 5170600060, Final Approval of Site
   and Landscape
    Site and Landscape Comments- This submittal is for a plan revision requesting the
    Board’s after the fact approval of a site and landscape change. DOT has denied
    the request for on-street parking closest to Coleman Blvd. as approved previously
    by DRB. The project improvised by removing from the plans both the on-street
    parking along with the curb and gutter, filling in this area with sod and a single
    species of ground cover. What is installed at present and is now up for review
    and approval this month is not in keeping with the quality of design the Town is
    seeking along Coleman Boulevard, arguably the Town’s most design focused
    district. If curb and gutter, components of an urban corridor, are going to be left
    off for a “bioswale”, this swale should be better designed and not left in its
    current state as an afterthought on a prominent corner. Also, there is no buffer
    requirement for this side street but there is a street tree requirement of one 4”
    canopy tree per 100 linear feet along Broadway. Now that on-street parking is
    not present, there is adequate room to fulfill the street tree requirement. At
    present there are three 4” street trees along approximately 270’ feet of Broadway
    frontage. All of this being said, the sidewalk that is supposed to be in the public
    right-of-way in accordance with the code is now inside the property lines of the
    project and any new street trees will need to be located in the public right-of-way
    which DOT may or may not allow at this point. Staff recommends the applicant
    restudy the bioswale area to meet the requirements as set forth in the Town’s
    code of ordinances and to also feature a higher quality of landscape design with a
    resubmittal to staff for final approval.
    Architecture Comments- N/A

2. Sea Island Hamlet, 1047 Chuck Dawley, 532-08-00-001, Final Preliminary Approval
   of Architecture, Site and Landscape
    Site and Landscape Comments-
The residential project on Chuck Dawley Blvd. is before the Board for review and
comments on the activity zone. The submittal has not followed the provided
checklist of submittal requirements and is highly cluttered with information that is
illegible on most sheets. Unrequired information has also been provided that
makes the submittal even more difficult to decipher. Any information not
required by the provided checklist shall be eliminated on the next submittal.
Some checklist items that require fixing include: The project address is not listed
on pages 35-37, the submittal type is not specified on pages 15-20, pages 15-20
and 35-37 need complete dates, building setbacks are not clearly identified on the
site plan, flood zone and silt fencing is not indicated clearly on the grading plan,
the required bufferyard and landscape requirements chart is missing, location of
trees to be saved have not been shown on all plans and individual sheet titles
we’re given. Staff recommends the Board defer the project as there is either not
enough information to properly review the project or the necessary information is
not readable.


Architecture Comments- The applicant has met with staff to discuss this
project. Section 156.318 O.3.a requires the primary entrance of buildings
facing boulevards to have their entrance from the street. As a result, the
end units should appear to front Chuck Dawley Boulevard. The design seeks
to address Chuck Dawley by creating a secondary gable to break down the
massing of the long side of the unit. Doors face this side but the detailing
needs to be refined to create a true primary façade. The second floor doors
are larger than the primary entrance below. Side lights and a transom could
help establish a more prominent entry and focal feature. The second floor
deck is a mere extension of the primary porches that are covered facing the
internal street. The porch should be extended and roofed like a porch.
Ideally both porches should wrap the end unit. While the design has a
strong start to create a nice façade for the end units facing Chuck Dawley, it
should be further refined to establish the primary façade on the boulevard.

The primary facades on the internal street have a strong vernacular design
with subtle variations in material and design to create interest and variety
without appearing too forced. Staff would recommend some further study
to alter the triple gable more in the overall streetscape mixture similar to
    Building C or add more of this unit type throughout. Also, the gable
    projections appear to hang over the driveway without any support. The
    corner columns should appear to support the structure above and be an
    extension of the house to the ground. Staff recommends preliminary
    approval with further study and refinement of details as noted.

   Site and Landscape Comments- Towne Centre wishes to gain additional parking
   by adjusting an existing drive aisle and other parking areas near Barnes and
   Noble. The reformatting of this parking area also features an overhaul of the
   landscaping in the vicinity. Comments include:
      1. No encroachments at all will be allowed into any tree protection zones as
         stated by the Town Arborist. Compacting earth in the protection zones for
         plantation mix installation, digging to install 7 gallon southern yews and
         other proposed work may have a detrimental effect on these trees. The
         59” live oak’s protection zone will also need expansion. As shown there are
         approximately four proposed parking spaces that overlap the edge of the
         tree protection zone. The Town Arborist has recommended that these four
         spaces become a parking island to protect this grand tree. He also noted
         that this tree has been recognized by Town Council as one of importance
         and therefore greater measures shall be taken to ensure its protection and
         health moving forward. Stages, sidewalks and concrete plank pavers will
         not be allowed within the protection zone. The same goes for the 46” live
         oak that shows brick walls and plank pavers in the protection zone.
      2. Contours need to be labeled. Staff cannot determine grading impacts to
         trees.
      3. A schedule of removed trees is needed to assess mitigation. Species, size
         quality and such must be provided for these trees.
      4. Reasoning for removal of trees not directly in the way of proposed parking
         or drive aisles is required.
      5. Proposed sidewalk may not run through tree protection zones to the
         southwest.
   6. Protection zones for numerous trees need to be expanded to their full
      extent.
   7. A great number of parking islands in the vicinity have lost their required

      156.200C4 requires the owner to guarantee all landscaping be of good
