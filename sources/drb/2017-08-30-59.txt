2. Saltwater Cowboys Signage, 130 Mill Street, 517-15-00-018, Final Approval of Signage
    Site and Landscape Comments- n/a

    Architectural Comments—The width of the building has not been provided to
    determine the amount of building sign area allowed. The building width should be
    shown graphically on the application (provide at meeting).
    Section 156.150 Purpose and Findings states:
     (A)(2) To encourage signs that are of a quality design, pleasing in appearance,
    and are appropriate in size, materials, and illumination to the type of activity to
    which they pertain;
        (A)(3) To encourage signs that are compatible with the architectural style,
    characteristics and scale of the building to which they may be attached, and to
    encourage signs that are compatible with adjacent buildings and businesses;
    (B)(6) In furtherance of this objective, such design elements shall be tastefully
    integrated into the architectural design of the building or incorporated into the
    landscape features of the site as approved through the Design Review Process.
    Section 156.155 (J) Roof signs. states “Roof signs shall not project or extend
    above the ridge line of a roof.”

    The proposed signs do not project above the roof line but staff is concerned that
    they do not meet the purpose of the sign ordinance as stated above. The
    photograph of the gazebo element with a roof mounted sign shows room to
    incorporate the sign on the building wall behind in a more aesthetically pleasing
    manner. Placing the sign on the accessory structure is out of keeping with most
    building signs in the Town. The large roof sign on the main building roof could also
    be incorporated on the wall of the building directly below on the main floor level
    wall. Staff recommends that the building signs be located on the wall in areas
    appropriate for sign bands since the building has areas where these could be
    incorporated. The signs must be reduced to meet the allowable sign area. If the
    Board requires the signs to be relocated, future approvals could be handled by
    staff, if desired.

Architectural Comments—The applicant has met with staff to discuss this
design. Similar to other daycare facilities, the large roof creates several
difficulties of scale and proportion for the architect. To address the design
guidelines, the applicant has built on existing details, such as windows,
doors, and stoop coverings to begin to create a rhythm of small gabled
porches and traditional paired window patterns. The result helps to create
focal points on the elevations and minimize the visual impact of the large
roof.

 East Elevation: The East Elevation is the primary street elevation. This is
 also the elevation that should establish the major decorative components
 of the building. The gable element over the center “entry” should be used
 as the primary feature both in terms of design and scale over the South
 Elevation drop off. The smaller flanking gable elements may need to be
 enlarged for better scale on the side of the building. The windows should
 follow an established rhythm also and include some sort of mullion
 possibly a horizontal bar near the top to create a storefront or clerestory
 above the lower windows. All of the windows should follow this triple or
 double design and be located in a regular pattern. Currently, they are
 placed adjacent to doors and in single, double, and triple combinations
 without respect to the overall elevation.
      North Elevation. The sloped rear roof should spring from the eaves line
      across the rear which would result in lowering it a few feet for better
      scale and to be more typical of porch style designs. Staff is unsure how
      the second floor space is actually finished—it appears to be a flat, open
      wall and deck. This area needs further clarification.
      West Elevation. Same comments as the East Elevation
      South Elevation: Study the scale and detailing of the drop off canopy to
      lower the scale and follow the detailing of the East Elevation gable.

    Staff recommends restudy. The comments could easily be incorporated and presented
    for final at a future date.
