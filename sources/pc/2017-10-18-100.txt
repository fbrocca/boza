                               DRAFT MINUTES


Present:       Joseph Wren, Chair, Roy Neal, John McNeill, Rodly Millet, Kathy Smith,
               Alys Campaigne, Peter Lehman, DeAudre Gregg,

         Mr. Wren called the meeting to order at 3:01 pm.

1. Correspondence and Public Comments

There being no public comment, Mr. Wren continued with the agenda.

2. New Business
   A. Continued review and discussion of possible zoning code text amendments
      pertaining to tree retention, protected species, roadside buffers,
      mitigation, enforcement and related matters.

       Ms. Canon introduced Jeffery Ulma, new Director of Planning and
   Development. Mr. Ulma briefly introduced himself to the Commission.

   Ms. Canon reviewed the recommendations agreed upon by the Commission at
   their previous special meeting (attachment 1 & 2).

   Mr. Cuttino entered at this time (3:05 pm).

   Mr. Neal expressed concern with enforcement of tree removals in wetland or
   lower areas. He suggested that if those trees are preserved, there should be a
   credit given. Mr. Bernard stated that these are already protected areas and
   suggested that the premise is to protect trees that are located on developable
   land.

   Ms. Smith suggested that another reason was to maintain and maximize the
   existing forestation on the property.

Mr. Wren asked if the subdivision definition was sufficient from a legal
standpoint. Mr. Pagliarini answered in the affirmative and stated that the
language would be standardized once the ordinance was drafted.

   Ms. Canon reviewed staff comments pertaining to bufferyards with the
Commission.

Mr. McNeill asked the size of tree that would be required. Mr. Bernard
answered that it would be dependent on the use of the abutting property. He
referred to the minimum plant size table and reviewed this with the
Commission.

Mr. Neal suggested that stronger tree preservation and mitigation for
townhomes and multi-family development should be considered in addition to
requiring larger minimum tree sizes.

Mr. Millet asked if the width of the buffer would be discussed as well. Mr.
Bernard answered that the width was not previously identified as an issue, but
could be discussed if desired. Mr. Millet asked how the width of the buffer was
determined in addition to the number of trees required being less if the buffer
is wider. Mr. Bernard answered that if the buffer is wider, then less trees are
needed to mitigate sound, screening, etc.

Ms. Campaigne suggested that the option 2 table would be preferred. She
agreed that the more mature trees would be preferred as well and suggested
that preserving the existing trees on site would be the best mechanism to
achieve this. She suggested that the width options for buffers should remain to
have flexibility.

Ms. Smith agreed that preserving existing trees should be done and suggested
that this would be reviewed with the other staff items. Ms. Canon clarified that
item 8 deals with the special bufferyards and could be discussed at that time.

Mr. Wren asked if class IV species are allowed in buffers. Mr. Bernard answered
that they are not a protected species and do not need to be shown on a survey.
He stated that some credit is given for some class IV species such as pine trees
that are retained on the site to count toward the minimum tree inches required
to be maintained on the site. He stated that the class IV species could not be
used for mitigation.

Mr. McNeill expressed concern with being able to remove trees within a buffer.
Mr. Bernard answered that class IV species in a buffer could be removed but
would count against the number of canopy trees that must be maintained in the
buffer and would require a replacement canopy tree. He stated that for class IV
trees on a property not in a buffer, they would not be required to be shown on
a survey and would not require a permit to be removed nor mitigated if
removed.

Mr. Millet asked about the number of bufferyards in the ordinance and if the
number of bufferyard options could be reduced. Ms. Canon answered that this
has been in place since 1979 and could certainly be a consideration. Mr. Millet
suggested that this should be reviewed and possibly reduced. Mr. Bernard
stated that the different buffer options do correlate with the different uses, with
a stronger and wider buffer being required for more intense uses. Mr. Millet
suggested that the number of buffers allowed should be reviewed and
simplified.

Ms. Smith expressed concern that reducing the number of buffer options could
prevent flexibility. She suggested that maximizing vegetation should be the
focus.

Mr. Millet suggested that reducing the number of buffer options could provide
more consistency in the buffers throughout the town.

Ms. Campaigne asked if Mr. Millet has suggestions on which buffer options
should be removed as an option. Mr. Millet answered that the premise is to
have more vegetation and suggested that it appears the differences between
the buffer options is minimal and therefore some could be eliminated so that
stronger buffer vegetation is required.

    Mr. Neal moved for approval of staff’s recommendation to utilize option 1
for the bufferyard chart and bufferyard planting chart for items 8 and 9. Ms.
Gregg seconded the motion.

Mr. McNeill suggested that item 8 has two parts and suggested that they should
be individually taken. Ms. Canon stated that item 8 pertains to the special
bufferyards.

Mr. Neal suggested that the special buffers are usually addressed during
planned developments, impact assessment, sketch plan approvals, etc. that are
reviewed by the Commission.

Ms. Smith suggested that #8 should be discussed separate from the other issues.

Mr. Pagliarini suggested that the motion could be amended if desired.

Mr. Neal withdrew motion. Ms. Gregg withdrew second.

     Mr. Neal moved for approval of staff’s recommendation under item #9 for
the bufferyard requirements and bufferyard plantings using option 1 with the
requirement of five (5) trees for category B. Ms. Smith seconded the motion. All
in favor.

Ms. Canon reviewed the special buffers with the Commission (attachments 1 &
2).

Ms. Smith suggested that perhaps specific buffers should be determined for
major roadways. Mr. Wren agreed but expressed concern that where the
different buffers end and begin could be an issue, particularly when they are
crossing through different overlay districts.

Mr. McNeill asked what would be staff’s recommendation regarding this issue.
Ms. Canon answered that other municipalities have different buffers for some
major roadways, but the specifics should be determined by the Commission as
it would be forwarded as a recommendation from the Commission.

Mr. Wren asked if pine and sweet gum species are protected in special buffer
areas. Mr. Bernard answered in the affirmative.

Ms. Campaigne asked what the difference would be in having an undisturbed
buffer for some areas. Mr. Bernard answered that it would mean that some
areas would not be able to remove the underbrush. He stated that for
commercial areas this could be a concern in having signage visibility.

Ms. Gregg asked what species would be desired to be kept. Mr. Bernard
answered that the invasive species such as a hackberry or tallow would not be
protected. Ms. Gregg suggested that the invasive, exempt species should be

specified for the buffers. Mr. Bernard stated that there is a national standard
of invasive species for South Carolina that is referenced in the ordinance.

Ms. Campaigne asked if there would be any concern with specifying the buffer
be undisturbed. Mr. Bernard answered that the only undisturbed buffers are
along Mathis Ferry Road and critical line buffers outside the vision corridor. Ms.
Campaigne suggested that the current regulations might be sufficient.

Mr. McNeill asked if the buffers need to be changed. Mr. Bernard answered
that it would be the decision of the Commission. He suggested that some
clarification would be beneficial. Mr. McNeill suggested that staff should bring
a recommendation to the Commission for consideration.

    Ms. Smith moved to recommend for Planning staff to review the current
special bufferyard requirements and bring back recommendations for any
changes to the special bufferyards. Mr. Millet seconded the motion.

Mr. Neal asked for clarification on the motion. Ms. Smith clarified that her
motion was to forward this information to the Planning Committee and for the
Committee to consider and make recommendations for any changes to the
special bufferyards.

Mr. Neal suggested that the Commission should make a decision regarding the
special bufferyards. Mr. Wren stated that the recommendations would be
forwarded to the Planning Committee.

    Ms. Smith amended her motion to include that the recommendation be
forwarded to the Planning Committee as a recommendation from the
Commission. Mr. Millet seconded the amendment.

Ms. Gregg asked if these issues would be reviewed again by the Commission.
Ms. Canon answered that the recommendations would be forwarded to
Planning Committee and Town Council and, once further direction is provided,
text amendments would be drafted and reviewed by the Commission during the
public hearing process. Ms. Gregg asked if staff is recommending changes to
the special buffer areas along major roadways. Ms. Canon answered in the
negative and stated that the information was provided to the Commission for
consideration of a recommendation.

Mr. Neal suggested that the Commission should make a definitive
recommendation on the issue of special buffers so that it can be provided to the

Ms. Smith withdrew her motion and amendment. Mr. Millet withdrew his

Ms. Smith suggested that no changes need to be made with the special buffers.

    Ms. Smith moved to recommend that no changes be made to the natural

Ms. Canon reviewed critical line buffers with the Commission (attachments 1 &
2).

Mr. Wren asked if the critical line buffer is a percentage or absolute. Mr.
Bernard answered that the critical line buffer must be a minimum of 15 feet,
with an average buffer of 35 feet so that there is some flexibility within the
development. He stated that there is 130 foot allowed for a vision corridor.

    Mr. Neal moved to recommend no changes to the critical line buffer. Mr.
Cuttino seconded the motion.

    Ms. Campaigne stated that resilience and shoreline protection is a concern,
but South Carolina is one of the few states that has a Coastal Zone Management
Act and is a model for other states. She suggested that having a critical buffer
at 35 feet should be considered. She suggested that discussion for a shoreline
buffer, as required in Florida, should be considered and discussed during the
Comprehensive Plan process. She suggested that the critical line buffer should
be increased to 35 feet or 50 feet. She suggested that additionally, more ways
to include vegetation between the shoreline and development should be
considered.

Mr. McNeill suggested that the critical line should be a minimum of 50 feet.

Mr. Neal suggested that increasing the buffer could limit the development rights
of a property owner, particularly because there are smaller parcels that could
be rendered undevelopable if a larger buffer was required.

Ms. Campaigne suggested that a 50 foot buffer should be considered.

Mr. Neal withdrew his motion. Mr. Cuttino withdrew her second.

   Ms. Campaigne moved to recommend increasing the critical line to 50 feet.
Mr. McNeill seconded the motion.

Ms. Campaigne asked if shoreline should be included in the motion or
considered as a separate change to the zoning code. Mr. Pagliarini answered
that the recommendation could be made if desired and would be forwarded
along with the other recommendations.

    Mr. Wren called for a vote on the motion. Motion passed on a 8 to 1 with
Ms. Campaigne, Ms. Gregg, Mr. McNeill, Mr. Wren, Ms. Smith, Mr. Lehman, Mr.
Millet, and Mr. Cuttino in favor; Mr. Neal opposed.

     Ms. Campaigne moved to recommend that staff explore a shoreline or
waterfront buffer standard that works in tandem with critical line to increase
resiliency and provide information to the Planning Committee. Mr. Neal
seconded the motion.

Mr. Prause asked the difference between a shoreline buffer and critical line
buffer. Ms. Campaigne answered that there are areas in Florida where the
critical line is undisturbed, but the shoreline buffer would not allow building
within it, but would allow living shoreline projects or natural mitigation that
would not be allowed in the critical line. Mr. Bernard stated that there are areas
where pathways, dock access, vision corridor, etc. that are allowed within the
critical line buffer. Ms. Campaigne suggested that other states are utilizing
different tools and suggested that these should be explored to determine if they
would be beneficial. Ms. Canon suggested that Hilton Head had a similar buffer.

   Mr. Wren called for a vote on the motion. All in favor.

Mr. Neal asked about tree bank payments and what staff’s recommendation
would be if it were to be changed. Mr. Bernard answered that the ordinance
was based on the current market value at that time. He stated that the market
value price has increased.

Mr. Prause suggested that if desired, a definitive cost should not be determined,
but left at the current market value.

Mr. Millet suggested that this was included for discussion to encourage the
developer to plant trees as opposed to paying into the Tree Bank Fund. He
suggested that the potential impact to the single family homeowner should be
considered in determining and changes.

   Ms. Smith moved to recommend that payment into the Tree Bank Fund
should be 1.5 times the current market value per tree. Mr. Neal seconded the
motion. All in favor.

    Mr. McNeill moved to recommend that the current requirement for 200%
replacement for illegal removal should remain in effect. Mr. Millet seconded the
motion. All in favor.

There being no further business, the meeting adjourned at 4:21 pm.
