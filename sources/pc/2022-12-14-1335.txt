D.   Proposal to amend Section 156.326, of Chapter 156, Zoning Code, of the
     Mount Pleasant Code of Ordinances, pertaining to Principal Use
     Conditions. Proposed is to amend the condition associated with density
     for residential uses in commercial districts, specifically to lower the
     maximum density from 12 units per acre to 4 units per acre but allow up
     to 8 units per acre if the additional 4 units are dedicated to attainable
     housing.

     Ms. Reed reviewed staff comments with the Commission.

     Ms. Myra Jones, 730 Dragoon Drive, member of Commission on
Homelessness, asked that the amendment be denied as it would limit the amount
of attainable housing. She stated that the Comprehensive Plans notes that there
is not a diversity of housing stock and supports providing lifestyle housing to better
meet the needs of residents throughout their life cycle as well as the need for
more attainable housing. She suggested that decreasing the density would
increase home values, making them not attainable.

    Ms. Denise Fugo, Housing for All Mt Pleasant Executive Director, stated that
Gregory Ferry development would not be successful without having the additional
density to include attainable housing. She suggested that the majority of those
taking advantage of attainable housing are single and aging women. She stated
that one reason for a shortage in teachers is that there is no attainable housing in
the Town. She asked that the density not be lowered so that more attainable
housing is available throughout the Town.

    Mr. Dan Doyle, representative for Beach Company, asked that the amendment
be denied as it will negatively affect their properties in the future. He suggested
that more attainable housing is needed in the Town. He suggested that more
attainable housing should be allowed not lessened.

    Mr. Allen McMann, 610 Chimney Block Drive, agreed with the previous
speakers and would like the amendment denied. He suggested that if the
attainable housing is reduced, it would mean more traffic, negatively impact
greenspace due to sprawl, and inhibit growth in areas already built out. He
suggested that it is already difficult to hire and keep vital workers for businesses
and schools.

    Mr. Jason Munday, 972 Equestrian Drive, agreed that there should be more
attainable housing so that there is a vibrant workforce. He suggested that smart
growth should be considered as opposed to reducing the amount of attainable
housing. He suggested that there are areas within the Town where more density
makes sense.

   There being no further comments, Ms. Smith closed the public hearing.

   Mr. Davis moved for denial of the request. Mr. Gates seconded the motion.
Motion failed on a 2 to 7 vote with Mr. Davis and Mr. McCormick in favor; Mr.
Smyth, Mr. Bennett, Mr. Koedding, Mr. Lacy, Mr. Gates, Ms. Smith and Ms. Ireland
opposed.

   Ms. Smith moved for approval of the request. Mr. Koedding seconded the
motion.

    Ms. Ireland moved to amend the motion to allow 2 residential units within
commercial districts with an increase up to 10 units per acre if the additional units
are attainable housing as provided under 156.296(A)(3). Mr. Gates seconded the
motion. Motion passed on an 8 to 1 vote with Mr. Smyth, Mr. Bennett, Mr.
Koedding, Mr. Lacy, Mr. Gates, Ms. Smith, Mr. McCormick, and Ms. Ireland in
favor; Mr. Davis opposed.

    Ms. Smith called for a vote on the motion as amended. Motion passed on an
8 to 1 vote with Mr. Smyth, Mr. Bennett, Mr. Koedding, Mr. Lacy, Mr. Gates, Ms.
Smith, Mr. McCormick, and Ms. Ireland in favor; Mr. Davis opposed.
