   lots, located at 651 McCants Drive / Corner of McCants Dr. & Jackson St.

Mr. McNeill asked about lots 4-9 and if access would be from Jackson Street.

Mr. Wren asked about access for lot 10. Mr. Mitchell answered that it would be

Mr. Cuttino asked about the trees near lot 7 and lot 9. Mr. Bernard answered

Mr. McNeill asked who owns the right-of-way. Mr. Bernard answered that they
are state streets.

Ms. Campaigne suggested that SCDOT approval could be stipulated in the
motion.

Mr. John Tarkin reviewed the request with the Commission. He stated that they
would like to preserve as many trees as possible including some pine trees that
have sentimental value.

Mr. Millet asked if the applicant would be amenable to having a sidewalk on
Jackson Street. Mr. Tarkin answered that he is not opposed to a sidewalk, but
if constructed, the sidewalk would not connect to an existing sidewalk and
would mean significant trees would need to be removed.

Ms. Smith stated that staff did not recommend a sidewalk on Jackson Street and
suggested that impervious surface should not be increased in this area.

Mr. Wren asked if the trees in the right-of-way can be preserved. Mr. Tarkin
noted that tree removal is not necessary for installation of driveways. Mr. Tony
Wooddy answered that the main reason for the laurel oak and live oak are being
proposed for removal is because of installation of a sewer line. He stated that

event they needed to be removed.

Mr. Neal asked about the laurel oak by the easement for lot 10 as well as the
other trees between lot 9 and the easement and asked if they can be better
preserved. Mr. Tarkin answered that the easement is 20 feet wide, but the
driveway would only be twelve feet wide and would have pervious material of

D. Request to amend the Vegetation and Tree Protection divisions of Chapter
   156 of the Mount Pleasant Code of Ordinances, pertaining to various
   sections regarding bufferyard requirements, special bufferyards including
   the Critical Line Buffer, and tree protection, removal and replacement
   requirements.

    Ms. Canon reviewed staff comments with the Commission (attachments 1
& 2).

Mr. Wren asked the reason for the 160 inch requirement. Mr. Bernard reviewed
the history of how this requirement was determined.

Mr. Neal asked if the 160 inch requirement would be for residential and if they
do not meet the requirement, they would have to plant additional trees. Mr.
Bernard answered in the affirmative. Mr. Neal asked if trees on HOA property
would count toward the 160 inch requirement. Mr. Bernard answered in the
affirmative. Mr. Neal asked if trees on the perimeter of a pond would count.
Mr. Bernard answered in the affirmative.

Mr. McNeill stated that when this was discussed in October 2017, surrounding
municipalities have the same requirement.

Mr. Bernard reviewed the critical line buffer requirements for Beaufort County
with the Commission.

Ms. Denise Holtz, Charleston Trident Association of Realtors, expressed concern
with property rights and the inclusion of pine trees as a protected species. She
suggested that pine trees should not be protected as they can be hazardous
during storm events. She suggested that tree mitigation for pine trees should
be one-to-one and not 1½. She suggested that pine trees should not be
protected.

Ms. Betty Niermann, Seamon, Whiteside, and Associates, suggested that pine
and sweetgum trees should not be protected. She suggested that if pine trees
are protected it should be specified that only long leaf and white pine trees are
a protected species. She suggested that buffers for duplexes and townhomes
when adjacent to other single family dwellings, vacant land, and streets should
                                                               Planning Commission

not apply. She suggested that trees within wetlands, right-of-way, and ponds
should count toward the 160 inch requirement.

Mr. Mickey Seabrook, 732 Chatter Road, stated that he affirms his comments
from the previous meeting regarding changes to the tree ordinance (attachment
3). He referred to an opinion from Snyder Tree Care regarding the suitability of
pine, pecan and sweetgum trees and read a portion for the Commission. He
stated that over the past few storm events there was significant property
damage to his home due to falling pecan trees.

Ms. Joan Spier, 1848 W. Canning Drive, expressed concern with tree removal for
homes on custom lots and suggested that the tree ordinance should be enforced
and the tree protection zones increased to better protect the trees during
construction. She stated that she is in favor of protecting pine trees in groupings
in common areas where they would not damage homes.

There being no further comments, Mr. Wren closed the public hearing.

Mr. McNeill suggested that the reason for protecting pine trees was to preserve
a predominant species. He stated that he is opposed to clear cutting lots and
suggested that this can only be done through preservation of the pine species.

Mr. Millet suggested that the diameter requirement should be changed so that
more trees are considered protected for all species. He suggested that lowering
the diameter of residential trees that are protected to 13 inch trees would
preserve more trees, including more oak trees.

Ms. Smith suggested that moving the pine trees from category IV to category III
is appropriate for retention of stormwater, mature root systems for better
water quality, etc.

Mr. Wren suggested that the replacement schedule could be changed if desired
to allow less mitigation for existing residential. He asked if pine trees are
protected in buffers. Mr. Bernard answered that only for special buffer areas.

Mr. Neal suggested that the critical line setback should not be 50 feet for all
property. He suggested that different areas should have different critical line

requirements. He suggested that the majority of correspondence was not in
favor of protecting pine trees and suggested that this should be considered.

Ms. Gregg referred to the Comprehensive Plan open house feedback and stated
that the majority of the comments were in favor of protecting existing trees and
buffers. She stated that the Town would be in line with other municipalities if
the 160 inch requirement is applied to residential properties as well as
commercial. She suggested that protection of pine trees is comparable to other
municipalities as well.

Ms. Campaigne stated that based on the correspondence, she has consulted
forestry experts, refuge managers, and studies on long leaf pine trees and storm
events. She stated that there is a difference with the pine tree species and
suggested that there should be specification on which species are protected.
She stated that the studies indicate that there was significantly more damage to
other pine tree species when compared to the long leaf pine species. She
suggested that flooding risk should be lessened and suggested that construction
should not be allowed where there has been 50% or more damage from flooding
and storm events.

Mr. Lehman agreed with Ms. Gregg, Ms. Campaigne, and Ms. Smith’s
comments.

Ms. Smith agreed with Ms. Campaigne regarding the pine species but suggested
that the majority of the pine species are the faster growing species planted for
harvesting. She suggested that trees should be maintained to preserve the
overall tree canopy.

   Ms. Campaigne moved to define the inclusion of pine species as protected to
long leaf pine species. Mr. Neal seconded the motion.

Mr. Neal stated that white pines were also recommended for protection and
asked if this should be included. Ms. Campaigne answered that she is not as
familiar with the white pine tree and suggested that there would be limited
numbers of these types of trees.

Mr. McNeill asked the difference between the pine species. Mr. Bernard
answered that often the surveyors do not know the difference between species.
