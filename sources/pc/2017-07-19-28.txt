   Mr. Robert Hill, representative for T-mobile, explained the need for the cell
   tower. He showed a depiction of the locations of the current cell towers located
   near Rivertowne and the coverage for the area (attachment 3). He stated that
   they had several community meetings regarding this request.

5. Staff Review of Process Pertaining to Tree Removal, Clearing and Field
   Inspections

Mr. Chapman stated that he met with staff concerning this issue to address some
concerns regarding tree removal.

Ms. Farrell reviewed staff’s process regarding tree removal, clearing, and field
inspections through a PowerPoint presentation (attachment 2). Ms. Salomon
reviewed the Municipal Separate Storm Sewer Systems (MS4) review and approval
process with the Commission. Mr. Charlie Hansen reviewed the stormwater review
and National Pollutant Discharge Elimination System (NPDES) approval process
with the Commission (attachment 2).

Ms. Campaigne asked what is a drainage as-built. Ms. Raby answered that as-builts
plans show what has actually been constructed and if it has been correctly installed.

Mr. Cuttino asked how long it takes to receive approval for clearing and grading.
Mr. Hansen answered that it depends on when the MS4 letter is released and
approval is received from the Department of Health and Environmental Control
(DHEC). Mr. Cuttino asked what work can be done prior to issuance of a building
permit. Mr. Hansen answered that only work allowed prior to the clearing and
grading permit is to put the erosion control measures in place.

Mr. Chapman asked how many inspectors are currently employed. Mr. Hansen
answered that for the 265 construction sites, we have there are four inspectors—
two for NPDES and two for pipe infrastructure. Mr. Chapman suggested that more
inspectors are needed.

Mr. Millet asked how removal of preserved trees is handled. Ms. Farrell answered
that when this occurs, it becomes an enforcement issue. She stated that
enforcement is handled in different ways with the most extreme being 200%
mitigation required.

Mr. Mitchell reviewed the final plat approval and building permit process with the
Commission (attachment 2).

Ms. Farrell stated that this review was provided to show the coordination between
staff and the different departments for projects and new construction. She stated
the Commission has purview over tree removal as previously discussed.

Ms. Gregg asked how the public can be better educated on the ordinance
requirements. Ms. Farrell answered that the ordinance is available on the website
for review and that there have been several meetings such as the one tonight to
review the ordinances. She stated that staff is also available to answer any
questions.

Mr. Chapman asked Ms. Farrell to review a recent tree removal issue in Indigo
Square. Ms. Farrell reviewed this with the Commission and stated that it was an
instance where enforcement was required for trees that were removed at the rear
of the site near Wando East/ Wando Lakes. She stated that besides that matter,
                                                                  Planning Commission
                                                                          July 19, 2017
                                                                         Page 11 of 14

many of the trees removed from the site were not protected per the ordinance.
She stated that the mitigation would be planted after infrastructure and building
construction.

Mr. Wren asked about paying into the Tree Bank to meet their mitigation
requirements. Ms. Farrell answered that this can be approved in instances where
there is no room on the lot for the mitigation trees to be planted. She stated that
this is not a usual occurrence.

Ms. Smith asked what purview the Commission has with approval of projects. Ms.
Farrell answered that the Commission has purview over tree removal and open
space. She stated that the Commission should take these requirements into
consideration with approvals of requests. She stated that the Commission would
not be able to extend requirements beyond that allowed by the ordinance. Mr.
Pagliarini agreed and stated that often the question is if conditions can be included
that would be in excess of or above the requirements outlined in the ordinance or
required by state law and stated conditions should not be above that allowed by
ordinance.

Mr. Millet asked if revisions of the tree ordinance are being considered. Ms. Farrell
answered that revisions to the ordinance can be done when it is determined that
they are necessary and there has been discussion with the Planning Committee.
Mr. Millet asked if mitigation can be required by lot. Ms. Farrell answered that as
the individual lot is developed, there could be additional removals and mitigation
required. She stated that the Commission typically sees plans for entire
neighborhoods where the tree removal and mitigation would be required for the
entire neighborhood as opposed to an individual lot.

Mr. McNeill asked what mitigation is required for commercial. Ms. Farrell
answered that commercial is required to retain or plant 160 inches per acre with a
minimum size of four caliper inches. Mr. McNeill asked about residential property.
Ms. Farrell answered that if protected trees are removed, then mitigation would
be required. She stated that often the “clear-cutting” that is seen involves trees
that are not protected such as pine trees.

Ms. Campaigne suggested that the Commission consider the relationship between
the front yard setback in relation to the critical line and critical line buffers. She
                                                                  Planning Commission

suggested that there are instances where the front yard setbacks are causing the
homes to be closer to the critical line, but if the front setbacks were reduced, then
construction could impede less into that critical area.

Mr. Brimmer, 2080 Parsonage Lane, thanked staff for their presentation and
suggested that it shows that staff is enforcing the ordinance requirements. He
stated that he sees where some revisions of the ordinances might be necessary to
better preserve trees within the Town. He asked the reason for different
preservation requirements between commercial and residential development. Ms.
Farrell answered that residential development not only includes development of
an entire neighborhood, but the individual lots as well. She stated that if the same
requirements were applied for an individual homeowner, it could become
burdensome, so Town Council decided to take this into consideration when
enacting the ordinances.

Mr. Wren suggested that the buffer requirements should be reviewed, particularly
when there is infill townhome development. Ms. Farrell answered that there is a
buffer required between townhome developments and existing single family
residential.

Ms. Gregg asked how mitigation is determined. Ms. Farrell answered that a
mitigation plan is submitted indicating the size, species, and location of the
plantings. She stated that any buffer requirements are included as well. She stated
that the mitigation plan is reviewed and approved by staff. Ms. Gregg suggested
that the mitigation species should be reviewed to ensure that there are quality
species being replanted.

Mr. Chapman thanked staff for their work on this issue and for the information that
was provided.

   which is currently comprised of one hole of the golf course and the golf course maintenance facility
   building. The remainder of the property is wooded, undeveloped land.
2. The Applicant is proposing a 130-foot stealth monopole tower, including a building and all ancillary
   equipment.
3. While the 29.46 acre parcel in question is designated for Recreation Open Space within the PD, the
   proposed amendment is specific to this parcel only and is not requested for other parcels with the
   same designation.
4. There was a second amendment to Article 3 Section 3.4 of the Declaration of Covenants,
   Conditions and Restrictions for Parkers Island, dated April 12, 2017, to allow the use of
   telecommunication facilities in addition to the golf club and its associated recreational and social
   activities.
5. The assumption based on the submittal is that with the PD amendment, this use would be a
   Permitted Use and as such, subject to no further conditions or restraints. If allowed as a
   Conditional Use (as is in LI, Light Industrial District) or as a Special Exception (as is in AB, Areawide
   Business District and AB-2, Areawide Business-2 District), the provisions of Zoning Code Sections
   156.117 and 156.118, respectively, would be applicable.

 Class IV trees are not protected trees and may be removed without replacement within new residential or commercial developments or from existing
  residential or commercial lots
    - Exception: Pines and Sweet gum are protected within the Natural Roadside, Mathis Ferry, and Critical Line Buffers. Invasive species are not
        protected in these buffers.
 Class IV trees are not protected because of one of the following:
    - They are invasive species.
    - The tree is short lived, the structure of the tree is multi-stem resulting in significant mitigation.
    - The trees were added to the list of non- protected species through an ordinance amendment.
 Invasive Species
    - Callery Pear varieties
    - Mimosa
    - Chinaberry
    - Chinese Tallow (very prevalent in town)
    - Camphor tree (rare in town)
    - White poplar (rare in town)
    - Mulberry (not listed but also invasive)
 Native Species
    - River Birch (on list because they are short lived and usually multi-stem with significant mitigation as a result). Generally not native to Mount
        Pleasant as closest are around some inland rivers in Francis Marion.
    - Pine and Sweet Gum (added to list in early 2000’s)

    - The tree is short lived, the structure of the tree is multi-stem resulting in significant mitigation.
    - The trees were added to the list of non- protected species through an ordinance amendment.
 Invasive Species
    - Callery Pear varieties
    - Mimosa
    - Chinaberry
    - Chinese Tallow (very prevalent in town)
    - Camphor tree (rare in town)
    - White poplar (rare in town)
    - Mulberry (not listed but also invasive)
 Native Species
    - River Birch (on list because they are short lived and usually multi-stem with significant mitigation as a result). Generally not native to Mount
        Pleasant as closest are around some inland rivers in Francis Marion.
    - Pine and Sweet Gum (added to list in early 2000’s)
