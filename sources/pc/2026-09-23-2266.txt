     A. PUBLIC HEARING, R-27-26, Oakland Plantation PD, Planned
         Development, Request to establish a Townhouse Development within
         Market at Oakland. Staff Report

        Mr. Stone reviewed staff comments with the Commission.

         Mr. Bennett asked about the setbacks for the proposed development. Mr.
     Stone answered that it depends on if the townhome is front loaded or rear
     loaded; if rear loaded, the five-foot setback would be applied.

        Mr. David Yowell, Millstreet Partners, reviewed the request with the
     Commission through a PP presentation.

        Mr. Davis asked the reason for the change in use for these parcels. Mr.
     Yowell answered that they were not involved with the previous applicant.

        Mr. Barber asked about the configuration of the townhomes. Mr. Yowell
     answered that they would predominately be three bedroom units.

         Mr. Gilmore asked additional positive impacts with the development. Mr.
     Yowell answered that this will provide more of a mixed use feel for the Market
     at Oakland and help the area be more vibrant. He also suggested that the town
     homes are a more reasonable price point than a single family home.

         Ms. Harris asked what the impact would be for schools in the area. Mr.
     Yowell answered that given the number of town homes proposed, the impact
     to the local schools would not be significant.

         Mr. Barber asked about traffic impact and if it has been reviewed by staff.
     Mr. Stone answered that an impact assessment was not required for this
     proposal. He stated that the impacts recommended with the previous impact
     assessment were included in the Highway 41 improvements. Mr. Barber asked
                                                             Planning Commission

if staff has reviewed this proposal in terms of traffic. Mr. Stone answered in
the affirmative and stated that no additional improvements were needed. Mr.
Barber questioned that there was less impact for this use. Mr. Gilmore
suggested that it was fewer trips as there are fewer cars in the area. Mr.
Bennett agreed. Mr. Yowell suggested that more detail calculations were
included in the traffic analysis.

    Mr. Davis asked when the development construction would commence.
Mr. Yowell answered that it depends on the review process but estimates in
nine to twelve months.

    Mr. Gilmore asked about sustainability improvements for the site. Mr.
Yowell answered that there is tree preservation, wetland preservation, and
14% green space that is above the required percentage is proposed. Mr.
Gilmore asked if other aspects such as permeable paving, solar roofs, etc.
would be included. Mr. Yowell answered that these will be considered as the
development moves forward. Mr. Gilmore asked about walkability. Mr. Stone
answered that pedestrian connections would be included with Mount Pleasant
Way and/or Highway 41 improvements, which is a joint SCDOT and Town
project. Mr. Gilmore asked about pedestrian access to the commercial uses
and suggested there should be careful consideration for inclusion and
connection.

     Mr. Ed Trevisani, 1192 Sharpstowne Court, and President of Porcher
Preserve HOA, stated that they are in support of the proposal. He suggested
that the proposed 87 town homes would be more compatible than the
previously approved commercial plans. He stated that there is a proposed
reduction in traffic of approximately 60%, which is an important consideration
for Porcher Preserve. He suggested that the information presented would be
more compatible and mean fewer peak hour trips and traffic. He stated that
the proposed improvements to Porcher Bluff Road and the Highway 41
improvements will provide relief in the area needed. He asked for the proposal
be approved.

   Mr. Andrew Harris, 1160 Sharpestowne Court, stated that he supports the
request as well. He stated that the developer is a quality builder with a quality
product that has high aesthetics. He suggested that having the residential
component would help to support the exiting commercial uses. He state that

B.   PUBLIC HEARING, R-28-26, 663 Faulkner Drive, TMS No. 596-15-00-026,
     Request to zone RR, Rural Residential District. Staff Report

     Mr. Stone reviewed staff comments with the Commission.

     There being no applicant, Mr. Bennett moved to public comment.

Being no comments, Mr. Bennett closed public comment.

    Mr. Barber suggested that the RC-2 zoning designated has larger lot
requirements and setbacks. He asked if the RC-2 could be recommended. Mr.
Stone answered that denial of the request for RR zoning would mean that it
could be annexed with RC-2 zoning.

    Ms. Ireland suggested that the density would protect the property given
the acreage of the lot. She suggested that another aspect is that there is not
a lot of roadway frontage as it is already a flag lot in the County.

    Mr. Barber suggested that deviating from the RC-2 zoning would be
deviating from the Comprehensive Plan.

    Mr. Davis asked if there is any concern with it being a flag lot. Mr. Bennett
answered that the flag lot exists in the County. Mr. Stone concurred and stated
that it was configured in 1999.

   Mr. Barber moved for denial. Ms. Ireland seconded the motion. Motion
passed on a 6 to 2 vote with Mr. Bennett, Mr. Barber, Mr. Davis, Mr. Gilmore,
Ms. Mansour and Ms. Ireland in favor; Mr. Smyth and Ms. Harris opposed.

     Ms. Noble reviewed staff comments with the Commission.

     Mr. Bennett asked the elevation of the property. Ms. Noble answered that

    Mr. Barber asked about the three properties adjacent to the proposed
parcel. Ms. Noble showed the location on the site plan for the Commission.
Mr. Barber expressed concern that the lots are not being configured to mirror
the adjacent lots. Mr. Barber asked about the retention pond. Ms. Noble

    Ms. Mansour asked who maintains the private road. Mr. Davis McNair,
applicant, stated that the parcel has the right to utilize the right-of-way in
conjunction with the three adjacent properties. He stated that the HOA would
be responsible for maintenance of the roadway and pond.

    Ms. Ireland asked about LDR waiver and expressed concern with

7.   Discussion of Data Center Land Use and Zoning

     Ms. Reed reviewed the item with the Commission.

    Ms. Ireland asked about Information technology uses and that a data center
could be classified under this category. Ms. Reed answered that they are more
classified as data processing and computing infrastructure uses.

   Mr. Bennett suggested that the definitions need to be carefully considered and
any changes implemented to ensure there are specific parameters.

   Mr. Davis suggested that the Town is moving away from being a residential
town. He suggested that this type of use is not needed.

  Ms. Ireland suggested that there should be some limits regarding size, capacity,
employees, etc. She stated that electrical and water usage is a concern as well as
                                                                 Planning Commission
                                                                  September 23, 2026
                                                                        Page 10 of 11

the amount of noise. She stated that the Town should be mindful on what the
impact of the size of center would be not only for the Town but also the surrounding
areas and neighborhoods.

    Mr. Barber suggested that having them allowed with a special exception should
be considered.

   Ms. Ireland agreed with Mr. Barber but expressed concern with making sure the
parameters are clearly outlined.

    Mr. Gilmore asked if any of the parcels would allow a large data center. Ms.
Reed answered that she was not sure. Mr. Gilmore suggested that a large data
center could not be built in Mount Pleasant. He asked the acceptable size of a data
center. Mr. Pagliarini answered that the zoning restrictions should be reviewed to
determine if they are sufficient and if necessary, further restrict them. He stated
that currently the one megawatt is the national standard at this time. He stated
that noise, pollution, etc. should be reviewed and those parameters and restrictions
put in place to be followed in the future.

    Mr. Bennett suggested that firms such as SPAWAR (now NAVWAR),
BenefitFocus, Blackbaud, etc., should be used as benchmarks.

    Mr. Davis asked the zoning of the property on Faison near the airport. Ms. Reed
answered that it is zoned PD with AB and ED zoning uses allowed. She stated that
another factor is that an impact assessment would be necessary if they meet those
thresholds.

Ms. Reed stated that the use table would be reviewed to determine if some of the
current uses should be broken out.

   Mr. Bennett suggested that another factor is the proximity of property to the
ocean and the possible impacts and additional scrutiny.

   Ms. Ireland suggested that coordination with the County would be beneficial.
She suggested that an overlay district might be another solution particularly for
properties currently in the County jurisdiction.

Mr. Barber suggested that megawatts might not be the only way to restrict them
and that a square footage requirement might be needed as well.
