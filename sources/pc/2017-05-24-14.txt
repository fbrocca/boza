Mr. Neal asked if the fact that the tree survey is more than a year old would
render the submittal incomplete. Mr. Bernard answered that the tree survey
should not be more than one year old, but this project has been in progress for
some time. Mr. Neal asked if the request should be deferred. Mr. Bernard
answered that given the minimal time that has passed, the tree size would not
be significantly different.

Ms. Campaigne asked if any of the 30 trees requested for removal would be of
concern. Mr. Bernard answered that many of the trees not shown on the
preliminary plat were shown on the sketch plan. Additionally, he stated that
some of the building footprints have been changed from the sketch plan as well.
He suggested that it appears that some of the trees requested for removal could
be retained.

Mr. McNeill asked if staff has met with the applicant regarding the
discrepancies. Mr. Bernard answered in the negative.

Mr. Millet asked if a SCDOT encroachment permit was approved. Mr. Bernard
answered that the application indicates that SCDOT approval was received in
August 2016.

Mr. Wren asked about tree mitigation. Mr. Bernard answered that there are
several options including payment into the tree bank, a tree bond, planting in
the HOA area, retention of trees, etc.

Mr. Chapman asked about sidewalks and why the sidewalks in Shell Landing
were not extended. Mr. Bernard answered that the sidewalk would be retained
with the exception of where the proposed access would be located. He stated
that there would be a sidewalk on Rifle Range Road that connects to this
development.

Mr. Matt Cline, Cline Engineering, reviewed the request with the Commission.

Ms. Smith asked the reason for the discrepancy between the sketch plan and
the preliminary plat in regard to trees. Mr. Cline answered that there was a
change in the requirements between the sketch plan approval and preliminary
                                                              Planning Commission

plat request. He stated that additionally, it was determined that based on the
grading and drainage plan, some additional trees would need to be removed.

Mr. Neal expressed concern with the number of trees being requested for
removal that were not previously indicated with the sketch plan approval. Mr.
Neal expressed additional concern with the number of trees being requested
for removal within the proposed right-of-way. He suggested that the lots could
be reconfigured in order to preserve additional trees.

Ms. Campaigne expressed concern with trees #22, #18, #33, #97, #103, and #92
and asked the reason for being removed. Mr. Cline answered that some of the
trees are not protected, while others were in poor condition and reviewed these
with the Commission.

Mr. Millet concurred with Ms. Campaigne’s concerns regarding the number of
trees requested for removal. He asked about tree #17 and #32. Mr. Cline
answered that #17 would be within the building footprint and would be below
the roadway grade once the grading was completed. He stated that tree #32 is
in good condition but requested for removal because of the amount of grading
that would have to occur for that lot. Mr. Millet asked about tree #38. Mr. Cline
answered that it is between two houses in a swale.

Mr. Neal suggested that a number of significant trees in good condition are
proposed for removal and asked if this could be reviewed to determine if more
trees could be preserved. Mr. Cline answered that the applicant has worked
extensively with staff and asked that a decision be made on the request.

Ms. Gregg asked for clarification on what would be accomplished with “working
with staff on the tree survey”. Mr. Cline answered that he was not a part of the
project at the beginning and stated that he would like to have an opportunity to
work with staff.

Ms. Smith moved for denial of the request. Mr. Wren seconded the motion.

Mr. Wren asked the difference between a deferral and denial of a preliminary
plat. Mr. Pagliarini answered that the denial of the preliminary plat would mean
the applicant would have to bring back a different project layout for approval.

applicant to have an opportunity to bring a different plan back for approval.

Ms. Campaigne suggested that there are a number of protected trees that are
of concern and suggested that the concerns should be addressed before a
decision is made. Ms. Smith and Mr. Wren agreed.

Mr. McNeill suggested that one reason for an updated tree survey is to provide
information to address some of the concerns raised by the Commission. He

Mr. Chapman called for a vote on the motion. All in favor.

between this project and Covington via Wagner Road. He suggested that if this
could be accomplished, it could mean one less curb cut on Bessemer Road. Mr.
Mitchell answered that interconnectivity from Covington to this project was not
accounted for when Covington was approved for development. He suggested
that eliminating the curb cut on Bessemer Road would not accomplish diverting
traffic; having both the access on Bessemer and through Covington would be
preferred.

Ms. Sarah Hamblin, Seamon, Whiteside, and Associates, reviewed the request
with the Commission.

Mr. Chapman suggested that the mail kiosk should be relocated to prevent a
traffic hazard at the ingress/egress. Ms. Hamblin answered that this could be
reviewed.

Mr. Neal thanked the applicant for the addition of the sidewalk. He asked the
reason for removal of tree #30. Ms. Hamblin answered that it is in poor
condition and in a drainage easement. Mr. Neal asked about tree #32. Ms.
Hamblin answered that it is in poor condition and would be affected by
drainage. Mr. Neal asked if the tree was located in the buffer. Ms. Hamblin
answered in the affirmative. Mr. Neal suggested that it should be preserved.
Ms. Hamblin answered that this could be considered. Mr. Neal asked about tree
#18. Ms. Hamblin answered that it is in a utility easement. Mr. Neal suggested
that this could be preserved as well. Mr. Kevin Steelman answered that #32
could be preserved with restudy of tree #30.

Mr. McNeill asked how the trees would be mitigated given the significant
number of mitigation inches required. Mr. Steelman answered that there is
adjacent HOA property where a significant number of trees could be planted.
Mr. McNeill expressed concern with the typical “clear cutting of property” for
development and suggested that as many trees should be preserved as possible.
Mr. Steelman agreed and stated that there are increased drainage requirements
from the state that requires more and more grading to be accomplished which
further impacts development and preservation of trees. He stated that this in
turn causes more clear cutting of property.

Mr. Steelman stated that they would like to tie into the sidewalk already
approved and planned for the adjacent development in order to not duplicate
sidewalks on Bessemer Road.

Mr. Chapman suggested that the sidewalk should be extended to the other side
of the pond. Mr. Steelman answered that this could be accomplished and would
provide a destination to the pond. He suggested that a crossover could then be
provided to connect to the sidewalk on the other side of Bessemer Road.

Ms. Gregg asked if a pathway could be provided for the remaining portion of the
property where the HOA property and tree mitigation is proposed. Mr.
Steelman answered that there are buffers between properties, so a path might
not be possible. Ms. Gregg asked how some of the mitigation would be
achieved. Mr. Steelman answered that some of the trees within the buffers
could count toward the mitigation required. Mr. Bernard clarified how the tree
mitigation is determined and that the mitigation required is based only on the
project acreage.

Ms. Campaigne expressed concern with not extending the sidewalk along
Bessemer Road. She suggested that the amount of traffic could be a pedestrian
safety issue. Mr. Steelman answered that this could be considered and
potentially completed by the HOA. Ms. Campaigne suggested that a pervious
pathway or bike path should be considered. Mr. Steelman answered that this
could be considered.

Ms. Smith expressed concern with not having traditional sidewalks. She
suggested that the sidewalk should be required to connect to Larch Lane.

excluding staff’s comment to extend the sidewalk to the lake with following
conditions:
    Save trees #30 and #32;
    move kiosk deeper into the subdivision and coordinate with staff on an
      appropriate location;
    sidewalk should connect from Covington to the lake as suggested by
      applicant.

Mr. Neal stated that he would rather have a natural path for the remaining area
from the lake to Larch Lane given the topography of the area. He suggested that
an asphalt or gravel pathway would not be appropriate.

proposed sidewalk to connect with the sidewalk on the other side of Bessemer
Road. Mr. Neal seconded the motion.

Mr. Mitchell stated that the Transportation Department approves the location
of crosswalks and stated that given the location of the intersection, a crosswalk
would likely not be approved at this location.

Mr. McNeill withdrew his amendment. Mr. Neal withdrew his second.

Ms. Campaigne suggested that the applicant leverage their influence to request
the extension and improvements for a sidewalk from the proposed sidewalk to
Larch Lane. Mr. Mitchell clarified that the sidewalk would be in the right-of-
way.

Ms. Campaigne moved to amend the motion to direct the applicant discuss with
the HOA the option of applying for an encroachment permit and funding the
additional sidewalk portion as suggested by Mr. Steelman.

Mr. Pagliarini stated that the Commission cannot condition the approval based
on the action of a third party. Ms. Campaigne withdrew her motion.

Ms. Smith expressed concern with the project, the removal of trees and tree
mitigation that would be required. She stated that based on this, she would not
be supporting this project.

Ms. Gregg asked for clarification on what purview the Commission would have
regarding the sidewalks. Mr. Chapman answered that during the recent
changes to the Dunes West/Park West PDs, a portion of this property was
deeded to the HOA. He stated that the Commission cannot bind the HOA as a
third party entity to complete the sidewalks as the HOA is not the applicant.

Mr. McNeill suggested that Mr. Steelman has controlling interest of the HOA.
Mr. Pagliarini answered that the HOA is a separate entity and cannot be
subjected to conditions regarding this request.

Mr. Millet moved to convene into executive session for legal advice. Ms. Smith
seconded the motion. All in favor.
