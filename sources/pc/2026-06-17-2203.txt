B.    PUBLIC HEARING, R-16-26, Gregorie Ferry Towns, TMS No. 580-00-00-319,
      Planned Development Amendment request for Gregorie Ferry Towns,
      Attainable Housing to modify AMI, Area Median Income Limits. Staff
      Report

     Mr. Stone reviewed staff comments with the Commission.

    Mr. Bennett asked how the AMI is calculated and if it is adjusted over time.
Mr. Stone answered that typically there is a range between 80%-120% AMI. Mr.
Bennett asked if it periodically adjusted. Mr. Stone answered in the affirmative
and stated that currently the planned development stipulates a cap of 80% AMI.

    Mr. Benjamin Brown, Gregory Ferry Towns HOA president, reviewed the
request with the Commission.

     Mr. Bennett asked if the HOA voted for approval. Mr. Brown answered in the
affirmative. Mr. Bennett asked the rationale for the one opposition. Mr. Brown
answered that he is not sure the reason for the one owner being opposed. Ms.
Erin Stampe stated that she did speak with the one in opposition, and there were
some language barrier issues that once resolved, he was comfortable with the
request.

     Mr. Berry asked if all of the owners are under the 80% AMI. Mr. Brown
answered in the negative and stated that there are some units that had different
requirements. He stated that this request would mean that all of the units would
have the same AMI requirements. Mr. Berry asked if there are different ranges
for dual income households. Mr. Brown answered in the negative.

     Mr. Barber asked if raising the AMI defeats the intent of the attainable
housing. Ms. Ireland suggested that having a bigger range allows for more
families to qualify for the housing. Mr. Brown stated that the goal is to have the
attainable housing and having the larger range allows for a larger price point so
those families moving on to their second home can have a larger profit margin to
help them be able to purchase a larger home for their needs.

    Ms. Ireland asked if only a certain profit margin is allowed or a maximum sale
price. Mr. Brown answered that there are restrictions on the sale price of the
housing and that only a certain percentage increase is allowed.

   Mr. Barber asked what the original price point was for the homes. Mr. Brown
answered that the cost of the dwellings varies.

    Ms. Ireland suggested that there is a formula for calculating the sale price.
Mr. Brown agreed. Ms. Ireland suggested that increasing to the 150% AMI is a
reasonable request.

   Mr. Barber asked what the sale price would be if sold at market rate. Mr.
Brown answered that he is not sure.

    Mr. Berry asked if there is a non-profit agency that calculates the sale price.
Mr. Brown answered that it is based on the HUD requirements.

7.   Discussion and possible recommendations to amend Chapter 155, Land
     Development Regulations (LDR).

     Mr. Mitchell reviewed the recommendations with the Commission.

    Ms. Ireland stated that her reason for considering changes to the LDR was to have
a stop-gap for the historic settlement community areas while they complete their
character appraisals and put the historic overlay district in place.

   Mr. Stone reviewed the history and culmination of the historic overlay district with
the Commission.

    Mr. Bennett suggested that working with the historic communities to try and find
a solution would be beneficial.

    Ms. Ireland stated that she has some relief since this was first approached because
of the efforts of the Commission and Town Council to deny and/or delay approvals
until the County and the Town were able to approve the overlay district.

     The Commission discussed if there is progress in moving forward with the
settlement communities character appraisals and that the County and the Town
would eventually have some guidelines in place for preservation of these areas.

    Ms. Ireland asked if a governing body has been decided to date. Mr. Pagliarini
answered that until the character analysis is completed for each community, there is
no need to establish a governing body. He also stated that it is not legal to have a
joint commission between the county and the Town.

    Ms. Reed stated that Philips, Hamlin Beach, and Ten Mile communities all have
historic designation. She stated that the character appraisals for these communities
are needed in order to have a basis to set requirements within those communities.

    Mr. Barber asked if the zoning defers to the historic commission guidelines. Ms.
Reed answered in the negative and stated that the County does not have jurisdiction
over properties in the Town in the overlay district area. Properties in the Town would
have to abide by the zoning overlay district for that area. Mr. Barber asked if a
governing body is needed to review details such as character. Ms. Reed answered in
the affirmative, but the character appraisal is needed in order to put those guidelines
and regulations appropriate for that area in place. She stated that once the character
                                                           Planning Commission Agenda
                                                                        June 17, 2026
                                                                            Page 8 of 8

appraisal is completed and approved, then those can be added to the overlay district
as guidelines.

   Ms. Reed stated that the majority of the properties in these communities are
under county jurisdiction and the Town does not have jurisdiction.

    Mr. Pagliarini stated that currently the most effective means for preservation of
the communities is to deny annexation. Once the character appraisals are completed
and approved, then those guidelines can be incorporated.

    Mr. Bennett asked if denying annexation or rezoning puts the Town at some legal
risk. Mr. Pagliarini answered that annexation should be approved before the zoning
is assigned and approved. He stated that not assigning a zoning for a property could
be problematic as the property could not be developed.

The Commission agreed that the best course of action at this time is to continue to
recommend denial of the zoning while these different character appraisals are
completed and the Town has more information on what the different parameters
could be for the different communities.
