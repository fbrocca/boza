B. Proposal to amend Section 156.201(I)(2)(c), of Chapter 156, Zoning Code,
   of the Mount Pleasant Code of Ordinances pertaining to the vision corridor
   provision of the Critical Line Buffer. Proposed changes include language
   clarifying that the vision corridor provision only applies to residential
   property; clearing cannot occur for the benefit of an adjoining property
   owner; the 33 percent clearing requirement cannot be incremental and
   must remain unified; and how the Critical Line Buffer and Vision Corridor is
   measured.

    Ms. Canon and Mr. Bernard reviewed staff comments with the Commission
(attachment 1).

Mr. Cap Prescott, 213 South Plaza Court, stated that the Renaissance is located
in the Waterfront Gateway District that was established to provide enhanced
areas along the waterfront and expand water access. He suggested that the
proposed change would be a significant change and would be against the
Comprehensive Plan recommendation and the intent of the Waterfront
Gateway District. He suggested that the vision corridor is vertical vision and not
horizontal.

Mr. Shaun Willis, attorney for I’On HOA, showed a map of the I’On buffer for the
Commission. He suggested that because of the configuration of the HOA buffer,
there would only be allowed one section for the view corridor and would
mean that some of the residents would not be able to take advantage.

Mr. Lee Gastley, SWA, agrees that there should be a better way to measure
the vision corridor. He stated that there should be flexibility in the way the

   administered and should be allowed to be broken up into sections, particularly
   where the buffer is lengthy and expands a larger piece of property. He also
   suggested that the vision corridor should be allowed for commercial properties
   as well.

   Mr. Sid Boone, attorney for Nimlorp, suggested that commercial properties
   should be allowed to have a vision corridor. He suggested that because the
   stumps of the trees are required to remain, clearing for a vision corridor would
   not cause a runoff issue. He asked that the proposed text be denied and revised
   to include commercial property and that those with existing vision corridors be
   able to maintain them.

   Ms. LeAnn Adkins, 34 Hopetown Rd, stated that the ordinance established in
   January 2000 allows for multiple corridors as long as it does not exceed 33%.
   She suggested that the intent is better met if multiple sections are allowed as
   opposed to only one section.

   There being no further comments, Mr. Wren closed the public hearing.

       Mr. Gates moved for deferral to the next meeting to allow staff and
   participants to revise the text so that it is better clarified and easier to
   understand. Mr. Wren seconded the motion.

   The Commission discussed what changes to the proposed text should be
   considered, whether the vision corridor should be allowed for commercial
   property, and how vision corridors on HOA property should be handled.

      Mr. Wren called for a vote on the motion. All in favor.
