1. Current Request: This request is to annex and zone PD-CD, Planned Development—Conservation

2. Current Zoning: Subject parcels are currently unincorporated and zoned S-3, Special Management
   District, in Charleston County, which allows for a maximum of three dwelling units per acre. Two of
   the parcels appear to have single-family dwellings.
3. Density: As noted in the Relationship to Comprehensive Plan section above, the Future Land Use
   Map recommendation is Community Conservation. Standard residential density associated with
   this designation is three dwelling units per acre, which results in a maximum of 23 dwelling units;
   correspondingly, the PD proposes 23 dwelling units. The PD cannot modify maximum density, but
   can limit the overall number of dwelling units.


Staff Report – Farm at Oyster Point (FKA Farm at John Ballam)                              Page 4 of 8
4. Comprehensive Plan: The Future Land Use Map recommendation of the Comprehensive Plan
   guides density, but the stated goals and strategies in the Comprehensive Plan should also be
   considered in conjunction with a zoning request. For instance, the Comprehensive Plan specifically
   identifies annexation as an important goal, but it also identifies goals such as preservation of
   character of existing neighborhoods and compatibility with surrounding development patterns. So
   while the PD-CD District is recognized as an appropriate zoning district in Community Conservation,
   its application must support the goals and intent of the district. The development submitted with
   this zoning request is not consistent with the goals and strategies that support the Community
   Conservation land use designation, as further explained below and throughout the staff report.
       a. The primary purpose of this land use designation is to protect and recognize the importance
          of the unique development characteristics of historic African-American areas in the
          planning area and sustain their strong sense of community. Future development in these
          areas should be compatible with the existing land uses and development patterns.
       b. The primary purpose of the PD-CD District is to perpetuate low-density rural character,
          preserve and protect natural resources and sensitive areas, promote agricultural pursuits,
          and balance the urban environment.
       c. Under the current Charleston County zoning, minimum lot size in the S-3 District is 12,500
          sq. ft. and the minimum lot width is 70 feet. If the parcels were annexed in under the
          recommended CC zoning, likewise the lot size would be 12,500 sq. ft. with 80 foot minimum
          lot width.
       d. Conversely, the proposed PD-CD regulations would allow 6,000 sq. ft. lot size and 50 foot lot
          width. These types of development standards represent a significant difference in lot sizes
          and lot widths from the CC and S-3 zoning districts. While the smaller lot size is allowed in
          the PD-CD District, its purpose is to incorporate cluster development patterns.
5. Design: The PD-CD District requires incorporation of cluster development patterns. Zoning Code
   Section 156.305 (H) (2) (b) 1. a. and b. state: “a. This is a development design technique that
   concentrates buildings in specific areas on a site to allow the remaining land to be used for
   recreation, common open space, and preservation of environmentally sensitive areas. b. No
   minimum lot area allows creative site layout.”
   The proposed development does not comply with the requirements, nor is it in keeping with the
   overall intent of the PD-CD District. Generally speaking, properties best served by a PD-CD and
   cluster design techniques are those that are larger in size and include unique topographical
   features, large historic trees, wetlands, and historical features, as the zoning district allows
   opportunities for a unique design to preserve these sensitive features. What is proposed is the
   development of a seven and a half acre tract that contains no wetlands, has a limited number of
   historical trees, and was previously developed with two single family dwellings. The only feature
   that potentially provides for a unique site is its proximity to the Confederate Fortification Line and
   Palmetto Fort Trail, though it appears neither are directly on site. During preliminary review of the
   proposed plan, Staff expressed concern that the conceptual layout did not meet the intent of the
   PD-CD and that redesign should be considered. No changes were offered with this submittal. While
   it is not the role of Staff to design the site, consideration could have been given to the following to
   better incorporate certain elements of a PD-CD.
