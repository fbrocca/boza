6.   Requests
     A. Zoning Case: R-13-23 , Request to Rezone CC, Community Conservation
         a 1.25- acre incorporated parcel, located on Joseph Glover Road,
         identified by TMS# 583-00-00-276. Property is currently zoned CO,
         Conservation- Open Space District.

          Mr. Stone reviewed staff comments with the Commission.

          The applicant not being present, Ms. Smith moved to the public hearing.

        Mr. Jonathan Ford, Philips Community, stated that they are not in favor of
     any zoning that would not be compatible with settlement community.

     There being no further comments, Ms. Smith closed the public hearing.

         Ms. Ireland moved for approval. Mr. Lacy seconded the motion. All in
     favor.

     B.    Zoning Case: R-14-23 , Request to Annex and Zone R-2, Low-Density
           Residential District, an approximately 3.45-acre parcel, at the terminus
           of Ida Road, identified by TMS No. 577-00-00-090. Further Indicated on
           a plat recorded by the Charleston County ROD Office in Plat Book ED,
           Page 949.

          Mr. Stone reviewed staff comments with the Commission.

        Mr. William Wallace, Engineer for applicant, reviewed the request with the
     Commission.

         Ms. Cassandra Johnson-Davis, 1593 Hamlin Rd, Hamlin Board secretary,
     stated that she is opposed to the rezoning request. She stated that it is a
     settlement community and the Town is working with the Gullah Geechee
     Heritage to have the community designated as a historic settlement
     community. She suggested that the R-2 zoning would not be in alignment with
     the current character of the community and would negatively impact the
     traffic in the area. She suggested that the property should be developed in
     keeping with the community.

    Ms. Myra Snipe Richardson 2755 Earl Johnson Lane, acting president of
Hamlin community, stated that the community is opposed to the rezoning
request. She suggested that the development would negatively affect the
neighborhood by causing drainage issues. She suggested that adjacent
property would be affected because the proposed development would require
an easement to connect to sewer. She suggested that a more detailed plan
should be provided before approving the request.

    Mr. George Freeman, 4050 Bowman Rd, suggested that there was an
agreement between the county and the Town to help preserve this historic
community. He suggested that the agreement should be upheld and the lots
should remain as 12,500 square foot lots.

    Ms. Kelly Hall, 1522 Boston Grill Rd, expressed concern that the
development would increase traffic.

     There being no comments, Ms. Smith closed the public hearing.

   Ms. Smith moved for denial of the request. Ms. Ireland seconded the
motion. All in favor.

C.    Zoning Case: R-15-23 , Request to Rezone R-2, Low-Density Residential
      District, an approximately 0.45- acre parcel, located at 242 6th Avenue,
      identified by TMS No. 514-10-00-033. Further indicated on a plat
      recorded by the Charleston County ROD Office in Plat Book D, Page 180.
      The property is currently zoned R-1, Low-Density Residential District.

     Mr. Stone reviewed staff comments with the Commission.

     The applicant declined the opportunity to provide a presentation.

    Mr. Ed Lee, 150 6th Avenue, stated that he is opposed to the request. He
stated that the request would not be in keeping with the character of the
neighborhood and how it was originally designed. He suggested that this
would set a precedent for other lots to request similar zoning. He also
suggested that there have been instances where property has been taken
from owners and suggested that all claims should be carefully reviewed prior
to any requests being reviewed.

    Ms. Jacquelin Gore, 308 6th Ave, stated that the Scanlonville neighborhood
is the original settlement community and asked the Commission to preserve
the layout of the neighborhood as it was originally designed as half acre lots.

    Ms. Evetta Richardson, 228 6th Ave, stated that she is opposed to the
rezoning request. She suggested that the character of the neighborhood has
been changed by installation of landscaping and fencing. She suggested that
both the main home and ADU are rented.

    Mr. Eric Vincent, owner, clarified that they do live in the main home. He
suggested that they have tried to maintain the property to help preserve the
aesthetics of the property and maintain the character of the neighborhood.

    Ms. Diane Vincent, owner, stated that there is not a fence or bamboo in
the front of the home. She suggested that they would like to provide a home
for themselves when not visiting their children.

    Ms. Cynthia Vanderhorst, 243 6th Avenue, suggested that it appears that
the owners have only recently been parking their vehicles on the property and
possibly living there. She suggested that it has been used as a short-term
rental with multiple vehicles on the property. She suggested that the property
was purchased strictly for profit.

    Ms. Cassandra Johnson-Davis, 1593 Hamlin, stated that she is opposed to
the rezoning as it would mean that the property could possibly be sold off.

   There being no further comments, Ms. Smith closed the public hearing.

   Ms. Smith moved for denial of the request. Mr. Lacy seconded the motion.

Mr. Stone noted that as allowed under the Commission’s by-laws, the
applicant does have an opportunity for rebuttal. Ms. Smith asked the Vincents
if they would like to provide any final comments.

    Mr. and Ms. Vincent clarified that they are living on the property and that
the art school is no longer located on the property. Mr. Vincent stated there
have been few occasions where a number of vehicles on the property.

   Ms. Smith moved for approval of the request. Mr. Koedding seconded the
motion. All in favor.

F.    Proposal to amend Chapters 152 and 155 of the Mount Pleasant Code
      of Ordinances, pertaining to newly established land development
      standards to encourage building and development practices for
      subdivisions of land, and for construction of new homes. Specifically
      proposed is to establish in the Land Development Regulations a 10’
      setback from the perimeter of the subdivision that would prohibit fill
      and grading, that could cast stormwater on to adjacent properties. Also
      proposed is an amendment to the Flood Damage Prevention regulations
      that would modify Building Practices in Flood Zones and Low-Lying
      Areas, prohibiting the construction of slab on grade type of foundations
      for new residential construction.

   Mr. Mitchell and Ms. Gerling reviewed staff comments with the
Commission.
