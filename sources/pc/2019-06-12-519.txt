3.Public Comment

   Mr. Ivan Milano stated that he is opposed to short-term rentals. He
suggested that short-term rentals can have a positive economic impact on the
Town. He suggested that the rentals do not need to be regulated and the fees
are too exorbitant. He suggested that the HOAs should be responsible for
regulating where short-term rentals are allowed. He suggested that requiring a
business license ahead of the regulations is unfair as the regulations are
currently not in place.

Ms. Christina Carl, 660 Bank St, stated that she is in favor of licensing, but
suggested that the fees should be reconsidered, particularly because of other
fees that are assessed. She expressed concern with having the 1% maximum.
Mr. Kevin High, Walkway Stays, suggested that the multi-family units should not
be restricted from having short-term rentals. He suggested that the HOAs
should regulate where short-term rentals are allowed. He suggested that
allowing access to the home should be reconsidered and that this could be
unconstitutional. He suggested that a permit should not be required.

Mr. Trey Tezza, Mount Pleasant property owner, suggested that how the 1%
maximum is determined should be reviewed. He stated that short-term rentals
help supplement his income.

Mr. Mark Sanford, King Street, suggested that it is important to have the
opportunity to have short-term rentals. He suggested that the home owner
should be able to be the steward of their home. He agreed that there should
be reasonable regulation of short-term rentals.

Ms. Alicia Lockwood, 316 Lapwing Lane, stated that she has a multi-family unit
and would like to be able to have short-term rentals. She suggested that there
are not problems with short-term rentals other than wanting to increase
revenue and controlling potential overgrowth.

Ms. Sarah Jacob, 1152 Village Creek Lane, stated that affordable housing should
be a consideration when looking at short-term rental regulations. She
suggested that short-term rentals eliminate property that could be utilized for
affordable housing.

Mr. Kenny Craft, 204 Spooner Lane, suggested that there is a perceived
problem with short-term rentals and not an actual problem. He suggested that
the parking requirements should be reviewed as they seem extreme. He
suggested that there should be allowance for existing short-term rentals.

Mr. Alec Graham, 1321 Belleview Drive, stated that he is in favor of short-term
rentals. He suggested that there should be a fair and equitable comparison
between the number short-term rentals and the number of hotel rooms and
apartments in the Town.

Mr. Zac Moore, 1041 Marsh Cove Lane, suggested that there has not been a
complaint of short-term rentals. He suggested that the regulation of short-term
rental is due to trying to regulate hotel use. He suggested that only a few
members of Town Council see that short-term rental is an issue for the Town.
He suggested that because of the differences between each short-term rental,
it would be difficult to regulate them.
Ms. Karen Keys, Turnkey rentals, stated that she has short-term rentals in
townhomes and there have not been any complaints. She suggested that they
should be allowed to continue to have short-term rentals. She expressed
concern with having a 1% maximum for short term rentals.

Ms. Denise Milano, Charleston, suggested that a bed and breakfast should not
be regulated. She suggested that the HOAs should be allowed to regulate
short-term rentals.

Mr. Bill Macchio, Mount Pleasant Magazine, suggested that having short term
rentals provides additional economic revenue for the Town. He suggested that
there should be regulations and reasonable fees.

Mr. Badge Humphries, 634 Williamson Dr, stated that he is supportive of short-
term rentals. He suggested that the regulation of short-term rentals is
overstepping the authority of the Town. He suggested that the regulations
could be an infringement on property owner rights and HOA rights.

Mr. Thomas Tisdale, 211 6th Avenue, asked for clarification on how access to
the home would be regulated and expressed concern that there would not be
advance notice for access to the property. He asked what would be considered
an emergency and how the response would be regulated.

Mr. Ben Stanio stated that he is in favor of short-term rentals. He suggested
that HOAs should be held responsible for regulating short term rentals.

Mr. John Clark, 521 Pelzer Drive, stated that he has a new short-term rental
that has done well the past few months. He suggested that the short-term
rentals add to the economic revenue for the Town. He suggested that the
regulations should be as user friendly as possible.

4.Requests
   A.Text amendment: Proposal to amend chapter 156 of the Mount Pleasant
      Code of Ordinances by adding a new section entitled Short Term
      Rentals. The purposed of this new section is to define and regulate the
      use of residential units as short-term rentals in order to protect the
      integrity and character of the Towns existing neighborhoods, as well as
      the health and safety of occupant of short-term rental units.

     Ms. Canon reviewed the request with the Commission.

  Mr. McNeill asked the reason for generating the ordinance. Ms. Canon
  answered that the original intent of accessory dwelling units (ADUs) was to
provide affordable housing for the Town; however, they have evolved more
for short-term rentals. She stated that this discussion continued, and it
became a question of whether they should be regulated. Mr. McNeill
suggested that there is not a justified reason for the ordinance.

Mr. Neal suggested that the base fee appears to have an excess of revenue.

Mr. Wren asked if other municipalities have full-time employees. Ms. Canon
answered in the affirmative and stated that the City of Charleston has four
full-time employees.

Mr. McNeill questioned the need for the fee and asked about the need for
the affidavit.

Mr. Ulma stated that in terms of enforcement and staffing, the Planning
Department absorbs different programs and enforcement of those programs
without always adding additional staff. He stated that currently the
department has only one enforcement officer. This would be a way to pay
for the cost of additional staff to handle enforcement of the program.

Mr. Prause stated that the software currently used is only the basic software
and it is anticipated that additional modules would need to be purchased to
effectively enforce and regulate this program.

  Mr. Neal moved for denial of the request. Mr. McNeill seconded the
motion.

Mr. Neal suggested that the Committee has already extensively reviewed
this issue and suggested that the Commissions role should not be to
completely re-craft this ordinance. He suggested that this ordinance is
“overreach and over taxation”.
Ms. Smith suggested that the Commission should review the proposed
ordinance and makes recommendations for changes if desired. She
suggested that the cost can be justified and makes sense. She suggested
that more discussion is needed.

Mr. McNeill stated that the Commission should make a recommendation
and not craft the ordinance. He suggested that because of the extensive
changes that would be proposed, it would be better to deny the ordinance
in its entirety.

Mr. Wren agreed that there is still work that needs to be done with this
ordinance. He suggested that there should be some regulation of short-
term rentals, but the question is to what extent that regulation should be.

Mr. Neal suggested that there are numerous items included in the ordinance
that are in opposition of how short-term rentals should be regulated.

Ms. Gregg suggested that there should be discussion. She noted that not all
neighborhoods have an HOA and expressed concern that this would mean
that more short-term rentals would be “pushed” to these neighborhoods.

Mr. Gates asked if a joint meeting could be held to better understand the
mindset of the Committee. Mr. Pagliarini answered that a recommendation
from the Commission would need to be made within 30 days and suggested
that it might not be feasible to schedule a joint meeting within that
timeframe.

Mr. Campbell suggested that there is not justification for the governance of
short-term rentals.

Mr. Wren also suggested that the progress of the proposed ordinance went
to the Committee first, which is not how proposed ordinances typically
progress.

Ms. Smith suggested that the Commissions work might be for naught as the
Committee worked on the proposed ordinance before it came to the
Commission.

  Mr. Wren called for a vote on the motion. All in favor.

Mr. Wren closed the public hearing.

There being no further business, the meeting adjourned at 6 pm.
